# Medivale Family Medicine

## 04D. Weight and Nutrition

Adult obesity treatment uses the AGA medication-eligibility framework with product-specific U.S. labeling; surgery selection uses ASMBS/IFSO. ADA 2026 informs diabetes-specific weight care. GLIM 2025 guides malnutrition assessment, NICE NG69 informs eating-disorder care, and AGA/NICE pathways inform iron/B12 evaluation. Population dietary guidance is not a substitute for a disease-specific nutrition prescription.

### Start with the trajectory, not a judgment about body size

Weight includes fat, muscle, bone, organs, and water; scale changes do not necessarily represent fat changes. Establish measured trajectory, intent, appetite, intake, and function. Ask permission to discuss weight without stigma, which can deter care or disclosure of restriction, vomiting, and medicine use.

Calculate BMI as weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. Conventional adult categories are underweight below 18.5, the usual reference category 18.5 to below 25, overweight 25 to below 30, and obesity at 30 or above. Obesity classes are 30 to below 35, 35 to below 40, and 40 or above. **BMI does not directly measure fat or distinguish it from muscle.** It is a screening and treatment-eligibility tool, not a complete assessment of health. Pregnancy, edema, muscularity, and loss of muscle can make interpretation particularly misleading. [CDC BMI interpretation](https://www.cdc.gov/bmi/faq/index.html)

Assess waist distribution, pressure, metabolic findings, mobility, strength, and priorities. Central adiposity can confer risk below usual BMI thresholds; high BMI does not explain every symptom. Use population- and method-specific waist criteria. Children need age- and sex-specific growth assessment.

Rapid gain with edema, orthopnea, or abdominal distension requires cardiac, renal, or liver assessment. Gradual gain may reflect activity, sleep, life changes, or medicines, including glucocorticoids, antipsychotics, selected antidepressants/antiseizure drugs, and insulin. Coordinate alternatives without abrupt withdrawal. Genetics, food access, work, pain, and stress interact with physiology. [NIDDK factors affecting weight](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/adult-overweight-obesity/factors-affecting-weight-health)

Look for a secondary disorder when the pattern supports it. Thyroid testing belongs in the obesity assessment; interpret an abnormal TSH with free T4 and the clinical context. Test for cortisol excess when there are discriminatory features such as easy bruising, proximal weakness, broad violaceous striae, or an unusual combination of progressive findings, not simply because obesity is present. Treating confirmed hypothyroidism is appropriate; prescribing thyroid hormone to a euthyroid patient for weight loss is not. Severe early-onset hyperphagia, developmental findings, or a suggestive family pattern can warrant genetic/endocrine assessment. [ESE endocrine work-up in obesity](https://academic.oup.com/ejendo/article/182/1/G1/6653910)

### Investigate unintended loss while protecting nutrition

Unintended loss may reflect inadequate food access, impaired swallowing/absorption, increased losses, or systemic illness. Ask about dental pain, dysphagia, GI symptoms, bereavement, depression, cognition, substances, and food insecurity. Hyperglycemia, hyperthyroidism, infection, inflammation, or malignancy can cause loss despite intake. Review medication effects. Reassess even intended loss if rapid or functionally harmful.

Examine hydration, oral health, swallowing when indicated, lymph nodes, cardiopulmonary and abdominal findings, muscle bulk, strength, gait, and signs of deficiency. Guide testing by history and examination, commonly beginning with a blood count, chemistry/renal and liver assessment, and selected glucose, thyroid, inflammatory, or infection testing. Follow organ-specific symptoms and complete indicated cancer evaluation; neither indiscriminate tumor-marker panels nor reassurance from an up-to-date screening record replaces diagnostic assessment. Persistent unexplained loss needs documented follow-up even if initial tests are unrevealing.

**Clinical Case: Muscle loss at a high BMI.** An older adult with BMI 31 loses 7 kg after bereavement and now needs both arms to rise. Evaluate nutrition, depression, and function; restore meals and practical support. Increasing edema may conceal further tissue loss. ESPEN recommends routine malnutrition screening and individualized support, not indiscriminate restriction. [ESPEN geriatric nutrition guidance](https://www.espen.org/files/ESPEN-Guidelines/ESPEN_practical_guideline_Clinical_nutrition_and_hydration_in_geriatrics.pdf)

### Assess the diet the patient can actually obtain and eat

Review workday and nonworkday intake, drinks, alcohol, snacks, portions, timing, exclusions, supplements, shopping, and cooking. Ask about money, storage, transport, culture, sensory restrictions, allergies, and chewing/swallowing. One day's recall cannot establish usual intake. Dietitians integrate disease needs, growth, function, and resources.

Build a sustainable pattern of vegetables, fruit, fiber-rich grains, varied protein, and adequate micronutrients; address sugary drinks and energy-dense, nutrient-poor foods. The **2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans** require adaptation for pregnancy, childhood, frailty, CKD, and diabetes; population protein targets are not renal or malnutrition prescriptions. [Current federal dietary guidance](https://cdn.realfood.gov/DGA.pdf)

Diabetes nutrition therapy should fit glycemic and vascular goals, medicines, preferences, and cost; no single macronutrient ratio suits everyone. Carbohydrate quality, portions, and consistency matter particularly with mealtime insulin. Restrictive diets may compromise nutrients or medication safety. Link counseling to glucose monitoring and sick-day care. [ADA behavior and nutrition standards](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690188)

Agree on specific, feasible changes: shift workers may carry meals or replace sweetened drinks; poor dentition may require texture changes and dental care. Avoid energy restriction in active restrictive eating or nutritional instability. Supplements cannot replace adequate food, safe swallowing, or malabsorption treatment.

### Identify obesity-related disease and set meaningful outcomes

Adipose tissue is metabolically active. Visceral and ectopic fat, inflammatory signaling, and insulin resistance contribute to cardiometabolic disease, while mechanical loading affects joints and breathing. Evaluate for hypertension, dyslipidemia, prediabetes/diabetes, sleep apnea, steatotic liver disease, osteoarthritis, reflux, and reproductive problems when indicated. Obesity also increases risk for several cancers. Assess depression, function, and the effect of stigma without assuming that a psychological disorder explains the weight. [NIDDK obesity-related health risks](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/adult-overweight-obesity/health-risks)

Assess pressure, glucose, lipids, and liver/renal function as indicated. Witnessed apnea or sleepiness warrants sleep assessment; abnormal liver enzymes need evaluation of causes and fibrosis risk. Treat hypertension, dyslipidemia, apnea, and diabetes alongside weight, without waiting for weight loss.

When weight concerns coexist with menstrual irregularity or androgen-excess symptoms, use the reproductive-endocrine assessment in 14B, including PCOS/PMOS, rather than attributing every finding to body size. Acanthosis nigricans also prompts assessment for insulin resistance and dysglycemia; it does not establish diabetes without appropriate testing.

Metabolic syndrome describes a cluster, not a separate explanation for every symptom. In the common five-component framework, the components are enlarged waist, elevated triglycerides, reduced HDL, elevated blood pressure, and elevated fasting glucose; three establish the cluster, with specified treatment equivalents and population-sensitive waist criteria. **LDL is important for cardiovascular care but is not one of those five defining components.** Identifying the cluster should prompt treatment of its individual risks, not replace formal cardiovascular assessment. [NHLBI metabolic syndrome diagnosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/metabolic-syndrome/diagnosis)

Agree on outcomes such as easier walking, better sleep, lower blood pressure, improved glycemia, or reduced liver disease risk, as well as weight trajectory. For people with type 2 diabetes and overweight/obesity, ADA describes metabolic benefit from roughly 5-7% loss, with larger sustained reductions often providing greater benefit. Response differs between patients; diabetes remission, when achieved, still requires surveillance. Chronic obesity therapy may need continuation after a goal is reached, because stopping an effective treatment often permits regain. [ADA 2026 weight-management standards](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690172/)

### Combine behavioral support with activity and long-term follow-up

Offer or refer adults with BMI at least 30 to an intensive, multicomponent behavioral program. Effective care involves repeated contacts, individualized nutrition and activity plans, self-monitoring, problem-solving, and maintenance support, rather than a single instruction to eat less. Choose monitoring that helps the patient; frequent weighing or calorie tracking may be unsuitable when it reinforces disordered eating. Address transportation, cost, language, and digital access when selecting a program. [USPSTF behavioral intervention recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/obesity-in-adults-interventions)

Energy deficits reduce stored tissue, but changing appetite, expenditure, illness, and composition prevent fixed calorie reductions from predicting indefinite weekly loss. At a plateau, review intake, adequacy, goals, and treatment fit without blame. Maintenance requires active care.

For general adult health, work toward 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity weekly, or an equivalent vigorous combination, plus muscle strengthening on at least two days. Start below this when necessary and build gradually. Activity can improve health even when the scale changes little. Adapt for pain, disability, falls risk, cardiopulmonary symptoms, and patient preference; resistance work is especially useful when weight loss threatens muscle and function. New exertional chest pain or syncope needs evaluation, not a more demanding exercise prescription. [HHS physical activity guidance](https://odphp.health.gov/our-work/nutrition-physical-activity/physical-activity-guidelines/about-physical-activity-guidelines/questions-answers)

### Choose medication by eligibility, complications, and safety

AGA supports pharmacotherapy alongside lifestyle care at adult BMI at least 30, or at least 27 with weight-related complications, after inadequate lifestyle response. Discuss benefit, adverse effects, chronic use, pregnancy, and access. Product indications differ from guideline recommendations; the 2022 guideline's list is not a complete current formulary. [AGA adult obesity pharmacotherapy guidance](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/pharmacological-interventions-for-adults-with-obesity/)

The following table highlights selection issues, not every contraindication or a titration schedule. Screen for interactions, renal/hepatic restrictions, and product-specific monitoring before prescribing. Avoid combining products that duplicate an active ingredient or incretin mechanism. Weight-loss medications are not used for intentional weight reduction during pregnancy. Pediatric use is agent- and age-specific; adult eligibility cannot simply be applied to children. [NIDDK prescription weight-management overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/prescription-medications-treat-overweight-obesity)

| Option | Main action | Important selection issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semaglutide | GLP-1 receptor agonism | Product/formulation and indication matter; MTC/MEN2 contraindication and GI precautions |
| Tirzepatide | GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonism | MTC/MEN2 contraindication; oral contraceptive precautions during initiation/escalation |
| Liraglutide | GLP-1 receptor agonism | Daily injection; MTC/MEN2 and GI precautions; labeled response assessment |
| Orforglipron | Oral small-molecule GLP-1 receptor agonism | MTC/MEN2 and GI precautions; CYP3A4, simvastatin and contraceptive review |
| Phentermine/topiramate ER | Appetite effects through combined mechanisms | Pregnancy prevention/testing; cognitive, mood, acid-base, renal and eye risks |
| Naltrexone/bupropion ER | Appetite/reward-pathway effects | Avoid with uncontrolled hypertension, seizure risk, anorexia/bulimia or chronic opioids |
| Orlistat | Intestinal lipase inhibition | GI effects, fat-soluble vitamin replacement and absorption-related interactions |

Semaglutide and tirzepatide affect appetite and food intake; gastric emptying effects also influence tolerability and medication/procedure planning. Nausea is not the therapeutic goal. Persistent vomiting, dehydration, severe abdominal pain, inability to eat adequately, or hypoglycemia requires reassessment rather than automatic escalation. Review insulin or sulfonylurea doses when glucose falls. Distinguish uncomplicated GI intolerance from pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, severe gastroparesis, and volume-related kidney injury. Their MTC/MEN2 contraindication is not a blanket prohibition for ordinary primary hypothyroidism.

Wegovy has both injection and oral formulations; they are not milligram-for-milligram substitutes. Indications differ by formulation: the injection's noncirrhotic MASH indication concerns specified F2-F3 fibrosis and accelerated approval, not every patient with steatosis. Cardiovascular risk reduction requires the labeled population, not obesity alone. Semaglutide used for weight reduction should be stopped when pregnancy is recognized and at least two months before a planned pregnancy. Discuss GI, retinal, and anesthesia-related precautions using the current product instructions. [Wegovy prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ee06186f-2aa3-4990-a760-757579d8f77b)

Oral Wegovy is taken on an empty stomach in the morning with no more than 4 ounces of water, then at least 30 minutes before food, other drinks, or oral medicines. These instructions do **not** apply to orforglipron. Coordinate the schedule when the patient also takes levothyroxine rather than telling the patient to take both fasting medicines together.

Zepbound has an adult moderate-to-severe OSA indication in obesity in addition to chronic weight management. This does not authorize stopping PAP without reassessment. Its label advises patients using oral hormonal contraception to switch to a nonoral method or add a barrier method for four weeks after starting and for four weeks after each dose escalation. Stop when pregnancy is recognized. Do not combine it with another tirzepatide product or a GLP-1 receptor agonist. [Zepbound August 2026 labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=487cd7e7-434c-4925-99fa-aa80b1cc776b)

Liraglutide is a daily injectable GLP-1 option. Its adult Saxenda label calls for assessing weight change sixteen weeks after initiation and stopping if loss is less than 4% of baseline. This illustrates why response criteria must be product-specific rather than borrowed from another drug. Review its MTC/MEN2, GI, pregnancy, and other precautions. [Saxenda labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3946d389-0926-4f77-a708-0acb8153b143)

Orforglipron, labeled as Foundayo, is an oral small-molecule GLP-1 agonist for adult chronic weight management, taken with or without food. Do not exceed simvastatin 20 mg daily during coadministration. With a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, Foundayo's maximum is 9 mg daily; avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors that also inhibit OATP1B, and avoid strong CYP3A4 inducers. Review GI/volume effects and MTC/MEN2 contraindications. Use a nonoral contraceptive or added barrier method for thirty days after starting and each escalation, not tirzepatide's four-week interval. Stop when pregnancy is recognized. [Foundayo July 2026 labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=8ac446c5-feba-474f-a103-23facb9b5c62)

Phentermine/topiramate is contraindicated in pregnancy, glaucoma, hyperthyroidism, and within 14 days of an MAOI. Its label recommends pregnancy testing before treatment and monthly, with effective contraception. Check bicarbonate, creatinine, potassium, mood/cognition, and stone risk. For adults, Qsymia starts at 3.75/23 mg orally each morning for 14 days, then 7.5/46 mg daily. If weight loss is below 3% after twelve weeks at that dose, increase through 11.25/69 mg for 14 days to 15/92 mg daily when appropriate. If loss remains below 5% after twelve weeks at 15/92 mg, discontinue; taper that dose to every other day for at least one week to reduce seizure risk. Maximum dose is 7.5/46 mg daily with CrCl below 50 mL/min or moderate hepatic impairment; avoid Qsymia in patients receiving dialysis or with severe hepatic impairment. Pediatric response uses BMI change, not adult weight-loss criteria. [Qsymia label, sections 2-5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=40dd5602-53da-45ac-bb4b-15789aba40f9)

Naltrexone/bupropion is unsuitable with uncontrolled hypertension, seizure disorders, anorexia or bulimia, chronic opioid use, certain withdrawal states, or relevant MAOI exposure. It can block opioid analgesia or precipitate withdrawal; do not assume that an opioid listed as as-needed is irrelevant. Review all bupropion-containing products, blood pressure, mood, and drug interactions. [Contrave current labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=485ff360-32c8-11df-928b-0002a5d5c51b)

For Contrave, assess response after twelve weeks **at the maintenance dosage**, not twelve weeks after the first tablet. Stop if weight loss is below 5% of baseline; an unchanged ineffective prescription is not maintenance care.

Orlistat reduces fat absorption and can cause oily stool, urgency, and reduced fat-soluble vitamin absorption. Use the labeled multivitamin timing and review levothyroxine, warfarin, cyclosporine, and other interactions. Chronic malabsorption and cholestasis are contraindications; renal oxalate injury and rare severe liver injury are additional safety concerns. Its availability does not make it the universal first choice. [Xenical current labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6240792b-9224-2d10-e053-2a91aa0a2c3e)

Single-agent phentermine has short-term labeling, unlike the chronic phentermine/topiramate product. Review cardiovascular disease, uncontrolled hypertension, hyperthyroidism, glaucoma, pregnancy, MAOI exposure, and misuse risk before considering it. Do not generalize its duration limit to every obesity medicine. [Adipex-P labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f5b2f9d8-2226-476e-9caf-9d41e6891c46)

Review tolerability, intake, hydration, weight/function, complications, adherence, and affordability using product-specific assessment and stopping rules, not a universal twelve-week rule. Revisit ineffective or inaccessible treatment. A 52-year-old with OSA and knee pain may prioritize walking and sleep; review contraindications and continue sleep treatment.

### Refer for metabolic surgery without relying on obsolete eligibility rules

Under ASMBS/IFSO, metabolic/bariatric surgery is recommended for BMI at least 35 regardless of whether a comorbidity has already been identified. Consider it at BMI 30-34.9 with metabolic disease or inadequate durable benefit from nonsurgical treatment. The guideline uses lower thresholds for Asian populations, including offering surgery at BMI at least 27.5. Multidisciplinary assessment addresses individual benefit, perioperative risk, nutritional and psychological readiness, and capacity for follow-up; eligibility does not mean automatic operation. Insurance rules may be more restrictive than the clinical guideline. [ASMBS/IFSO surgical indications](https://asmbs.org/app/uploads/2022/10/ASMBS-IFSO-Guidelines-2022-PIIS1550728922006414.pdf)

Sleeve gastrectomy reduces volume and alters appetite signaling; Roux-en-Y bypass also changes nutrient passage, absorption, and metabolism. Choose by reflux, metabolic disease, anatomy, nutritional risk, and preference. Discuss reoperation, bleeding, leak, thrombosis, reflux, obstruction, and nutritional complications. [NIDDK surgery mechanisms and scope](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/bariatric-surgery/definition-facts)

After surgery, continue long-term nutritional surveillance, prescribed vitamins/minerals, adequate protein, and adjustment of diabetes or blood-pressure medicines. Persistent vomiting, severe pain, tachycardia, dehydration, or inability to take supplements warrants prompt evaluation. Bypass can cause dumping symptoms after meals, later hypoglycemia, internal hernia, or deficiencies; postoperative abdominal pain is not automatically dietary indiscretion. Pregnancy planning needs coordination during rapid weight loss and assessment of nutritional adequacy. Weight regain warrants evaluation and renewed treatment, not loss of access to care. [NIDDK postoperative risks](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/weight-management/bariatric-surgery/side-effects)

### Recognize eating disorders without using appearance as a screening test

Eating disorders impair eating behavior and psychological or physical function at any age, gender, or body size. Ask privately about restriction, weight fears, body image, bingeing, purging, laxatives/diuretics, compulsive exercise, sensory avoidance, choking fears, and low food interest. Assess depression, substances, and safety. [APA eating-disorder overview](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/eating-disorders/what-are-eating-disorders)

| Presentation | Distinguishing clinical feature |
| --- | --- |
| Anorexia nervosa | Restriction with significantly low weight and fear of gain or persistent behavior interfering with gain, with disturbed weight/body-image appraisal |
| Atypical anorexia | The anorexia syndrome without significantly low weight despite substantial loss; medical instability can still occur |
| Bulimia nervosa | Recurrent binge eating with inappropriate compensatory behavior and excessive weight/shape influence |
| Binge-eating disorder | Recurrent distressing binge episodes without regular compensatory behavior |
| Avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder | Restriction causing nutritional or functional harm without the characteristic weight/shape motivation |

Section 12B develops eating-disorder diagnostic frequency/duration criteria and psychological treatment; the priority here is nutritional and medical stability. Gastrointestinal, endocrine, neurologic, and medication-related conditions can mimic or coexist with an eating disorder. A normal BMI, a normal initial potassium, or a reassuring appearance cannot establish medical stability. A short screen supports assessment but cannot rule a disorder in or out alone.

Assess weight trajectory, intake, hydration, temperature, pulse, blood pressure including orthostatic change when appropriate, and cardiac/neurologic symptoms. Obtain an ECG and relevant blood count, glucose, electrolytes, renal/liver tests, magnesium, and phosphate according to risk. Purging can cause hypokalemia and acid-base disturbance, increasing arrhythmia risk. Severe restriction can cause bradycardia, hypothermia, hypoglycemia, loss of muscle, and endocrine/bone effects. [APA 2023 assessment statements](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.23180001)

Instability, syncope, significant ECG/electrolyte abnormalities, inability to maintain intake, or immediate psychiatric danger requires urgent higher-level care. Do not wait for a particular BMI to refer or admit. [NICE NG69 assessment and safety](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng69/chapter/recommendations)

**Clinical Case: Instability despite reference-range BMI.** At BMI 24, rapid loss, rigid food rules, prolonged daily exercise, and presyncope require stability assessment, eating-disorder care, and investigation of alternatives. Avoid further appetite suppression or praise for rapid loss.

Combine nutritional rehabilitation, medical monitoring, and focused psychotherapy, with developmentally appropriate family involvement. Binge-eating treatment restores regular eating rather than simply pursuing weight loss. Medicines do not replace rehabilitation or therapy; account for purging-related electrolytes and seizure risk. Primary care retains responsibility for monitoring and referral follow-through. [NICE NG69 treatment recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng69/chapter/recommendations)

### Diagnose malnutrition from intake, tissue loss, and cause

Malnutrition can develop from inadequate intake, impaired absorption, inflammation, or combinations of these. It can coexist with obesity. After identifying nutritional risk, the GLIM framework requires at least one phenotypic finding, such as involuntary weight loss, low BMI, or reduced muscle mass, and at least one etiologic finding, such as reduced intake/assimilation or inflammation. Use its age/population-sensitive definitions and validated measurement methods when formally classifying severity; a generic BMI cutoff alone is insufficient. [GLIM 2025 consensus update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12053077/)

Assess tissue stores, function, trajectory, and inadequate intake; edema can conceal loss. Inflammation, illness, and fluid shifts affect albumin and prealbumin, making them unsuitable as stand-alone malnutrition tests or measures of body protein. Low albumin does not determine calorie requirements. [ASPEN guidance on visceral proteins](https://dev.nutritioncare.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Appropriate-Use-Visceral-Proteins-Nutrition-Screening-Assessment.pdf)

Treat causes and support meals, texture adaptation, symptom control, and oral fortification/supplements. If intake remains unsafe or inadequate, assess enteral support when the gut is usable; parenteral nutrition has specific indications and risks. Match monitored support to swallowing safety, goals, prognosis, and preferences.

### Anticipate refeeding complications before restarting substantial intake

Prolonged inadequate intake depletes phosphate, potassium, magnesium, and thiamine despite initially unremarkable serum values. Carbohydrate reintroduction drives intracellular uptake and fluid retention, risking arrhythmia, weakness, respiratory failure, and neurologic injury by any feeding route. Assess risk with marked loss, minimal intake, low electrolytes, alcohol-related nutritional risk, or severe malnutrition.

High-risk patients need a trained nutrition/medical team, a cautious individualized feeding plan, thiamine and other indicated supplementation, and close monitoring of phosphate, potassium, magnesium, glucose, volume, and clinical status. Use a defined local protocol matched to the population and severity rather than a single outpatient calorie rule. The NICE adult nutrition-support pathway provides risk and monitoring criteria; specialist eating-disorder refeeding protocols require their own population-specific application. [NICE adult nutrition support](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg32/chapter/Recommendations)

Confusion, gait disturbance, or ocular abnormalities after sustained vomiting or severe dietary depletion raises concern for thiamine-related neurologic injury; the complete classic triad need not be present. Urgent parenteral treatment should not await a vitamin result when the clinical concern is high. Arrange thiamine promptly before or alongside carbohydrate administration, but never delay lifesaving glucose for hypoglycemia while waiting for thiamine. Alcohol use is not required: prolonged vomiting after bariatric surgery is another important setting. [NIH thiamin clinical review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Thiamin-HealthProfessional/)

### Treat iron deficiency and look for its source

Iron supports hemoglobin production, so depletion may cause fatigue, reduced exercise tolerance, pallor, pica, or restless legs. Early deficiency need not be microcytic or frankly anemic. A blood count, ferritin, and selected iron indices help distinguish depleted stores from other causes of anemia. Ferritin rises with inflammation; a normal or high value can therefore coexist with restricted iron availability. Low serum iron alone is nonspecific. Interpret transferrin saturation, inflammation, renal disease, and the blood-count pattern together. [NIH iron assessment](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Iron-HealthProfessional/)

AGA uses ferritin below 45 ng/mL rather than below 15 when diagnosing iron deficiency in a patient with anemia. This is not a universal screening cutoff for every healthy person or every chronic disease. Identify menstrual or gastrointestinal loss, blood donation, low intake, celiac disease, and altered absorption. Men and postmenopausal women with unexplained iron-deficiency anemia generally need upper and lower GI evaluation; menstruation does not automatically explain all anemia in a younger adult. GI symptoms, severity, family history, and shared decisions guide the appropriate evaluation. [AGA iron-deficiency GI evaluation](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/gastrointestinal-evaluation-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/)

Replace iron while investigating its source. AGA advises oral dosing no more than daily; alternate-day dosing may improve tolerability. Specify elemental iron, interactions, GI effects, and response monitoring. Use IV iron for intolerance, failure, or poor absorption, including selected post-bariatric patients. Nonresponse warrants reassessment of adherence, loss, absorption, inflammation, and diagnosis. [AGA 2024 iron treatment update](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/management-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/)

### Recognize B12 and folate deficiency before irreversible injury

Vitamin B12 participates in DNA synthesis and neurologic function. Deficiency may produce macrocytic anemia, glossitis, paresthesias, impaired vibration/proprioception, gait change, or cognitive symptoms. Neurologic disease can occur without anemia or macrocytosis, and concurrent iron deficiency can obscure the expected cell size. Risks include low intake without reliable fortified foods/supplementation, autoimmune gastric disease, gastric or ileal surgery, malabsorption, and medicines such as metformin. Nitrous oxide can impair B12 function even when the initial total concentration is not clearly low. [NIH vitamin B12 clinical review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/)

Measure total or active B12 using the local assay; methylmalonic acid can help resolve an indeterminate result in a symptomatic patient, but kidney impairment can raise MMA independently. Investigate autoimmune gastritis with appropriate testing when suspected; a negative intrinsic-factor antibody does not exclude it. Draw diagnostic samples before replacement when feasible, but do not delay treatment for significant neurologic disease while awaiting confirmatory results. NICE NG239 recommends lifelong intramuscular replacement for B12 deficiency due to autoimmune gastritis, total gastrectomy, or complete terminal ileal resection; other causes may permit oral replacement with adequate dosing and follow-up. [NICE NG239 diagnostic and replacement pathway](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations)

Folate deficiency can also impair DNA synthesis and cause megaloblastic anemia. Consider poor intake, alcohol-related disease, malabsorption, increased requirements, or interfering medicines. Folate can improve the anemia of B12 deficiency without correcting its neurologic injury. Check and treat B12 appropriately before, or together with, folate replacement rather than treating unexplained macrocytosis with folate alone. Preconception folic-acid prevention is a separate indication discussed in preventive and reproductive care. [NIH folate review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Folate-HealthProfessional/)

### Recognize additional deficiency patterns and avoid supplement toxicity

Select nutrient tests by exposure and phenotype. Malabsorption, bariatric anatomy, restrictive diets, prolonged vomiting, and excessive supplements suggest potentially multiple deficiencies. Replace deficits, investigate causes, and follow function as well as laboratory values.

| Setting or finding | Nutritional consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Anemia with gait/sensory problems after gastric surgery or excess zinc | Copper deficiency as well as B12 deficiency |
| Poor wound healing, taste change, characteristic dermatitis or prolonged diarrhea | Zinc deficiency in the appropriate context |
| Bleeding gums, perifollicular bleeding, severe dietary restriction | Vitamin C deficiency |
| Fat malabsorption with visual, bone, neurologic or bleeding manifestations | Consider vitamins A, D, E and K according to the affected function |
| Restricted intake with photosensitive dermatitis, diarrhea and neuropsychiatric change | Consider niacin deficiency and broader malnutrition |

Copper deficiency can cause anemia and neurologic dysfunction. Excess zinc can impair copper absorption, so ask about high-dose supplements and denture products rather than adding zinc reflexively to every poor diet. [NIH copper review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Copper-HealthProfessional/)

Zinc supports many cellular processes, but its symptoms are nonspecific and serum interpretation is affected by illness and context. Excess intake is not harmless. A person with chronic diarrhea and poor healing needs evaluation for the underlying gastrointestinal disease and nutritional losses, not only an isolated zinc prescription. [NIH zinc review](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)

Vitamin C deficiency impairs connective-tissue integrity and can cause gum and skin bleeding; evaluate the whole restricted diet. Niacin deficiency affects skin, gastrointestinal, and neurologic function, but high-dose niacin is not benign replacement for an unspecified symptom. Avoid assuming that a water-soluble vitamin cannot be toxic: chronic excessive vitamin B6 exposure can itself cause neuropathy. [NIH vitamin C](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/), [niacin](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Niacin-HealthProfessional/), [vitamin B6](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB6-HealthProfessional/)

Vitamin A deficiency can impair dark adaptation and damage the ocular surface; excess preformed vitamin A can injure the liver and harm a developing fetus. Vitamin D deficiency can impair mineralization, but testing and replacement should follow the appropriate bone/clinical pathway rather than indiscriminate high-dose supplementation. Severe vitamin E deficiency can cause neurologic dysfunction in malabsorption. Vitamin K deficiency impairs coagulation; evaluate bleeding urgently and consider liver disease, anticoagulants, and other causes rather than assuming every prolonged clotting time is dietary.
