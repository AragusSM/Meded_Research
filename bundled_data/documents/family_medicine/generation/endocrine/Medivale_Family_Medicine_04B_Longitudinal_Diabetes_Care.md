# Medivale Family Medicine

## 04B. Longitudinal Diabetes Care

The ADA 2026 Standards are the main U.S. care pathway, supplemented by the October 2026 ADA/EASD type 2 consensus and product-specific labeling. Adult outpatient care is the focus; pregnancy and pediatric protocols require their own pathways. Diagnosis and emergencies are covered in 04A.

### Build a follow-up plan that measures more than A1C

Diabetes care preserves health, function, and daily life while preventing symptoms and complications. Judge A1C improvement alongside safety and burden. Ask what medicines are actually taken, the work and meal schedule, and the hardest treatment task. Revisit classification when deterioration suggests insulin deficiency or a secondary cause.

Reconcile medicines and supplies, glucose patterns, hypoglycemia, weight, blood pressure, symptoms, and treatment burden. Confirm completion of eye, kidney, and foot assessments. Coordinate nutrition, activity, tobacco cessation, sleep, oral health, immunization, and vascular prevention; an at-goal A1C does not complete the visit. [NIDDK ongoing diabetes care](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/managing-diabetes)

Self-management education teaches glucose interpretation, medication use, missed-meal management, and how to obtain help. Use demonstration and teach-back, agree on a feasible change, and arrange review. Reassess skills after treatment, complication, or living changes. Ask about diabetes distress without mistaking it for unwillingness. [ADA 2026 health behaviors and well-being](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690188)

### Choose targets that are safe for this individual

The following treatment goals are for many nonpregnant adults, not diagnostic cutoffs. A1C below 6.5% may suit low-risk treatment; less stringent goals may be safer with frailty, functional limitations, or substantial hypoglycemia risk. Document the agreed goal and rationale.

| Measure | Common adult treatment goal |
| --- | --- |
| A1C | Below 7%, individualized |
| Before-meal capillary glucose | 80-130 mg/dL |
| Peak after-meal capillary glucose | Below 180 mg/dL; assess 1-2 hours after the meal begins |
| CGM time in 70-180 mg/dL | More than 70% |
| CGM time below 70 / below 54 mg/dL | Less than 4% / less than 1%; older adults generally need less than 1% below 70 |

Assess glycemia at least twice yearly when stable and meeting goals, and usually about every three months after treatment changes or when not at goal. Do not wait for the next A1C to address symptomatic highs or recurrent lows. A1C cannot show when excursions occur; its limitations with altered red-cell turnover are explained in 04A. [ADA 2026 glycemic goals and assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690178/)

**Clinical Case: An unsafe average.** A 62-year-old on insulin has A1C 6.8% but wakes sweaty twice weekly and eats extra before driving to prevent lows. Identify and reduce the unsafe exposure. Conversely, persistent above-goal A1C without hypoglycemia requires investigation and action.

### Use glucose monitoring to answer a clinical question

Connect monitoring to decisions: fasting readings assess basal insulin; paired meal readings assess food or prandial treatment. Intensive insulin plans also cover exercise, symptoms, driving, and recovery from lows. CGM is recommended with insulin, hypoglycemia-producing noninsulin therapy, or other management benefit. Choose the device together, train the user, and retain a meter backup. [ADA 2026 diabetes technology](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690173/)

CGM measures interstitial glucose, so readings may lag during rapid change. When symptoms conflict, use a finger-stick and device instructions; act promptly on severe symptoms. Check placement, skin irritation, missing data, interference, and audible, actionable alerts. A receiver can replace an incompatible smartphone. [NIDDK CGM principles](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/managing-diabetes/continuous-glucose-monitoring)

Review representative days with meals, insulin timing, activity, and symptoms. Address lows first, then repeated overnight, fasting, or meal patterns. Illness or holiday data may be atypical. The glucose management indicator is not laboratory A1C. Professional or intermittent CGM can answer focused questions when continuous use is impractical.

### Select treatment for the person's organs, weight, and circumstances

Type 2 treatment is not a universal sequence in which every patient must fail metformin before receiving organ-protective therapy. Evaluate established or high-risk atherosclerotic disease, heart failure, CKD, weight goals, hypoglycemia, tolerability, and access. The October 2026 ADA/EASD consensus supports earlier consideration of SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1-based therapy, potentially from diagnosis. That does not make every product interchangeable or remove contraindications. Proven organ benefit can matter independently of the current A1C. [ADA/EASD 2026 type 2 consensus](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-026-06855-7)

Match evidence to the dominant problem: an SGLT2 inhibitor with proven heart-failure benefit, an agent with demonstrated cardiovascular benefit in ASCVD, or appropriate kidney-protective therapy in CKD. Check renal eligibility. Effective GLP-1 or dual GIP/GLP-1 therapy may also serve weight goals. Reassess insulin and secretagogue doses when glucose improves substantially. [ADA 2026 pharmacologic recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690185/)

**Clinical Case: Glycemia and organ protection.** A 59-year-old with A1C 6.7%, persistent albuminuria, and CKD still needs kidney-risk treatment despite appropriate blood-pressure care. The same A1C with recurrent lows and food insecurity may instead call for reducing a hypoglycemia-producing drug.

### Understand the role and limits of metformin

Metformin lowers hepatic glucose output inexpensively, with little hypoglycemia alone. Slow titration, meals, or extended release may reduce gastrointestinal effects. Check renal function and consider B12 deficiency with anemia or neuropathy. Continue tolerated metformin when useful without delaying separately indicated organ-protective therapy.

For an eligible adult using the cited extended-release formulation, start 500 mg orally with the evening meal. Increase by 500 mg weekly as tolerated and needed, up to 2,000 mg once daily; swallow whole. Other formulations have different instructions and maxima. Recheck tolerability and glucose during titration, not only at the next A1C.

Under the U.S. label, do not initiate metformin at eGFR 30-45 mL/min/1.73 m2; reassess continued use if eGFR falls below 45, and discontinue below 30. Acute kidney injury, hypoxia, or significant dehydration requires reassessment. For iodinated contrast, the label specifies holding at or before the procedure with eGFR 30-60, liver disease, alcoholism, heart failure, or intra-arterial contrast, then reassessing eGFR after 48 hours and restarting if stable. This is a label-specific pathway, not a claim that every imaging test requires withholding metformin. [Metformin prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0ff1ba75-3c86-4baf-a6b1-1bb883839866)

### Use SGLT2 inhibitors with a clear safety plan

SGLT2 inhibition increases urinary glucose loss and changes renal sodium handling. The glucose-lowering effect diminishes as kidney function declines, but this does not mean the heart or kidney indication disappears at the same threshold. For empagliflozin, the label does not recommend use solely to improve glycemia below eGFR 30; heart-failure and CKD trial populations included eGFR down to 20. Check the actual indication, label, volume status, and concomitant diuretics rather than using a class-wide creatinine cutoff.

Counsel about genital fungal infection, volume depletion, and ketoacidosis that may occur without striking hyperglycemia. Reduced intake, illness, fasting, and inadequate insulin increase risk. For empagliflozin, withhold for at least three days, if possible, before surgery or procedures involving prolonged fasting; restart when clinically stable and oral intake has resumed. Other agents can have different interruption intervals. Nausea, abdominal pain, or abnormal breathing requires urgent evaluation for ketoacidosis even when the meter looks reassuring. [Empagliflozin prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5777b8a8-ada6-4950-8548-43a1de11f075)

### Distinguish incretin-based therapy from insulin replacement

GLP-1 receptor agonists enhance glucose-dependent insulin secretion, restrain glucagon, and affect appetite and gastric emptying. Tirzepatide also activates the GIP receptor. These drugs can support glucose and weight goals, but are not insulin replacement in type 1 diabetes. Product, formulation, indication, and outcome evidence matter: a diabetes product and an obesity product containing a related ingredient do not automatically share all approvals. The weight-management discussion continues in 04D. [ADA 2026 weight-treatment recommendations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690172/)

Ozempic's semaglutide label includes cardiovascular and kidney outcomes in specified type 2 populations. Its precautions include gastrointestinal intolerance, dehydration-related kidney injury, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, and retinopathy monitoring, especially with preexisting disease. It is not recommended with severe gastroparesis. The medullary thyroid carcinoma/MEN2 contraindication is not a blanket ban for every thyroid condition. Plan gradual, product-specific escalation and reassess insulin or sulfonylureas for hypoglycemia. Stop Ozempic at least two months before planned pregnancy. [Semaglutide prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=42bdd912-2393-44c4-b7e0-47672ca28991)

Persistent vomiting, severe abdominal pain, or inability to drink warrants medication review, hydration assessment, and evaluation for other causes or urgent care. Alert anesthesia teams to delayed gastric emptying and symptoms; perioperative plans should be individualized rather than applying a universal hold rule. [Multisociety perioperative guidance](https://www.asahq.org/about-asa/newsroom/news-releases/2024/10/new-multi-society-glp-1-guidance)

### Know when other glucose-lowering drugs help or create risk

DPP-4 inhibitors preserve endogenous incretin activity. They offer modest glucose lowering, usually without weight gain or substantial hypoglycemia by themselves, but do not supply the established organ-protection rationale of selected SGLT2 or GLP-1 agents. Sitagliptin requires renal dose adjustment; its label includes pancreatitis, hypersensitivity, and heart-failure precautions. Risk is product-specific: do not transfer a trial signal from one member to every drug as an identical effect. Combining a DPP-4 inhibitor with GLP-1-based therapy adds no useful routine glucose-lowering advantage and is not recommended. [Sitagliptin labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=eb81a45f-05b8-49de-99ce-5089bd360d9b)

For adult sitagliptin, the labeled oral dose is 100 mg once daily with eGFR at least 45 mL/min/1.73 m2, 50 mg daily with eGFR 30 to below 45, and 25 mg daily below 30, including dialysis. Check renal function before treatment and periodically; these doses do not apply to combination tablets or every DPP-4 inhibitor.

Sulfonylureas are affordable insulin secretagogues but cause hypoglycemia and weight gain, especially with missed meals, renal impairment, or older age. Glyburide can cause prolonged hypoglycemia in older adults. Meal-linked meglitinides also require reliable intake. [ADA/EASD medication comparison](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-026-06855-7)

Pioglitazone improves insulin sensitivity but can cause edema, weight gain, and fractures. Avoid it in symptomatic heart failure; initiating it in NYHA class III or IV heart failure is contraindicated. Evaluate liver disease and the label's bladder-cancer precautions. Its relatively low hypoglycemia risk alone does not make it a good choice for a patient with fluid overload or high fracture risk. [Pioglitazone prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=31619517-a590-408a-ae9e-76c99f0a0a1d)

### Start insulin when needed, then adjust the responsible component

Type 1 diabetes requires continuous basal and meal-related insulin by injections or pump. Basal insulin restrains between-meal and overnight glucose production; prandial insulin covers carbohydrate, with individualized corrections. Regular and intermediate-acting insulin differ in timing and peaks from analogs. Premixed regimens simplify dosing but reduce meal flexibility. Teach the actual preparation. [NIDDK insulin and treatment principles](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/insulin-medicines-treatments)

In type 2 diabetes, consider insulin for symptomatic hyperglycemia, A1C above 10%, or glucose at least 300 mg/dL, especially with catabolism. Assess for crisis or misclassified insulin deficiency. Otherwise, ADA prefers GLP-1-based treatment before insulin for glucose lowering. Insulin is treatment, not evidence of personal failure. [ADA 2026 treatment selection](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690185/)

When basal insulin is appropriate for an insulin-naive adult with type 2 diabetes, use a product-specific starting regimen and individualized follow-up. For U-100 Lantus, the label specifies 0.2 units/kg or up to 10 units once daily, with adjustment for metabolic need and monitored response. This is not a total insulin dose for type 1 diabetes and not a recipe for treating DKA. Teach the exact insulin name, concentration, units, timing, and device. Never share pens, withdraw insulin from a pen with a syringe, or assume concentrated products can be substituted by volume. [Lantus prescribing information](https://products.sanofi.us/Lantus/Lantus.html)

| Repeated pattern | Clinical reasoning before changing treatment |
| --- | --- |
| High fasting values without overnight lows | Review basal coverage, missed doses, evening intake, illness, and technique |
| Overnight falls or fasting lows | Reassess excess basal exposure, evening bolus, alcohol, activity, and renal change |
| Fasting at goal but after-meal highs | Address meal-related treatment; more basal may create nighttime hypoglycemia |
| Unexplained highs alternating with lows | Review timing, duplicate doses, absorption sites, data quality, meals, and access |

Written titration plans specify the glucose pattern, adjustment interval and limit, response to lows, and contact route. Long-acting products reach steady state at different speeds. Premature repeat correction doses cause insulin stacking. Base changes on repeated patterns, not an unusual reading or unresolved supply failure.

**Clinical Case: Target the meal.** A 47-year-old on basal insulin has fasting glucose 96-118 mg/dL but post-dinner values 250-290. More basal may cause nocturnal lows without correcting dinner hyperglycemia. Review food, timing, and daytime data and choose a meal-directed strategy.

### Make injections and technology workable in daily life

Observe injection technique and inspect/palpate for lipohypertrophy, which causes unpredictable absorption. Rotate sites and avoid abnormal tissue. Moving to normal tissue can increase effective delivery: review dosing and monitor closely. Use a new needle and device-specific priming and hold instructions. Follow product-specific storage limits; heat-damaged insulin may fail. [Diabetes Technology Society injection-site guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10658672/)

Automated insulin delivery links a sensor, pump, and algorithm. ADA 2026 favors AID for suitable insulin-treated populations according to preferences, training, and support. Meal tasks and failure recognition remain necessary. Provide backup insulin and a written interruption plan; pump failure can rapidly remove basal coverage. Investigate symptomatic hyperglycemia or ketones without waiting for an alarm. [ADA technology and delivery systems](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690173/)

Insulin-treated patients and others at substantial hypoglycemia risk need a rescue plan and accessible glucagon, with people around them trained to use it. Review actual events, including lows the patient treated without calling. Confirm that the person has rapid glucose available and knows not to drive while low. Rescue and urgent escalation are covered in 04A; prevention requires changing the precipitating circumstance, not only replacing the consumed glucose. [NIDDK hypoglycemia prevention](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/low-blood-glucose-hypoglycemia)

### Manage vascular risk independently of glucose

Under ADA 2026, adults aged 40-75 with diabetes but no ASCVD receive at least moderate-intensity statin therapy. With an additional ASCVD risk factor, use high intensity, aiming for at least a 50% LDL reduction and LDL below 70 mg/dL. Diabetes with established ASCVD calls for high intensity and LDL below 55 mg/dL; add proven nonstatin therapy if the goal is not reached on the maximum tolerated statin. Below 40 or above 75, individualize initiation; do not treat all ages identically. Pregnancy plans, intolerance, renal limits, and interactions can change the prescription. Section 02C develops statin selection and monitoring. [ADA 2026 recommendations 10.18-10.28](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S216/163933/10-Cardiovascular-Disease-and-Risk-Management)

For many nonpregnant adults, aim for blood pressure below 130/80 mm Hg; the 2026 ADA/EASD consensus encourages systolic pressure below 120 with high cardiovascular or kidney risk, balancing tolerability and orthostasis. Section 02B develops measurement and treatment. Controlled glucose does not remove either prevention task. [ADA/EASD vascular targets](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-026-06855-7)

### Screen for complications and act on the findings

Screening schedules apply to asymptomatic adults; an abnormal result or new symptom can require earlier assessment. Pregnancy and children have distinct timing. A normal examination last year does not justify delaying evaluation of a new visual complaint, ulcer, or renal decline.

| Domain | Adult surveillance starting point |
| --- | --- |
| Retina | Type 2: at diagnosis. Type 1: initial comprehensive dilated examination at five years after onset. Usually annual thereafter; selected normal examinations with glycemia at goal may permit 1-2-year screening |
| Kidney | UACR and eGFR at least annually in all type 2 diabetes and type 1 diabetes of at least five years' duration; established CKD needs more frequent follow-up |
| Peripheral/autonomic neuropathy | Assess from type 2 diagnosis or five years after type 1 diagnosis, then at least annually |
| Feet | Comprehensive assessment at least annually; inspect every visit with sensory loss or prior ulcer/amputation |

The comprehensive foot examination covers skin, deformity, circulation, and protective sensation: a 10-g monofilament or accepted touch test plus another neurologic modality. A normal sensation test does not establish normal perfusion. Retinal photography with qualified interpretation or approved AI can improve access but needs referral for abnormal or unreadable images. [ADA 2026 eye, nerve, and foot surveillance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690177/) [ADA 2026 kidney surveillance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690176/)

### Interpret kidney findings before assigning the cause

Use both UACR and eGFR: albumin leakage and filtration are different measures. Moderately increased UACR is 30-299 mg/g; severely increased UACR is at least 300 mg/g. Because excretion varies, confirm elevation with two abnormal results among three specimens over three to six months when appropriate. Exercise, infection, menstruation, marked hyperglycemia, and other transient conditions can distort a sample. Rapid decline, active urine sediment, or an atypical course raises concern for another kidney disease. [ADA 2026 CKD assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690176/)

Treat blood pressure and cardiovascular risk, review nephrotoxins, and select kidney-protective therapy where indicated. ACE inhibitors or ARBs are important in hypertensive diabetes with albuminuria, not automatic prophylaxis for every person with normal pressure and normal urine findings. Monitor creatinine and potassium after relevant changes; do not combine ACE inhibitor and ARB therapy. SGLT2 therapy requires indication-specific eligibility and monitoring. The detailed CKD pathway belongs in 06C. [NIDDK diabetic kidney care](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/diabetic-kidney-disease)

Finerenone's CKD indications now include adults with type 1 as well as type 2 diabetes, but the evidence statements differ: the type 2 indication specifies kidney and cardiovascular outcomes, whereas the type 1 indication is UACR reduction expected to reduce kidney progression. Do not present these as identical outcome evidence. Check potassium, eGFR, and interacting medicines before use; adrenal insufficiency and strong CYP3A4 inhibitors are contraindications. Use the CKD dosing and monitoring pathway in 06C, not the separate heart-failure dosing schedule. [Finerenone prescribing information, revised September 2026](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=fc726765-5d5a-4d6e-b037-b847bda9fb7c)

### Protect vision, sensation, and the foot at risk

Retinal disease can be advanced before symptoms appear. Leakage can threaten central vision through macular edema; ischemia can promote fragile new vessels, bleeding, and traction. New floaters, a curtain, or abrupt visual loss requires urgent ophthalmic assessment rather than waiting for annual screening. Ophthalmic treatment may include intravitreal therapy, laser, or surgery according to the lesion. Glucose and blood-pressure management remain important but do not replace indicated eye treatment. [National Eye Institute retinopathy overview](https://www.nei.nih.gov/eye-health-information/eye-conditions-and-diseases/diabetic-retinopathy)

Distal neuropathy often produces symmetric burning, tingling, or numbness beginning in the feet. Loss of pain is dangerous because it removes a warning signal. Evaluate alternatives such as B12 deficiency, alcohol-related disease, compression, or another neurologic disorder when appropriate; asymmetric weakness or rapid progression is not a routine stocking-pattern presentation. Pain treatment can improve symptoms without restoring protective sensation. Balance a neuropathic-pain medicine's potential benefit against sedation, falls, kidney function, and interactions. Ask separately about postural, gastrointestinal, urinary, and sexual symptoms that may suggest autonomic involvement. [NIDDK peripheral neuropathy](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/nerve-damage-diabetic-neuropathies/peripheral-neuropathy)

Initial painful-neuropathy drug classes include gabapentinoids, SNRIs, selected TCAs, and sodium-channel blockers. Choose for comorbidity, renal function, and adverse-effect risk rather than assuming that pain control restores sensation. Opioids, including tramadol and tapentadol, should not be routine therapy. [ADA 2026 neuropathic-pain recommendations](https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-s012)

Inspect soles and between toes daily, using a mirror or help if needed. Use suitable footwear; avoid barefoot walking, hot surfaces, and caustic callus treatments. Assess skin breaks promptly, even when painless, and arrange nail and pressure-area care when self-care is unsafe. [NIDDK practical foot care](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/preventing-problems/foot-problems)

An ulcer needs assessment of infection, depth, pressure, and perfusion. Do not prescribe antibiotics merely because an uninfected wound exists. Spreading infection, systemic illness, necrosis, suspected deep infection, or significant ischemia warrants urgent escalation and appropriate surgical/vascular involvement. An ABI can be misleading when vessels are poorly compressible; additional vascular assessment may be needed. Offloading and source control are treatment, not optional additions to an antibiotic prescription. [IWGDF/IDSA diabetic foot infection guidance](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/diabetic-foot-infections/)

A warm, swollen, red foot in a person with neuropathy can be active Charcot neuro-osteoarthropathy even with intact skin and little pain. Infection, thrombosis, and trauma remain alternatives. Arrange urgent specialist assessment and prompt appropriate immobilization/offloading while diagnostic evaluation proceeds; an initially normal radiograph does not exclude active disease. Continued unprotected walking can permit destructive collapse. [IWGDF active Charcot guidance](https://iwgdfguidelines.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/IWGDF-Guidelines-2023.pdf)

### Prepare for illness before the person becomes ill

Provide an individualized written sick-day plan with glucose and ketone monitoring, fluids and carbohydrate intake, medication adjustments, and an explicit contact route. Illness can raise insulin requirements even when less food is eaten. **Do not simply stop basal insulin in type 1 diabetes.** Persistent vomiting, inability to retain fluids, ketones with concerning symptoms, abnormal breathing, dehydration, or altered thinking requires urgent assessment. The acute-care distinctions are detailed in 04A. [CDC sick-day preparation](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/living-with/managing-sick-days.html)

Name medicines to hold and restart criteria. Interrupt SGLT2 inhibitors during ketoacidosis-prone situations; reassess metformin with dehydration, acute renal dysfunction, or hypoxia. Adjust secretagogues and meal insulin for reduced intake while preserving essential basal coverage. Reconcile temporary instructions after hospitalization or procedures to prevent accidental permanent omissions.

### Adapt care for pregnancy, aging, and access barriers

Discuss pregnancy intentions before prescribing. Preconception care includes glucose optimization, contraception while preparing, retinal and kidney evaluation, and review of potentially harmful medicines. Arrange transition before conception rather than abruptly removing effective therapy after a positive pregnancy test without replacement. Insulin is required for type 1 pregnancy and is the preferred glucose-lowering agent for type 2 diabetes and GDM under the ADA pathway. Pregnancy targets differ from this volume's adult table. Coordinate experienced diabetes and obstetric care, and use product-specific washout instructions. [ADA 2026 diabetes and pregnancy](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S321/163918)

Older age alone does not dictate one A1C goal. Healthy older adults often use a goal below 7.0-7.5%; complex/intermediate health may justify below 8%. With very complex illness, advanced cognitive impairment, or limited life expectancy, avoid relying on A1C and prioritize avoiding hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia. Assess cognition, vision, hand function, falls, nutrition, and available help. Simplify or deintensify a hazardous regimen while preserving necessary insulin in type 1 diabetes. A caregiver's ability to perform the plan is part of its safety. [ADA 2026 older-adult care](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690186/)

Ask about rationing, food, transportation, housing, refrigeration, pharmacy access, and insurance. Budget for needles, strips, sensors, rescue medicine, and ketone supplies as well as drugs. Lower-cost human insulin needs specific timing and hypoglycemia teaching, not unsupervised substitution. Secure bridge supplies and assign follow-up. [ADA 2026 population and access guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690171/)

### Integrate the visit into a practical next step

**Clinical Case: Falls and treatment burden.** A 71-year-old caregiver misses lunch, has fallen, and takes sulfonylurea plus basal insulin; A1C is 6.9%. Assess hypoglycemia, cognition, dosing, injury, and other fall causes. Reduce unsafe exposure and agree on feasible monitoring and follow-up.

Rising fasting glucose after an insurance change may instead reflect lost insulin access. Confirm dispensing and use, restore supplies, review technique, then reassess dosing.

Confirm the target, medication changes, low-glucose and sick-day actions, monitoring, and contact route. Assign responsibility for abnormal screening results and follow-up.
