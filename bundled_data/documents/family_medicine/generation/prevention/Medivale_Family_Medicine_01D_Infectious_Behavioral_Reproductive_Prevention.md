# Medivale Family Medicine

## 01D. Infectious, Behavioral, and Reproductive Prevention

### Ask in a way that makes an accurate answer possible

Explain screening and confidentiality, arrange privacy, and ask neutrally about sexual practices, substances, mood, and safety. Identify exposure, timing, anatomic site, prior testing, pregnancy possibility, and follow-up barriers rather than infer risk from demographic labels.

New symptoms need diagnostic assessment; a recent exposure may require urgent prophylaxis. Screening schedules and questionnaires do not replace either decision.

### HIV: pair testing with a prevention and follow-up plan

The **USPSTF framework** recommends HIV screening at **15-65 years**, at younger or older ages when risk is increased, and in all pregnant patients, including those presenting in labor with unknown status. Repeat testing depends on ongoing risk. An initially reactive screening assay requires the appropriate supplemental testing; it should not be presented as a fully classified diagnosis before the testing pathway is completed. [USPSTF HIV screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/human-immunodeficiency-virus-hiv-infection-screening)

Pre-exposure prophylaxis (**PrEP**) is prevention for a person without HIV. Before starting it, establish HIV status and consider whether a recent exposure or acute illness requires additional testing. Medication choice, baseline assessment, and follow-up depend on the regimen and patient circumstances; oral and long-acting options are not interchangeable schedules. [CDC PrEP guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hivnexus/hcp/prep/index.html)

Post-exposure prophylaxis (**PEP**) is time-sensitive. Evaluate a potentially qualifying exposure promptly and start indicated treatment **within 72 hours**, as early as possible, for a **28-day course**. Do not postpone the initial dose while waiting for every pending result. Arrange follow-up testing and a transition to ongoing prevention when appropriate. [CDC PEP guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hivnexus/hcp/pep/index.html)

**Clinical case.** A 29-year-old moving away reports HIV exposure the previous evening. Assess urgent PEP eligibility, obtain baseline tests, and arrange continuity. Today's negative test cannot exclude infection from yesterday.

### Hepatitis B and C: a positive antibody is not always active infection

CDC recommends **one-time adult hepatitis B screening beginning at 18** with **HBsAg, anti-HBs, and total anti-HBc**. Risk can justify repeat testing. These markers answer different questions: surface antigen suggests current infection, surface antibody indicates an immune response, and core antibody reflects prior or current natural infection rather than vaccination alone. [CDC adult HBV screening recommendation](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/rr/rr7201a1.htm)

| HBV marker pattern | Initial interpretation and next step |
| --- | --- |
| HBsAg negative; anti-HBs positive; total anti-HBc negative | Consistent with vaccine-associated immunity; interpret with vaccination history. |
| HBsAg negative; anti-HBs positive; total anti-HBc positive | Consistent with resolved natural infection; note reactivation implications before immunosuppression. |
| All three negative | Susceptible; offer vaccination when indicated. |
| HBsAg positive; total anti-HBc positive | Current infection needs further evaluation; these markers alone do not establish its duration. |
| Isolated total anti-HBc positivity | Several explanations are possible; do not label the patient simply immune or chronically infected without further assessment. |

[CDC HBV result interpretation](https://www.cdc.gov/hepatitis-b/hcp/diagnosis-testing/index.html)

For hepatitis C, CDC recommends testing adults **18 or older** and during **each pregnancy**, with additional testing for ongoing exposure risk. A reactive antibody should lead to **HCV RNA testing**: antibody can remain positive after a cleared or treated infection, whereas detectable RNA establishes current infection. Link current infection to evaluation and treatment rather than repeatedly ordering antibody tests. [CDC HCV testing](https://www.cdc.gov/hepatitis-c/hcp/diagnosis-testing/index.html)

Pregnancy requires a specific infection checklist. HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and syphilis screening are recommended in each pregnancy; earlier results in a previous pregnancy do not automatically satisfy the current one. Results must reach the team responsible for delivery and newborn care. [CDC pregnancy infection screening](https://www.cdc.gov/pregnancy-hiv-std-tb-hepatitis/php/screening/index.html)

### STI screening: select the person and the specimen

USPSTF recommends chlamydia and gonorrhea screening in sexually active women **24 or younger**, and those **25 or older at increased risk**, including pregnant patients in those groups. Its insufficient-evidence statement for asymptomatic men is not a prohibition on testing an exposed or symptomatic patient. [USPSTF chlamydia and gonorrhea screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/chlamydia-and-gonorrhea-screening)

Exposure determines the site to sample. A urine test cannot be assumed to exclude rectal or pharyngeal infection. Use the relevant CDC population- and site-specific recommendations, including more frequent screening for selected patients with ongoing risk. [CDC STI screening recommendations](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/screening-recommendations.htm)

Screen nonpregnant adolescents and adults at increased risk for syphilis. A risk assessment should reflect exposures and local epidemiology rather than stereotypes. Abnormal results require interpretation within the appropriate testing algorithm, assessment of stage and prior treatment, and linkage to treatment and partner services. [USPSTF syphilis screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/syphilis-infection-nonpregnant-adults-adolescents-screening)

**Clinical case.** A negative urine gonorrhea/chlamydia test does not exclude infection after receptive anal exposure. Obtain the appropriate site-specific specimen and arrange result follow-up and partner care.

### Latent tuberculosis: exclude active disease before preventive treatment

Screen asymptomatic adults at increased latent-TB risk, including those born in or resident in high-prevalence countries and certain congregate settings. IGRA or tuberculin testing identifies infection; IGRA has advantages after BCG vaccination. Neither distinguishes latent from active disease. Exclude active TB before preventive treatment; cough with systemic symptoms requires diagnostic assessment. [USPSTF latent TB screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/latent-tuberculosis-infection-screening)

### Mood, anxiety, and substance use: screening must lead somewhere

Screen adults for depression, including pregnant, postpartum, and older adults, and ensure that positive results lead to assessment and effective care. A positive instrument does not establish the diagnosis or exclude bipolar illness, substance effects, or a medical contributor. An insufficient-evidence statement about universal suicide-risk screening does **not** justify ignoring expressed thoughts of self-harm or other warning signs. Those findings require direct safety assessment. [USPSTF depression screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-depression-suicide-risk-adults)

USPSTF supports anxiety screening in asymptomatic adults **19-64**, including pregnancy and postpartum; evidence is insufficient for population screening at 65 or older. Pediatric screening follows a separate recommendation, not an extrapolation of this adult age range. Symptoms warrant evaluation at any age. [USPSTF anxiety screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/anxiety-adults-screening)

For adults, use a brief validated alcohol screen and provide appropriate counseling after unhealthy use is identified. Distinguish risky consumption from a use disorder and from withdrawal risk; they require different care. [USPSTF alcohol screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/unhealthy-alcohol-use-in-adolescents-and-adults-screening-and-behavioral-counseling-interventions)

Drug-use screening means **asking questions**, not universal urine toxicology. It includes nonmedical prescription use and should occur where accurate diagnosis and treatment can be offered or arranged. [USPSTF drug-use screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/drug-use-illicit-screening)

Ask about tobacco use and help patients quit. For nonpregnant adults, behavioral support and appropriate FDA-approved cessation medications are established interventions. In pregnancy, behavioral intervention is recommended; medication decisions require a pregnancy-specific discussion rather than automatic use of the nonpregnant pathway. [USPSTF tobacco cessation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/tobacco-use-in-adults-and-pregnant-women-counseling-and-interventions)

### Violence and reproductive planning: privacy is part of the intervention

USPSTF recommends screening women of reproductive age, including pregnant and postpartum patients, for intimate partner violence and connecting those who screen positive to ongoing, multicomponent support. Ask without a partner present. Assess immediate danger, the safety of children or other dependents, and safe ways to communicate. A visible portal message or printed summary can itself create risk. A negative questionnaire does not invalidate a concerning history, and an insufficient-evidence statement for screening vulnerable older adults does not remove the duty to evaluate suspected abuse. [USPSTF intimate partner violence](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/intimate-partner-violence-and-abuse-of-elderly-and-vulnerable-adults-screening)

Ask whether pregnancy is desired, possible, or to be avoided, without assuming a preferred answer. Review contraception, chronic disease control, medication and supplement exposures, vaccination history, and access to prenatal care. Do not abruptly stop an essential medication without a safer management plan. For people planning or capable of pregnancy, USPSTF recommends **400-800 micrograms of folic acid daily**, beginning at least a month before conception and continuing through early pregnancy. Prior neural-tube defects and other high-risk situations require a separate dosing assessment. [USPSTF folic acid](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/folic-acid-for-the-prevention-of-neural-tube-defects-preventive-medication)

### Everyday prevention should become a feasible action

Discuss food patterns and access, sleep quality and schedule, dental care, and injury risks relevant to the patient's life. Most adults should work toward **150 minutes of moderate activity weekly**, or an equivalent vigorous amount, plus muscle-strengthening activity on **two days**. Begin with achievable changes when illness, disability, safety, or resources limit activity. [CDC adult physical activity](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/adults.html)

Agree on achievable actions and document preferences and follow-up responsibility. See the corresponding chapters for sleep disorders, nutrition, injury prevention, and substance-use treatment.
