# Medivale Family Medicine

## 01F. Integrated Preventive Visits

Adapt preventive decisions to age, previous results, health conditions, goals, and access. A preventive appointment is not an indication for a fixed annual laboratory panel.

### Build the visit around decisions, not a checklist alone

Address urgent symptoms, review priorities and previous care, and select a feasible preventive plan using the encounter framework in **01A and 16A**.

### Children: development, prevention, and the caregiver's observations

Well-child care follows an age-specific schedule rather than a miniature adult annual physical. Growth trajectories, feeding, development, family circumstances, examination, immunization, sensory and oral health, and anticipatory guidance change with age. Use the AAP periodicity schedule and its footnotes to select universal versus risk-based assessment; a child with symptoms or a new developmental concern needs assessment outside the routine timetable. [AAP preventive care framework](https://www.aap.org/periodicityschedule)

Developmental surveillance belongs in routine care. Standardized developmental screening is recommended at **nine, 18, and 30 months**, with autism-specific screening at **18 and 24 months**. A concern raised between those visits should prompt evaluation rather than reassurance based solely on the next scheduled screen. [CDC developmental and autism screening](https://www.cdc.gov/autism/hcp/diagnosis/screening.html)

For children younger than five, apply fluoride varnish beginning with tooth eruption. Swallowed supplements are a separate decision: FDA currently advises limiting ingestible fluoride drug products to children at least three years old at high caries risk, while pediatric dental societies retain risk- and exposure-based recommendations that include younger children. Verify water exposure, product status, and current dental guidance before prescribing; see volume 11A for this current distinction. [USPSTF topical prevention](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/prevention-of-dental-caries-in-children-younger-than-age-5-years-screening-and-interventions1) and [FDA ingestible-product position](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/public-health-focus/ingestible-fluoride-drug-products)

Infant counseling includes a **supine position for every sleep**, a separate firm, flat sleep surface, and avoidance of soft bedding and soft objects. Room sharing is different from bed sharing. Ask where and how the infant actually sleeps so advice can address the family's situation. [CDC infant sleep safety](https://www.cdc.gov/reproductive-health/features/babies-sleep.html)

**Clinical case.** A caregiver reports difficulty affording food and a child's loss of previously used words. Arrange nutritional support and assess developmental regression promptly; vaccine completion does not resolve either concern.

### Adolescents and young adults: create space for confidential care

Include time to speak with the adolescent privately and explain confidentiality and its safety-related limits in understandable terms. Consent and disclosure rules vary by jurisdiction; do not promise secrecy that cannot legally be maintained. Ask about school, sleep, mood, eating, substance use, relationships, sexual exposures, violence, and personal goals without assuming that a parent knows every answer. Check whether portal access, insurance communications, or a shared telephone could inadvertently disclose sensitive care.

Discuss injury risks, sports symptoms, immunizations, reproductive goals, and medication access during school, work, or insurance transitions. A sports form does not replace preventive care; a normal examination does not dismiss concerning exertional symptoms. See sports medicine for clearance criteria.

**Clinical case.** A student moving away needs vaccine records and cannot obtain a chronic medication refill. Arrange records, safe treatment continuity, and a reachable new clinician before the move.

### Midlife: prioritize risk without turning care into indiscriminate testing

Review cancer-screening eligibility, blood pressure confirmation needs, diabetes risk, cardiovascular prevention, vaccines, behavioral health, and exposure-based testing. Family history should include the affected relative, condition, and age at diagnosis; "cancer runs in the family" is a starting point, not a sufficient risk classification. A prior abnormal result may move the patient from screening to surveillance.

Agree on manageable priorities, each with an indication and follow-up plan. Coordinate testing or offer an acceptable alternative when separate visits are impractical. A positive screen still requires diagnostic follow-up.

**Clinical case.** A 52-year-old has incomplete screening records, repeatedly elevated office pressure, and a parent diagnosed with colorectal cancer at 43. Clarify familial screening risk, arrange blood-pressure confirmation, and plan procedures around lost-wage concerns.

### Older adults: function and time to benefit change priorities

Assess daily function, mobility, falls, medications, nutrition, hearing, vision, cognition when concerns arise, caregiver support, and the patient's own goals. An age cutoff is one part of a screening recommendation, not a substitute for considering health, prior results, willingness to pursue treatment, and likely time to benefit. Avoid both extremes: ordering every test despite serious competing illness, or withholding useful care solely because of age.

Reducing medication-related dizziness, improving mobility, and securing food may offer more immediate benefit than screening. Bleeding or new focal weakness still requires diagnostic assessment after routine screening has stopped.

### Pregnancy and chronic illness: integrate rather than duplicate

Pregnancy-specific care includes infection screening, medication review, appropriate vaccination, reproductive counseling, and coordination with the prenatal team. Do not assume that a routine adult panel supplies the prenatal plan or that a prior pregnancy's records replace current testing. Clarify who will review and act on results. Postpartum care also connects pregnancy-related complications to long-term primary care.

Chronic illness changes prevention without erasing it. Review disease-specific complication surveillance, medication monitoring, vaccines affected by risk or immune status, and ordinary age-appropriate care. Identify the reason for each test so screening, disease monitoring, and evaluation of a new symptom are not confused. A preventive appointment should not become an excuse to leave a dangerous new complaint unassessed.

### Close the loop and make the plan usable

Use **16A's result and referral follow-up process** to assign responsibility, timing, and a reliable contact method. Record the indication, previous abnormal result, preferences, access barriers, and specific reasons to seek earlier care. Confirm understanding with teach-back; the recall system should distinguish a completed preventive service from an uncompleted order or an unresolved abnormal result.
