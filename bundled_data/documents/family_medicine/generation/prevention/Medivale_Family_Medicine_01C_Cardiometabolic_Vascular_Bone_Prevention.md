# Medivale Family Medicine

## 01C. Cardiometabolic, Vascular, and Bone Prevention

### Identify what is already known

A visit may combine diabetes screening, blood-pressure confirmation, lipid treatment, and fracture follow-up. Identify the purpose of each test and how either result would change care.

### Blood pressure: screening precedes confirmation

USPSTF recommends office blood-pressure screening for adults **18 and older** without known hypertension, with **out-of-office confirmation before starting treatment** in routine diagnostic evaluation. Use a validated upper-arm device and appropriate technique, including a seated rest. Ambulatory monitoring or properly obtained home readings can help distinguish sustained elevation from a clinic-only pattern.

The suggested screening frequency is annual for adults **40 or older** and those at increased risk; every **3-5 years** may be reasonable for low-risk adults 18-39 with a previous normal reading. These are screening intervals, not intervals for evaluating an already abnormal result. This recommendation also does not supply a complete medication-starting threshold or a universal treatment target. [USPSTF blood pressure screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/hypertension-in-adults-screening)

**Clinical case.** A 36-year-old has an elevated pressure after a stressful commute. Review measurement technique and arrange confirmation before diagnosing chronic hypertension. Symptoms of acute organ injury instead require immediate assessment.

### Diabetes screening: eligibility is not the diagnosis

The 2021 USPSTF framework screens **asymptomatic, nonpregnant adults 35-70 with overweight or obesity**. Consider earlier screening in higher-risk populations and a lower BMI threshold in Asian American patients. Accepted methods include fasting glucose, A1c, and a 75-g oral glucose-tolerance test. After a normal result, approximately **three years** is a reasonable interval, adjusted to context. Prediabetes should lead to an effective preventive intervention, not merely an abnormal-value label. Other organizations have different eligibility criteria, so identify which framework is being used. [USPSTF diabetes screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes)

| Laboratory test | Prediabetes range | Diabetes range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1c | 5.7%-6.4% | At least 6.5% |
| Fasting plasma glucose | 100-125 mg/dL | At least 126 mg/dL |
| Plasma glucose 2 hours after a 75-g oral glucose load | 140-199 mg/dL | At least 200 mg/dL |

In an asymptomatic person, a diabetes-range result ordinarily needs confirmation. A random plasma glucose of at least 200 mg/dL with classic symptoms can establish diabetes in the appropriate clinical setting. A1c may be unreliable when red-cell lifespan or hemoglobin characteristics distort its relationship to glucose; discordance warrants attention rather than automatic acceptance of one test. [NIDDK diabetes testing](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/tests-diagnosis)

**Clinical case.** A 48-year-old night-shift worker with a parent who has diabetes has an A1c of 6.1%. Explain prediabetes and plan prevention around work hours and food access, with follow-up; this result does not establish diabetes.

### Lipids and risk: identify the framework before using a percentage

Cardiovascular risk combines age, blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, and lipids. Interpret a percentage using its endpoint, time horizon, calculator, and eligible population.

In the **2022 USPSTF primary-prevention framework**, prescribe a statin for adults **40-75** with at least one specified risk factor and estimated **10-year CVD risk of 10% or greater**. Offer selectively at **7.5% to less than 10%**. For initiating therapy at 76 or older, evidence is insufficient. The framework excludes known CVD and does not address LDL above 190 mg/dL or familial hypercholesterolemia; those require other disease-specific guidance. [USPSTF statin recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/statin-use-in-adults-preventive-medication)

The **2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline** uses **PREVENT-ASCVD** and updated risk categories in its primary-prevention pathway. The applicable calculation population is adults **30-79**, without known ASCVD or subclinical atherosclerosis, with LDL-C **70-189 mg/dL**. Do not apply an older calculator's thresholds to a PREVENT result. **Section 02C, Lipids and Cardiovascular Risk**, contains the treatment pathway; the USPSTF criteria above remain a separately named preventive framework. [ACC guideline overview](https://www.acc.org/about-acc/press-releases/2026/03/13/18/01/accaha-issue-updated-guideline-for-managing-lipids-cholesterol)

**Example.** An 8% risk estimate cannot determine statin eligibility without the calculator and applicable guideline. Thresholds from different frameworks are not interchangeable.

### Avoid adding tests without a preventive indication

For **asymptomatic adults**, USPSTF recommendations distinguish a service with demonstrated lack of net benefit from one with insufficient evidence:

| Proposed population screen | USPSTF position |
| --- | --- |
| Resting or exercise ECG in adults at low CVD risk | Do not screen; evidence is insufficient at intermediate or high risk. |
| Carotid ultrasonography in the general adult population | Do not screen for asymptomatic carotid stenosis. |
| Spirometry solely to screen for COPD | Do not screen asymptomatic adults; this does not address symptoms or selected very-high-risk populations. |
| Adding ABI, hsCRP, or CAC to conventional risk assessment | Insufficient evidence for population screening benefit. |

An I statement does not prohibit diagnostic testing for symptoms. The separately named ACC/AHA use of **selective CAC risk refinement** is developed in 02C; it is not routine scanning of every well adult. [USPSTF ECG screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/cardiovascular-disease-risk-screening-with-electrocardiography), [carotid screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/carotid-artery-stenosis-screening), [COPD screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/chronic-obstructive-pulmonary-disease-screening), [nontraditional risk assessment](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/RecommendationStatementFinal/cardiovascular-disease-screening-using-nontraditional-risk-assessment)

### Aspirin: prevention has a bleeding cost

Under the **2022 USPSTF primary-prevention recommendation**, starting low-dose aspirin is an individual decision for selected adults **40-59** with **at least 10% estimated 10-year CVD risk**, with only a small net benefit and attention to bleeding risk. Do **not initiate aspirin for primary prevention at 60 or older** under this framework.

This initiation rule does not replace secondary prevention or require every older patient to stop aspirin. Confirm the indication before changing treatment. [USPSTF aspirin recommendation](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/aspirin-to-prevent-cardiovascular-disease-preventive-medication)

**Clinical case.** A 68-year-old taking self-initiated aspirin and a patient taking it after coronary intervention require different reviews. Age alone does not establish the indication.

### Abdominal aortic aneurysm: smoking changes the screening population

The **USPSTF AAA framework** recommends a **one-time ultrasound for men 65-75 who have ever smoked**, commonly defined as **at least 100 cigarettes over a lifetime**, not a 20-pack-year threshold. Selective screening is appropriate for men of that age who never smoked. It recommends against routine screening in women who never smoked and have no family history. Evidence is insufficient for women 65-75 who have ever smoked or have a family history. This is not an instruction either to screen or to avoid screening every woman in the latter group.

AAA screening addresses an asymptomatic population. New symptoms concerning for a vascular emergency require a diagnostic and triage response rather than an appointment for preventive ultrasound. [USPSTF AAA screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/abdominal-aortic-aneurysm-screening)

**Clinical case.** Two asymptomatic 70-year-old men have never had aortic imaging. Remote smoking makes one eligible for routine screening; the never-smoker needs a selective decision. Acute vascular symptoms require diagnostic assessment.

### Bone health: screening, fracture evaluation, and prevention are related

The **2025 USPSTF osteoporosis recommendation** supports screening women **65 or older**, and postmenopausal women younger than 65 who have risk factors and increased fracture risk on clinical assessment. Evidence is insufficient to establish the balance of screening men. This population-screening statement should not be used to avoid evaluation after a fragility fracture, prolonged glucocorticoid exposure, or another reason to suspect secondary bone disease.

Risk factors identify whom to assess; DXA adds bone-density information for fracture-risk and treatment decisions. See chapter 7 for secondary causes, drug selection, contraindications, and monitoring. [USPSTF osteoporosis screening](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/osteoporosis-screening)

**Clinical case.** A 58-year-old postmenopausal woman may qualify for screening after risk assessment. A low-trauma fracture creates a separate indication for evaluation, regardless of the routine screening age.

### Falls, nutrition, and supplements: match the intervention to the problem

A fall is not simply a sign of low bone density. Fracture prevention also depends on balance, strength, vision, medications, blood-pressure responses, footwear, and the home environment. Ask about falls and near-falls, fear of falling, and difficulty rising or walking. A person who has stopped moving because of fear may become weaker and less stable, even without a new fracture.

The **2024 USPSTF recommendation** supports exercise interventions for community-dwelling adults **65 or older at increased fall risk**. Programs commonly develop gait, balance, and strength. Offer multifactorial interventions selectively after considering the individual's risks, preferences, and circumstances rather than assigning the same bundle to every older adult. An unexplained collapse, acute neurologic change, or injury still needs diagnostic evaluation; referral to an exercise class does not resolve those problems. [USPSTF falls prevention](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/falls-prevention-community-dwelling-older-adults-interventions)

Dietary adequacy and medication-like supplementation are different questions. Assess food intake, weight change, swallowing or dental difficulties, food access, and conditions causing malabsorption. Calcium intake targets refer to **total intake**, not a supplement dose added regardless of food: generally **1,000 mg/day** for adults 19-50 and men 51-70, and **1,200 mg/day** for women 51-70 and all adults older than 70. A normal serum calcium does not establish adequate dietary intake because blood calcium is regulated separately. [NIH calcium reference](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Calcium-HealthProfessional/)

Routine fracture-prevention pills are not the same as correcting inadequate intake or treating osteoporosis. The active final **USPSTF 2018** statement recommends against daily supplementation with **400 IU or less of vitamin D and 1,000 mg or less of calcium** for primary fracture prevention in community-dwelling postmenopausal women. Evidence is insufficient for higher doses, or for this preventive use in men and premenopausal women; that is not a recommendation to prescribe larger doses. These conclusions do not govern treatment of vitamin D deficiency or osteoporosis, prior osteoporotic fracture, or increased fall risk. Select supplements for a defined indication and account for dietary intake and adverse effects. [USPSTF final supplementation statement](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/document/final-recommendation-statement/vitamin-d-calcium-or-combined-supplementation-for-the-primary-prevention-of-fractures-in-adults-preventive-medication)

**Clinical case.** A 73-year-old with two recent trips takes a sedating medicine, has a loose hallway rug, and struggles with stairs. Review medication, home hazards, strength, balance, and nutrition rather than prescribe calcium alone.
