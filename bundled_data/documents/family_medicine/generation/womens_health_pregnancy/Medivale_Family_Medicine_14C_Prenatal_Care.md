# Medivale Family Medicine

## 14C. Prenatal Care

Prenatal care establishes dates and risk, prevents illness, and prepares for birth and recovery. Center maternal symptoms and preferences; reassuring screening cannot guarantee well-being. Provide an urgent-care route alongside scheduled prevention.

The main pathways are ACOG tailored prenatal care, ACOG-endorsed SMFM chromosomal screening and U.S. two-step glucose diagnosis. Maternal vaccination uses the named specialist guidance below, including SMFM's 2026-2027 RSV-season addendum. Screening tests, treatment targets and emergency thresholds answer different questions.

### Establish the pregnancy and a usable care plan

Confirm pregnancy and whether location is established. Pain, bleeding, syncope, or hemodynamic change requires emergency/location assessment. Record gravidity and prior term/preterm births, losses, ectopic pregnancy, uterine procedures, hemorrhage, preeclampsia, diabetes, and neonatal outcomes. Prior unexplained stillbirth or very early spontaneous birth may alter surveillance and referral.

Review disease, surgery, transfusions, allergies, all medicines/supplements/substances, family/genetic history, infection, occupation, housing, nutrition, and transport. Privately assess mood, violence, and substance use with actionable support. Explain confidentiality limits and arrange language, disability, and contact accommodations.

ACOG's 2025 model tailors visit frequency and format to risk, social needs, and preference. Telehealth complements but cannot replace indicated examinations, specimens, ultrasound, or reliable pressure measurement. Assign obstetric/chronic-disease care, results, and after-hours contacts. Address barriers after missed visits. [ACOG tailored prenatal care](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-consensus/articles/2025/04/tailored-prenatal-care-delivery-for-pregnant-individuals)

### Date once carefully; do not erase a growth problem

EDD anchors screening, growth, and delivery decisions. LMP dating requires reliable recall and interpretable cycles; irregularity, hormonal use, or uncertain bleeding reduce accuracy. First-trimester crown-rump length is the most accurate ultrasound method; assisted reproduction uses procedure-based dating. Document evidence and revisions, which should be uncommon and gestation-specific. [ACOG pregnancy dating](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date)

Late ultrasound is less precise for dating and should not replace reliable early estimates. Small fundal growth may reflect measurement, dating error, low fluid, or growth restriction; large measurements suggest size, multiples, or excess fluid. Review dates and assess growth/fluid by risk and trajectory.

Under ACOG's early-dating standard, a discrepancy from LMP dating of more than five days before 9 weeks, or more than seven days at 9 weeks through 13 weeks 6 days, supports ultrasound redating. Uncertain LMP favors the earliest reliable crown-rump-length estimate. For IVF, use embryo age and transfer date: a day-five transfer gives an EDD 261 days after transfer, not 280 days after transfer. These early rules must not be reused for third-trimester growth scans. [ACOG dating thresholds](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/05/methods-for-estimating-the-due-date)

**Clinical Case: Preserve established dates.** At 31 weeks by early ultrasound, fundal measurement and a later scan show small size. Redating could conceal growth restriction. Retain established dates, assess growth/fluid, and arrange indicated surveillance.

### Order initial tests for a reason and close the results loop

Assess pressure, weight/BMI, and a focused examination, with additional procedures guided by symptoms/history. Prior negative infection or antibody results may not answer current-pregnancy questions. Track pending, missed, and abnormal tests and referral completion.

| Initial assessment | What it helps establish | Follow-up implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CBC | Anemia and platelet baseline | Interpret with gestation, indices and clinical context |
| ABO/RhD type and antibody screen | Compatibility and alloimmunization risk | Identify the antibody; plan prevention or specialist assessment |
| Rubella immunity; varicella history/immunity assessment | Susceptibility to preventable infection | Exposure counseling and postpartum vaccination when indicated |
| Urine culture | Asymptomatic bacteriuria | Treat qualifying growth with a pregnancy-appropriate targeted agent |
| HIV, syphilis, HBsAg and hepatitis C testing | Maternal infection and transmission-prevention needs | Confirm, treat/link to care and document neonatal implications |
| Gonorrhea/chlamydia and other indicated tests | Age-, exposure- and risk-related infection | Treatment, partner management and organism-specific retesting |

Add tests by history, prevalence, and exposure; repeat CBC and other tests at appropriate windows. Reassess new symptoms despite normal baseline results and explain abnormal findings and next steps. [ACOG routine prenatal tests](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/routine-tests-during-pregnancy)

### Separate carrier testing, fetal screening and diagnosis

Carrier testing asks whether a parent carries a relevant inherited condition; fetal aneuploidy screening estimates the chance of selected chromosomal conditions. Neither should be restricted to stereotypes about ancestry or an age-35 gate. Discuss personal/family history and previous results, the conditions included, residual risk, partner testing when relevant and what information the patient wants. The preconception volume provides the broader carrier-counseling framework.

Offer every pregnant patient the opportunity to accept or decline screening and diagnostic testing after counseling. Under the ACOG-endorsed 2025 SMFM update, cell-free DNA is the most sensitive and specific screening option for common trisomies 21, 18 and 13 and should be routinely available. It is still a screening test, largely reflecting placental DNA. Sex-chromosome screening is an opt-in decision; routine general-population microdeletion screening is not recommended. Do not order several independent aneuploidy screens as though stacking tests creates certainty. [ACOG 2026 endorsement](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-advisory/articles/2026/01/screening-for-fetal-chromosomal-abnormalities), [SMFM updated recommendations](https://publications.smfm.org/publications/620-society-for-maternal-fetal-medicine-consult-series-74cell/)

Positive screens require genetic counseling, detailed ultrasound, and offered diagnostic confirmation before irreversible decisions. Negative screens reduce only tested risks. A no-call is not negative: offer counseling, ultrasound, and diagnostic testing as appropriate. Explain risks and alternatives before merely repeating a sample. [SMFM interpretation and counseling](https://www.smfm.org/news/a-brief-guide-to-smfms-updated-prenatal-genetic-screening-recommendations)

Cell-free DNA screening can begin at approximately 10 weeks. A second-trimester anatomic ultrasound, usually at 18-22 weeks, remains important even after a reassuring genetic screen; cell-free DNA does not assess all structural defects or replace neural-tube-defect evaluation. Discuss serum AFP when indicated within the laboratory's gestational window. [ACOG screening test scope and timing](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/prenatal-genetic-screening-tests)

CVS samples placental tissue, commonly at 10-13 weeks; amniocentesis usually occurs at 15-20 weeks, with later testing possible when indicated. The test selected depends on gestation, the suspected condition, imaging and the patient's priorities. Explain procedure-related risk using current local expertise and the specific procedure, not an outdated universal percentage. [ACOG diagnostic procedure overview](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/amniocentesis)

**Clinical Case: A no-call screen.** At 14 weeks, a 29-year-old's cell-free DNA result is nonreportable from low fetal fraction. Explain that this is not low risk. Review ultrasound and offer genetic counseling/diagnostic options; another sample may delay care or remain nonreportable.

### Build nutrition around adequacy, not supplement stacking

Discuss food access, nausea, dietary restrictions and previous bariatric surgery or eating disorders. A prenatal supplement helps fill predictable gaps but does not replace food. Average-risk neural-tube-defect prevention includes 400 micrograms of folic acid daily; a previous affected pregnancy requires a clinician-directed higher-dose plan, not several prenatal tablets. Avoid confusing micrograms with milligrams. [CDC folic acid clinical overview](https://www.cdc.gov/folic-acid/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html)

Pregnancy increases iron needs; the usual daily requirement is 27 mg. Evaluate anemia rather than assuming every low hemoglobin is iron deficiency or every modest decline is pathological. Calcium needs are generally 1,000 mg/day in adults and 1,300 mg/day in adolescents; vitamin D intake is generally 600 IU/day. Review dietary and supplement totals, tolerability and specific deficiencies. Excess preformed vitamin A and unreviewed megadose products are not a route to healthier fetal development. [ACOG nutrition in pregnancy](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/healthy-eating-during-pregnancy)

| Prepregnancy BMI, singleton pregnancy | Usual total weight-gain range | Clinical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below 18.5 kg/m2 | 28-40 lb | Assess nutrition and barriers to adequate gain |
| 18.5-24.9 kg/m2 | 25-35 lb | Follow trajectory, not one isolated measurement |
| 25.0-29.9 kg/m2 | 15-25 lb | Support nutrition without restrictive crash dieting |
| 30 kg/m2 or higher | 11-20 lb | Individualize with maternal health and fetal growth |

These are singleton ranges based on prepregnancy BMI, not targets for twins or a justification to ignore edema, hyperemesis or growth abnormalities. Use a respectful discussion of trends and health rather than blame. [CDC gestational weight-gain framework](https://www.cdc.gov/maternal-infant-health/pregnancy-weight/)

Encourage a varied diet, careful food handling and avoidance of unpasteurized or inadequately cooked foods. Fish need not be excluded: FDA/EPA advice supports 8-12 ounces weekly from lower-mercury choices, with species and local-catch advisories reviewed. [FDA fish advice](https://www.fda.gov/food/consumers/advice-about-eating-fish) Keep caffeine below 200 mg/day in the ACOG framework and avoid alcohol. Ask directly about nicotine, cannabis and other substances and offer treatment/support rather than reassurance that a "natural" exposure is harmless. [ACOG pregnancy counseling](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/having-a-baby)

For an uncomplicated pregnancy, moderate activity totaling about 150 minutes weekly is generally appropriate. Adapt exercise to prior conditioning, balance, heat, hydration and obstetric contraindications; symptoms require reassessment. Routine bed rest is not a general prevention strategy. [ACOG physical activity](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/exercise-during-pregnancy)

**Clinical Case: Supplement duplication.** A patient with poor intake takes two prenatal tablets, a hair supplement, and extra vitamin A. Review totals, remove unnecessary duplication with the team, and address nausea and food access. Supplements can cause toxicity without correcting inadequate energy or protein.

### Review medicines and imaging without creating untreated disease

Assess exact drug, dose, indication, gestation, and alternatives; old letter categories cannot adequately describe risk. Coordinate essential antiseizure, psychiatric, or opioid-use-disorder treatment rather than stopping abruptly. Include herbal/topical agents and share a written medication list.

ACOG's 2025 guidance continues to support acetaminophen as an appropriate analgesic/antipyretic when clinically indicated, using the lowest effective dose for the shortest necessary time. Assess persistent fever or pain rather than simply continuing a symptom drug. [ACOG acetaminophen guidance](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-advisory/articles/2025/09/acetaminophen-use-in-pregnancy-and-neurodevelopmental-outcomes) FDA advises avoiding NSAIDs from 20 weeks unless specifically directed; fetal renal effects and low amniotic fluid are concerns, with additional later-pregnancy ductal risk. Prescribed low-dose 81-mg aspirin for an obstetric indication is a specified exception, not a contradiction. [FDA pregnancy NSAID warning](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-recommends-avoiding-use-nsaids-pregnancy-20-weeks-or-later-because-they-can-result-low-amniotic)

Use ultrasound/MRI when appropriate, but do not withhold indicated radiography or CT solely for pregnancy. Coordinate with radiology/obstetrics and optimize exposure. Missed appendicitis, PE, or other serious disease may pose greater risk. [ACOG diagnostic imaging guidance](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/10/guidelines-for-diagnostic-imaging-during-pregnancy-and-lactation)

### Vaccinate using the current maternal framework

Review documented doses early and again when the pregnancy reaches a relevant window. Use current maternal guidance with the explicit seasonal RSV pathway below. Discuss benefits, contraindications and access; record product and timing. [ACOG maternal immunizations, 2026](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-statement/articles/2026/02/maternal-immunizations)

| Vaccine decision | Pregnancy approach | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Influenza | Seasonal inactivated or recombinant vaccine when indicated | Do not use live intranasal influenza vaccine in pregnancy |
| COVID-19 | ACOG recommends an updated vaccine during pregnancy, in any trimester | Use the current seasonal formulation and dose history, not an older schedule |
| Tdap | Each pregnancy, usually 27-36 weeks, preferably early in that window | Maternal antibodies protect the young infant |
| Maternal RSV | Eligible Abrysvo at 32 weeks 0 days through 36 weeks 6 days | SMFM's 2026-2027 pathway uses September 1 through March 1 in most continental U.S. locations, with regional adjustment |
| MMR and varicella | Avoid live vaccination during pregnancy | Plan indicated postpartum vaccination for susceptible patients |

Abrysvo is the maternal RSV vaccine; adult RSV products are not interchangeable here. The season above follows the final SMFM 2026-2027 addendum and AAP's September 2, 2026 schedule, not the September-January window still displayed on CDC's overview. A maternal dose in a previous pregnancy is not repeated; use the infant-protection pathway. Infants born less than 14 days after maternal vaccination also need infant antibody protection. Coordinate a planned infant-antibody strategy rather than routinely giving both approaches. Sections 01E and 13A develop that handoff. [SMFM current-season addendum](https://www.smfm.org/rsv-toolkit), [AAP schedule and RSV notes](https://downloads.aap.org/AAP/PDF/AAP-Immunization-Schedule.pdf), [CDC maternal overview](https://www.cdc.gov/rsv/hcp/vaccine-clinical-guidance/pregnant-people.html)

ACOG's March 2026 update recommends routine updated COVID-19 vaccination in pregnancy, a high-risk setting, despite federal/specialist schedule differences. An indicated dose need not await a particular trimester. [ACOG COVID-19 recommendation](https://www.acog.org/news/news-releases/2026/03/acog-reaffirms-strong-recommendation-covid-19-vaccination-pregnancy)

### Distinguish a glucose screen from gestational diabetes

Placental hormones increase insulin resistance as pregnancy progresses. Gestational diabetes occurs when insulin secretion does not adequately compensate, increasing risks that include fetal overgrowth and neonatal hypoglycemia. Assess for previously unrecognized preexisting diabetes early when indicated; a negative early assessment does not eliminate the usual 24-28-week GDM screen.

In the U.S. two-step pathway, a nonfasting 50-g load precedes a one-hour measurement at 24-28 weeks. Institutional positive cutoffs include 130, 135, or 140 mg/dL; the case uses 140. A positive screen requires fasting 100-g, three-hour OGTT. At least two Carpenter-Coustan values meeting thresholds diagnose GDM. The 75-g one-step thresholds and one-abnormal-value rule are separate. [ADA 2026 diagnostic frameworks](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690183/)

| Time after 100-g load | Carpenter-Coustan diagnostic threshold | Separate daily treatment goal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fasting | 95 mg/dL | Below 95 mg/dL |
| One hour | 180 mg/dL | Below 140 mg/dL after a meal |
| Two hours | 155 mg/dL | Below 120 mg/dL after a meal |
| Three hours | 140 mg/dL | Not a routine postmeal treatment time point |

Begin individualized nutrition, safe activity, and glucose monitoring; avoid severe carbohydrate restriction or weight-loss dieting. ADA prefers insulin when goals are unmet. Teach monitoring, hypoglycemia, and contact for illness/repeated elevations. Tailor medication and fetal surveillance to course. Arrange 75-g OGTT at 4-12 weeks postpartum and ongoing surveillance, detailed in 14D. [ADA 2026 pregnancy management](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/49/Supplement_1/S321/163918/15-Management-of-Diabetes-in-Pregnancy-Standards)

**Clinical Case: Confirming GDM.** At 26 weeks, the 50-g screen is 154 mg/dL. The 100-g OGTT gives fasting through three-hour values of 91, 184, 160, and 132 mg/dL. One- and two-hour values establish GDM. The daily one-hour goal is below 140, not the diagnostic threshold of 180.

### Recognize hypertension before it becomes a crisis

Use an appropriate cuff and repeat unexpected pressures. Chronic hypertension predates pregnancy or is identified before 20 weeks. Gestational hypertension is new pressure of at least 140 systolic or 90 diastolic mm Hg after 20 weeks without preeclampsia features, generally confirmed at least four hours apart. Assess renal function, liver tests, platelets and protein when indicated; new organ findings in chronic hypertension raise concern for superimposed preeclampsia.

Preeclampsia combines new hypertension after 20 weeks with proteinuria or specified organ dysfunction. Proteinuria can be at least 300 mg/24 hours or a urine protein/creatinine ratio of at least 0.3. Protein is not required with qualifying organ findings. Severe features include platelets below 100,000/microliter (100 x 10^9/L), creatinine above 1.1 mg/dL or twice baseline without another renal cause, liver enzymes more than twice the upper limit or persistent severe unexplained right-upper-quadrant/epigastric pain unresponsive to medication, pulmonary edema, severe-range pressure, or qualifying persistent headache/visual symptoms. A new headache must be assessed for alternatives, not classified from pain alone. [ACOG hypertensive-disorder framework](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-bulletin/articles/2020/06/gestational-hypertension-and-preeclampsia), [ACOG diagnostic teaching scope](https://www.acog.org/community/districts-and-sections/district-iv/whats-new/countdown-to-intern-year-week-3-hypertensive-disorders)

A systolic pressure of at least 160 or diastolic pressure of at least 110 mm Hg that persists for approximately 15 minutes requires urgent management. Do not wait four hours, a 24-hour urine collection or a full set of laboratory results before escalation. Activate the obstetric emergency pathway; confirmed severe hypertension should receive treatment as soon as possible, within 30-60 minutes. Protocol-selected IV labetalol, IV hydralazine or immediate-release oral nifedipine are first-line options. Magnesium sulfate prevents/treats eclamptic seizures when indicated; it does not replace blood-pressure treatment. [ACOG acute severe-hypertension statement, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30575639/)

For chronic hypertension, ACOG's CHAP integration uses 140/90 mm Hg as the threshold for starting or titrating therapy rather than waiting for severe pressure. That threshold is not itself proof of a universal treatment goal, and the acute severe-hypertension pathway still applies. Review existing drugs for pregnancy suitability; ACE inhibitors and ARBs need a planned alternative. [ACOG CHAP integration, reaffirmed 2025](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-advisory/articles/2022/04/clinical-guidance-for-the-integration-of-the-findings-of-the-chronic-hypertension-and-pregnancy-chap-study)

Offer low-dose aspirin 81 mg/day to eligible patients at high preeclampsia risk or with qualifying combinations of moderate-risk factors, after contraindication review. High-risk examples include prior preeclampsia, chronic hypertension, renal disease, preexisting diabetes, autoimmune disease and multifetal gestation. Start between 12 and 28 weeks, optimally before 16, and continue until delivery under the obstetric plan. Aspirin prevention does not treat established preeclampsia. Delivery timing and inpatient/outpatient monitoring require obstetric assessment of gestation, maternal severity and fetal status. [ACOG aspirin prevention](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2018/07/low-dose-aspirin-use-during-pregnancy)

### Prevent infection-related maternal and neonatal illness

Screen for HIV, hepatitis B/C, syphilis, and indicated gonorrhea/chlamydia. ACOG recommends syphilis testing at the first encounter, third trimester, and birth regardless of recognized risk. Follow local reporting and confirmatory algorithms. [CDC prenatal infection testing](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/pregnant.htm) [ACOG updated syphilis screening](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-advisory/articles/2024/04/screening-for-syphilis-in-pregnancy)

Syphilis requires stage-appropriate penicillin treatment; pregnant patients with a true penicillin allergy need desensitization and penicillin rather than an unproven substitute. Track doses, serologic response, partner treatment and fetal/neonatal planning. [CDC syphilis in pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/syphilis-pregnancy.htm) For newly diagnosed HIV, arrange expert perinatal care and start effective ART as soon as possible, regardless of CD4 count or viral load. Do not delay routinely to the second trimester. Delivery and neonatal plans depend on virologic control and circumstances, not HIV positivity alone. [NIH perinatal ART recommendations](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/perinatal/recommendations-arv-drugs-pregnancy-prevent-hiv-transmission-improve-health)

An HBsAg-positive result needs maternal assessment, including HBV DNA when indicated, and a documented newborn prevention plan. Infants born to an HBsAg-positive parent need hepatitis B vaccine and HBIG within 12 hours of birth at separate injection sites, followed by the appropriate series and serologic follow-up. Transmit the actual laboratory result to the delivery/newborn team. [CDC perinatal hepatitis B prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/hepatitis-b/hcp/perinatal-provider-overview/)

Culture urine once early. ASB at 100,000 CFU/mL or more generally needs 5-7 days of targeted treatment; single-dose fosfomycin is a lower-tract exception. Choose by culture, gestation, allergy, and precautions. Section 06A covers pregnancy-specific durations, drug precautions, fosfomycin, and follow-up. Assess symptoms regardless of colony count. Fever/flank pain suggests pyelonephritis: arrange urgent obstetric assessment and initial inpatient ACOG management. Nitrofurantoin and fosfomycin lack renal-parenchymal activity. Routine cultures at every visit after a negative screen are unnecessary. [ACOG pregnancy urinary infection guidance](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/clinical-consensus/articles/2023/08/urinary-tract-infections-in-pregnant-individuals)

Screen for GBS with a vaginal-rectal specimen at 36 weeks 0 days through 37 weeks 6 days unless intrapartum prophylaxis is already indicated by GBS bacteriuria during this pregnancy or a previous affected newborn. Any GBS bacteriuria is relevant to the intrapartum plan; low-count asymptomatic growth does not automatically require antepartum antibiotics. Prelabor cesarean with intact membranes does not require GBS-specific prophylaxis, though usual surgical prophylaxis still applies. Penicillin allergy needs a documented severity assessment and appropriate susceptibility/prophylaxis pathway. [ACOG GBS prevention](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2020/02/prevention-of-group-b-streptococcal-early-onset-disease-in-newborns)

**Clinical Case: GBS bacteriuria.** Asymptomatic GBS growth of 20,000 CFU/mL is below the antepartum ASB treatment threshold but indicates intrapartum prophylaxis. Document it, review symptoms/specimen context, and distinguish labor prophylaxis from current UTI treatment.

### Use Rh prophylaxis for prevention, not established sensitization

Check ABO/RhD and the antibody screen early. An unsensitized RhD-negative patient commonly receives antenatal anti-D prophylaxis at about 28 weeks and postpartum prophylaxis within 72 hours when the infant is RhD-positive. Review the exact product, route and dose; RhoGAM's label specifies 300 micrograms IM at 26-28 weeks for its routine antenatal indication. Bleeding, trauma and invasive procedures may require additional assessment and prophylaxis, with extra dosing for a large fetomaternal hemorrhage. [ACOG Rh overview](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/the-rh-factor-how-it-can-affect-your-pregnancy), [RhoGAM prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=d87e4d0b-2442-4135-b3f9-5c4f74845b87)

A positive antibody screen requires identification and interpretation: passive anti-D after prophylaxis differs from established immune anti-D, and other antibodies can also affect the fetus. Rh immune globulin does not reverse established anti-D sensitization. Coordinate blood-bank and maternal-fetal-medicine evaluation rather than simply repeating prophylaxis for every positive result. Early pregnancy loss/abortion before 12 weeks has a separate updated counseling pathway; do not automatically extrapolate later-pregnancy prophylaxis rules to every early loss.

### Anticipate preterm birth without using superseded prevention

Obtain details of prior preterm births, including spontaneous labor, membrane rupture and medically indicated delivery. Risk is not identical across these histories. Selected patients need transvaginal cervical-length surveillance and specialist prevention planning. Injectable 17-hydroxyprogesterone caproate is no longer recommended for recurrent preterm-birth prevention; vaginal progesterone is not a universal replacement for prior preterm birth without cervical shortening. [ACOG updated progestogen guidance](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-advisory/articles/2023/04/updated-guidance-use-of-progestogen-supplementation-for-prevention-of-recurrent-preterm-birth)

For an asymptomatic singleton pregnancy without a prior spontaneous preterm birth, SMFM defines a short transvaginal cervix as 25 mm or less. Vaginal progesterone is recommended for a length of 20 mm or less before 24 weeks; 21-25 mm is a shared-decision range. These recommendations are population-specific, not automatic rules for twins, cervical dilation or every prior-loss history. [SMFM short-cervix guidance](https://publications.smfm.org/publications/560-society-for-maternal-fetal-medicine-consult-series-70/)

Contractions, pelvic pressure, fluid leakage or bleeding before term warrant assessment of maternal infection, membrane status, cervical change and fetal well-being. Treatment is not indiscriminate suppression of every contraction. Antenatal corticosteroids are generally recommended at 24 weeks 0 days through 33 weeks 6 days when delivery is likely within seven days; selected earlier or later gestations require their own criteria and counseling. Do not delay an otherwise necessary delivery solely to complete steroids. [ACOG antenatal corticosteroids](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2017/08/antenatal-corticosteroid-therapy-for-fetal-maturation)

### Respond to bleeding, pain and changed movement promptly

Early bleeding or pain can reflect miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy or another abdominal process. Instability, syncope, peritoneal signs or severe pain require emergency care. In a stable pregnancy of unknown location, serial quantitative hCG and ultrasound may be necessary; one hCG result or one inconclusive scan should not automatically trigger a definitive loss diagnosis or treatment that could interrupt a viable pregnancy. [ACOG early pregnancy loss evaluation](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-bulletin/articles/2018/11/early-pregnancy-loss)

Later bleeding requires urgent obstetric assessment for causes including placenta previa and abruption. Amount seen externally may not reflect total blood loss. Abdominal pain, uterine tenderness, contractions, fluid leakage and maternal/fetal status guide urgency, not the presence or absence of one "classic" pattern. [ACOG bleeding assessment](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/bleeding-during-pregnancy)

When previa is possible, do not perform a digital cervical examination until ultrasound has excluded it; a carefully performed speculum examination is a different assessment. Do not delay resuscitation for imaging. [RCOG antepartum-bleeding examination safety](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)

Decreased fetal movement after a movement pattern is established needs prompt advice and assessment; do not defer a clear change until the next routine visit or use a home Doppler as proof of well-being. Severe headache, visual changes, persistent chest pain, trouble breathing, fever, one-sided leg swelling or inability to keep fluids down are additional warning signs. Give a specific contact/transport plan and use teach-back to confirm understanding. [CDC urgent maternal warning signs](https://www.cdc.gov/hearher/maternal-warning-signs/index.html)

**Clinical Case: Reduced movement.** At 34 weeks, markedly reduced movement since yesterday requires prompt obstetric assessment, not an appointment in three days. Absent pain/bleeding or a heartbeat on home Doppler does not establish well-being.

### Treat common symptoms while keeping the differential open

For nausea/vomiting, assess timing, intake, weight, urine, medicines, pain, fever, headache, and bleeding. Try small meals, trigger avoidance, and hydration. Vitamin B6 is first-line; add doxylamine when appropriate, checking formulation and sedation. Persistent symptoms need reassessment and possibly another antiemetic. [ACOG nausea and vomiting overview](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/morning-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy)

One labeled combination is Diclegis delayed-release doxylamine 10 mg/pyridoxine 10 mg per tablet: start two tablets nightly on an empty stomach, swallowed whole. Persistent symptoms permit stepwise titration to one morning, one midafternoon and two bedtime tablets, maximum four daily. Use scheduled rather than as-needed dosing; avoid alcohol/other sedatives and review MAOI contraindication. This formulation is not interchangeable tablet-for-tablet with other strengths, and was not studied for hyperemesis. [Diclegis formulation and dosing](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=8bde08f6-e8bd-4e90-baa7-82e01876a86a)

Hyperemesis can cause dehydration, electrolyte abnormalities and nutritional deficiency. Inability to eat/drink or major functional impairment warrants assessment, with IV fluids and monitoring when needed. Prolonged vomiting can deplete thiamine; administer thiamine before dextrose when depletion is suspected rather than precipitating neurologic injury during resuscitation. New severe vomiting later in pregnancy should prompt a broader differential, including hepatobiliary disease, infection, metabolic illness and preeclampsia. [ACOG hyperemesis treatment scope](https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/practice-bulletin/articles/2018/01/nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy), [RCOG current patient guidance](https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/pregnancy-sickness-nausea-and-vomiting-of-pregnancy-and-hyperemesis-gravidarum)

Reflux often improves with smaller meals and avoiding lying down soon after eating. Choose a pregnancy-appropriate antacid or alginate with pharmacist/clinician input; separate antacids from iron/folate supplements by at least two hours. Persistent symptoms may need acid suppression and diagnostic review. Dysphagia, bleeding, weight loss or severe chest/upper-abdominal pain should not be dismissed as routine heartburn. [NHS pregnancy reflux care](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/indigestion-and-heartburn/)

For constipation, review fiber, fluids, activity and medicines. Adjust an intolerable iron preparation with the care team rather than abandoning treatment of documented deficiency. Select a pregnancy-appropriate laxative when conservative measures are insufficient; severe pain, vomiting, bleeding or obstruction symptoms require evaluation. Urinary frequency without pain can be physiological, but dysuria, fever or flank pain needs testing. [NHS common pregnancy symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/common-health-problems/)

Back or pelvic-girdle pain may improve with activity modification and physical therapy. Fever, urinary symptoms, trauma, contractions or neurological deficits require assessment beyond mechanical pain. [NHS pelvic-girdle pain](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/pelvic-pain/)

New persistent itching, especially palms/soles without a primary rash, warrants serum bile acids and liver transaminases for intrahepatic cholestasis; repeat testing if symptoms persist despite initially normal results. Arrange obstetric assessment because bile-acid severity changes fetal surveillance and delivery planning. In SMFM's ACOG-endorsed pathway, ursodeoxycholic acid is first-line for maternal symptoms, not a guarantee against stillbirth. [SMFM cholestasis recommendations](https://publications.smfm.org/publications/374-society-for-maternal-fetal-medicine-consult-series-53/), [RCOG symptom and repeat-testing guidance](https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/intrahepatic-cholestasis-of-pregnancy/)

**Clinical Case: New late-pregnancy vomiting.** At 30 weeks, recurrent vomiting with right-upper-quadrant pain after resolved morning sickness needs vital signs, hydration assessment, and urgent obstetric evaluation for hypertensive, hepatic, and other acute disease.

### Make follow-up a continuous safety system

Review symptoms, pressure, growth, mental health, medicines, tests, and referrals. Document delivery/urgent contacts, GBS/antibody status, infections, and newborn prevention. Arrange postpartum feeding, contraception, mood, and chronic-disease follow-up.
