# Medivale Family Medicine

## 16C. Ethics and Professional Practice

Ethical guidance describes professional obligations. Legal authority, reporting thresholds, and required procedures also depend on jurisdiction and setting; a national ethics opinion is not a substitute for applicable law.

### Identify the decision before naming a principle

Define the decision: treatment refusal, authorization, disclosure, or resource allocation. Establish clinical facts, urgency, options, preferences, capacity, and participants' authority. Distinguish a relative's concern from legal authority and a clinician's recommendation from permission to treat.

**Autonomy** supports informed choice; **beneficence** and **nonmaleficence** address benefit and harm; **justice** addresses fairness and access. A request does not require inappropriate treatment, and expected benefit does not override a capable patient's refusal. Seek a clinically appropriate, minimally coercive resolution.

### Informed consent is a conversation, not a signature

Explain the working diagnosis, purpose of the proposed intervention, expected benefits, material risks and burdens, reasonable alternatives, and the consequences of forgoing it. Give the patient an opportunity to ask questions and decide voluntarily. Confirm understanding in accessible language and document the discussion and decision. A signed form cannot repair an absent conversation or an authorization obtained through coercion. [AMA informed consent](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/informed-consent)

**Decision-making capacity** is assessed for the particular decision at the particular time. Evaluate whether the patient can understand the information, appreciate its relevance to their own situation, reason about the options, and communicate a choice. A psychiatric diagnosis, disability, old age, or disagreement does not by itself establish incapacity. Address communication barriers and reversible contributors before concluding that capacity is absent. Capacity is a clinical assessment; competence is generally a legal determination. [AAFP capacity assessment](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2018/0701/p40)

An emergency exception is narrow: urgent treatment may proceed when the patient cannot participate and an authorized surrogate is unavailable, rather than delaying necessary care while consent is sought. It does not erase a known applicable refusal or turn routine convenience into an emergency. Explain events and obtain authorization for ongoing care as soon as possible. [AMA informed consent](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/informed-consent)

**Clinical case.** A 49-year-old refuses admission because a dependent relative is alone. Explain risks and alternatives, assess capacity, and offer practical help. Refusal does not establish incapacity; acute confusion impairing appreciation changes the pathway, regardless of a signed departure form.

### When capacity is impaired, preserve the patient's voice

Identify an authorized surrogate using the patient's designation and applicable law. Do not assume that the oldest relative, the loudest participant, or the person paying the bills automatically has authority. Continue involving the patient to the extent possible. **Substituted judgment** asks what the patient would choose, using known preferences and values. When those cannot reasonably be established, a **best-interests** assessment weighs expected benefit, burden, suffering, and the patient's circumstances. Unresolved disputes or a surrogate acting contrary to the patient's interests warrant ethics or other appropriate consultation. [AMA decisions for adults lacking capacity](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/decisions-adult-patients-who-lack-capacity)

An advance directive can communicate preferences and designate a decision-maker. Confirm the document, its applicability, and whether circumstances match the situation it addresses. A capable patient's current wishes take priority over an older directive. A document naming a future surrogate does not displace the decisions of a patient who can currently decide. Translate relevant preferences into accessible clinical documentation and appropriate orders rather than assuming that a scanned document will be found during a crisis. [AMA advance directives](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/advance-directives)

**Clinical case.** A designated agent describes an incapacitated patient's prior wishes while another relative objects. Verify authority and the applicability of those wishes; seek consultation for unresolved conflict. Family voting does not determine the patient's choice.

### DNR defines a resuscitation decision, not an absence of care

A **do-not-resuscitate / do-not-attempt-resuscitation order** addresses resuscitation after cardiopulmonary arrest. It does not by itself mean no antibiotics, no analgesia, no hospitalization, or no treatment before arrest. Decisions about intubation, intensive care, and other interventions must be clarified separately. Discuss likely outcomes in the patient's clinical context, document the decision, and revisit it when circumstances change. Periprocedural plans require explicit discussion rather than an assumed automatic cancellation or automatic continuation without considering the situation. [AMA DNAR guidance](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/orders-not-attempt-resuscitation-dnar)

There is no ethical distinction between withholding and withdrawing a life-sustaining intervention when the decision appropriately reflects the patient's wishes and goals. A time-limited trial can define the hoped-for response and the point for reassessment. Stopping an intervention does not mean stopping comfort care, communication, or support. Palliative care can accompany disease-directed treatment; specific symptom management is developed in chapter 13. [AMA withholding and withdrawing treatment](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/withholding-or-withdrawing-life-sustaining-treatment)

**Clinical case.** A patient with a DNR order wants hospitalization for an infection. Treat the infection in accordance with those goals; DNR limits CPR, not all care. Document any separate limits on hospitalization or intubation.

### Confidentiality requires a purpose and an appropriate recipient

Protect information disclosed during care. Before sharing, identify the purpose, recipient, authorization or other applicable basis, and the information needed for that purpose. Do not assume that family membership, employment, or a request from an outside organization creates unlimited access. When disclosure is ethically or legally justified, avoid unnecessary disclosure and explain it to the patient when appropriate. Privacy also applies to conversations in public areas, images, electronic messages, and records accessed without a care-related reason. [AMA confidentiality](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/confidentiality)

**Adolescent confidentiality is service- and jurisdiction-specific.** Parents usually serve as a minor's personal representatives, but exceptions can apply when the minor legally consents to a particular service, another person is legally authorized to consent, or a parent agrees to a confidential relationship. Access can still depend on applicable state law; abuse or endangerment raises additional exceptions. Do not teach a universal age at which every record becomes private or a universal promise that all adolescent care is confidential. [HHS parental access and exceptions](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/can-i-access-medical-record-if-i-have-power-of-attorney/index.html)

Ask developmentally appropriate patients to participate and seek their assent, even when a parent or guardian supplies legal permission. A parent's refusal that threatens a child's essential interests requires careful assessment and appropriate escalation rather than an assumption that parental authority is unlimited. Distinguish routine disagreement from a potentially dangerous refusal and seek the appropriate pediatric, ethics, and legal support. [AMA pediatric decision-making](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/pediatric-decision-making)

**Clinical case.** An adolescent asks about a sensitive result in a parent-accessible portal. Check consent rules, parental access rights, portal behavior, and insurance communications before promising confidentiality. Arrange a safe, lawful contact plan.

### Reporting duties and threat-related disclosure are not the same rule

When abuse, neglect, or another reportable concern is suspected, address immediate safety, document objective observations and relevant statements, and determine the applicable reporting duty, recipient, and timeline. Do not delay an indicated report solely to conduct an independent investigation or obtain certainty that the law does not require. Child, elder, dependent-adult, injury, and communicable-disease requirements differ; neither adult partner violence nor every difficult family interaction has one identical nationwide reporting rule.

The HIPAA Privacy Rule permits specified disclosures for public health, child-abuse reporting, certain abuse/neglect situations, and other defined circumstances. Permission under a federal privacy rule is not the same as a state law imposing a report. Identify both the disclosure basis and the actual reporting obligation, using current authoritative requirements. [45 CFR 164.512](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.512)

HIPAA can permit necessary disclosure to a person able to prevent or lessen a **serious and imminent threat**, consistent with applicable law and professional standards. A duty to protect or warn is a separate jurisdiction-dependent question. Assess the threat and immediate safety needs; obtain urgent help when indicated, and share information with an appropriate recipient rather than broadcasting it. Document the basis and actions taken. [HHS threat-related disclosure](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/what-constitutes-serious-imminent-threat-that-would-permit-health-care-provider-disclose-phi-to-prevent-harm-patient-public-without-patients-authorization-permission/index.html)

### Fairness, conflicts, and boundaries are everyday clinical obligations

Scarce-resource decisions should use transparent, medically relevant criteria and a fair process. Social status, perceived deservingness, or blame for illness should not determine who receives care. Explain the applicable process and involve institutional resources when a decision exceeds an individual clinician's role. Justice does not mean pretending that every patient has identical needs or opportunities. [AMA resource allocation](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/allocating-limited-health-care-resources)

A financial interest must not drive an unnecessary test or referral. Disclose relevant interests, provide appropriate alternatives, and manage the conflict; disclosure alone does not make a harmful arrangement acceptable. The ethical assessment and the legal rules governing financial arrangements are related but not interchangeable. [AMA self-referral](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/physician-self-referral)

Romantic or sexual involvement with a current patient is unethical. Ending care does not automatically remove the power imbalance or make a later relationship acceptable; exploitation of trust or foreseeable harm can persist. Maintain professional boundaries in person and through electronic contact. [AMA professional boundaries](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/romantic-or-sexual-relationships-patients)

### Clinician well-being and safe practice need different assessments

WHO describes burnout as a work-related phenomenon involving exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism toward work, and reduced professional efficacy, not a medical diagnosis in itself. Do not assume that every exhausted clinician has only burnout; depression, substance-use problems, sleep disorders, and medical illness may require assessment. Conversely, receiving mental health treatment does not by itself establish unsafe practice. [WHO burnout framework](https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/frequently-asked-questions/burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon)

Health professionals should obtain appropriate care, assess their ability to work safely, and avoid practice when illness or substance effects impair safe care. Planning coverage is part of protecting patients, not evidence that seeking help is a professional failure. [AMA physician health](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/physician-health-wellness)

When a colleague appears unable to practice safely, respond respectfully and protect patients through the appropriate supervisory, occupational-health, physician-health, or reporting pathway. Record observed behavior rather than an unsupported diagnosis. Reporting and return-to-work requirements depend on the circumstances and law. [AMA responsibilities to impaired colleagues](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/physician-responsibilities-colleagues-illness-disability-or-impairment)

Organizations should address workload, workflow, staffing, safe communication, and access to help alongside individual support. Resilience training alone cannot correct an unsafe system; organizational well-being frameworks are not diagnostic instruments. [NIOSH professional well-being](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/healthcare/impactwellbeing/professional-wellbeing.html)

**Clinical case.** A colleague repeatedly falls asleep during encounters and makes new documentation errors. Arrange safe coverage and assessment through clinical leadership. Document observed impairment without guessing its cause or merely completing the work for them.

### Use the correct authority and leave a clear record

Document the problem, urgency, understanding, preferences, authorized participants, alternatives, decision, and follow-up. Apply jurisdiction- and setting-specific law; consult ethics, safeguarding, privacy, or legal resources as needed without delaying immediate safety measures.
