# Medivale Family Medicine

## 12A. Mood and Anxiety Disorders

Mood/anxiety illness may present as insomnia, fatigue, pain, gastrointestinal symptoms, or impaired responsibilities. Ask about feelings, function, changes, and fears. Diagnosis requires clinical assessment, not a questionnaire or negative laboratory panel; medical illness, substances, trauma, and psychiatric disease can coexist.

VA/DoD depression and disorder-specific anxiety/OCD/PTSD guidance support adult care. Youth, pregnancy/postpartum, severe illness, and complex regimens require population-specific pathways; adult recommendations do not establish pediatric indications.

### Establish a depressive syndrome and its context

Assess mood, interest/pleasure, sleep, appetite/weight, energy, concentration, psychomotor change, guilt/worthlessness, and death/self-harm thoughts. A major depressive episode ordinarily requires **at least five symptoms in the same two-week period**, including depressed mood or anhedonia, with significant distress/impairment. Establish duration and daily pattern; assess substances, medicines, medical/psychiatric alternatives, and lifetime mania/hypomania. A depressive episode alone does not establish unipolar MDD. [VA/DoD depression assessment framework](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/mdd/VADoDMDDCPGFinal508.pdf)

Document function in concrete terms: missed work, inability to prepare meals, neglected hygiene, reduced caregiving, loss of school participation, or withdrawal from meaningful activities. Examine appearance, behavior, speech, mood/affect, thought process/content, perception, cognition, insight, and judgment. Psychosis, catatonia, inability to eat/drink or care for basic needs, and substantial safety concerns change urgency. Irritability can be prominent in youth; older adults may emphasize physical or cognitive complaints rather than sadness.

Depression reflects biological, psychological, developmental, and social influences, not one measurable serotonin deficiency; medication response does not prove that model. Assess prior episodes/treatment, family history, trauma, pain, sleep, substances, support, financial stress, discrimination, violence, and medical comorbidity. Neither a single stressor nor its absence fully explains the illness. [NIMH depression overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression)

| Presentation | Distinction to establish | Clinical implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major depressive syndrome | Persistent core symptoms with sufficient associated symptoms and impairment | Assess polarity, safety, severity, and treatment preferences |
| Grief after a death | Waves of loss-related distress may coexist with preserved positive emotion and self-esteem | Support and assess; bereavement does not exclude a concurrent major depressive episode |
| Adjustment disorder | Symptoms linked to a stressor without a better-fitting disorder | Address stress/support and safety; the label does not establish low suicide risk |
| Persistent depressive disorder | Chronic depressed mood, typically at least two years in adults | Assess chronic impairment and superimposed major episodes; chronicity is not normality |

Adjustment symptoms begin within three months of a stressor and generally do not persist beyond six months after the stressor and its consequences end; ongoing stress can sustain symptoms. Prolonged grief disorder is different from early bereavement: DSM-5-TR requires at least 12 months since the death in adults, or six months in children/adolescents, together with characteristic persistent symptoms and impairment exceeding cultural expectations. A time threshold alone does not diagnose it. Targeted grief treatment differs from automatically prescribing an antidepressant for normal grief. [APA stressor-related distinctions](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/ptsd/what-is-ptsd), [APA prolonged grief guidance](https://www.psychiatry.org/news-room/news-releases/apa-offers-tips-for-understanding-prolonged-grief)

For persistent depressive disorder, depressed mood is present most of the day, more days than not, for at least two years in adults or one year in youth, with at least two of appetite change, sleep disturbance, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration/indecision, or hopelessness. Symptom-free intervals do not exceed two months. Assess impairment, polarity, and alternative causes; chronic mild symptoms and a superimposed major episode both deserve treatment. [SAMHSA persistent-depression criteria](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/SAMHSA_Digital_Download/PEP20-02-01_004.pdf)

**Clinical Case.** Six weeks after a sibling's death, a 57-year-old has loss-related sadness but enjoys friends and retains self-worth. Offer support and assess function, sleep, substances, and safety. Reassess pervasive anhedonia, worthlessness, sustained neurovegetative symptoms, or suicidality for concurrent depression; bereavement does not preclude diagnosis.

### Use screening and testing for their proper purposes

A positive PHQ-2/PHQ-9 or another validated screen prompts diagnostic evaluation. A negative screen does not override concerning history, and a total score does not determine disposition. Review individual answers, particularly thoughts of death/self-harm, and use repeated measures to track symptoms alongside function. The USPSTF adult depression recommendation includes pregnancy/postpartum, but its insufficient-evidence statement on screening asymptomatic adults for suicide risk does not discourage direct safety assessment when symptoms or concerns exist. [USPSTF adult depression and suicide-screening scope](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-depression-suicide-risk-adults)

Use history/examination to select CBC, thyroid tests, chemistry, B12, or other investigations for fatigue, weight change, bleeding, neurologic symptoms, medicine effects, or systemic illness. Consider apnea, endocrine disease, anemia, pain, intoxication/withdrawal, and neurologic illness. Avoid indiscriminate panels or delaying needed care; normal results do not establish psychiatric causation.

### Ask directly about suicide and act on the whole assessment

Ask privately and directly about death, self-harm, and suicide: frequency, recency, intent, plan, means, preparation, attempts, and recent changes. Assess intoxication, agitation, psychosis, insomnia, impulsivity, hopelessness, losses, pain, support, reasons for living, and care participation. Protective factors do not cancel intent or behavior. Direct questioning does not create suicidal thinking.

A positive screen requires a timely clinical safety assessment. Current suicidal intent, inability to maintain safety, a recent attempt or concerning preparation, severe intoxication/psychosis, or an otherwise unstable presentation may require emergency psychiatric and medical evaluation. Maintain observation and a safe environment while arranging an appropriate handoff; do not leave an acutely unsafe person alone or send them to drive to another facility. Assess medical consequences of self-harm even if the person later denies intent. [VA/DoD 2024 suicide-risk framework](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/srb/VADOD-CPG-Suicide-Risk-Full-CPG-2024_Final_508.pdf)

Do not use a numerical score, a low/medium/high label, a denial of a plan, or a promise not to act as a discharge rule. Risk can change quickly, especially with intoxication, a new stressor, treatment changes, or care transitions. Explain the limits of confidentiality when immediate safety requires action, involve appropriate supports, and follow local law and institutional procedures. A screening tool can organize questions; it cannot predict an individual's future with certainty. [NICE self-harm assessment safeguards](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225/chapter/recommendations)

**Clinical Case.** A 45-year-old initially denies a suicide plan, then describes preparation, escalating alcohol use, and doubt about staying safe tonight. Arrange immediate supervised evaluation and communicate these findings; a denial, questionnaire total, or safety promise cannot establish safe discharge.

For appropriate outpatients, develop a usable collaborative safety plan, emergency triggers, and prompt follow-up. It is not a no-suicide contract or substitute for hospitalization. Document assessment, consultation, participation, disposition, support, and responsibility after transfer or missed visits. [NIMH adult outpatient safety assessment](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials/adult-outpatient/adult-outpatient-brief-suicide-safety-assessment-guide)

For adults who screen positive, the NIMH pathway suggests a check-in within 48 hours when possible; if further risk evaluation is needed but emergency disposition is not indicated, arrange assessment preferably within 72 hours. These intervals do not justify waiting when safety deteriorates.

| Safety-plan component | Practical focus |
| --- | --- |
| Recognize warning signs | Identify the patient's own changes in thoughts, sleep, behavior, or circumstances |
| Use coping strategies | Choose specific feasible actions rather than vague instructions to stay positive |
| Contact people and services | Identify accessible support, clinical contacts, crisis services, and emergency escalation |
| Reduce access to lethal means | Collaboratively arrange safer storage/access, including medicines, with appropriate help |
| Review and reconnect | Confirm the plan is available and understandable; arrange follow-up and reassess changes |

Match respectful, concrete means-safety counseling to household risks, including limited medicine quantities or supervised administration when appropriate. Confirm that caregivers and appointments are available. Safety planning cannot guarantee prevention; the 2024 VA/DoD framework does not establish one worksheet as a proven stand-alone treatment.

### Check polarity before prescribing an antidepressant

Ask about previous distinct periods of unusually elevated or irritable mood accompanied by increased energy/activity, decreased need for sleep, pressured speech, racing thoughts, grandiosity, distractibility, or consequential risk-taking. Reduced need for sleep differs from insomnia followed by exhaustion. Establish duration, observable change, consequences, substance/medication exposure, and whether other people noticed the episode. Ask about family bipolar history, prior antidepressant-associated activation, psychosis, and episodes around childbirth.

Mania generally lasts **at least one week**, or any duration when hospitalization is necessary, with marked impairment; psychotic features indicate mania rather than hypomania. Hypomania ordinarily lasts at least four consecutive days, represents a clear change, and lacks marked impairment, hospitalization, or psychosis. The mood/energy change also needs at least three associated manic symptoms, or four when mood is only irritable. Bipolar I requires a manic episode, not a mandatory history of depression. Bipolar II involves hypomania plus major depression without a manic episode. Do not diagnose either from ordinary daily mood variation alone. [NIMH bipolar definitions](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/bipolar-disorder), [SAMHSA episode criteria](https://library.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/SAMHSA_Digital_Download/PEP20-02-01_004.pdf)

Questionnaires cannot substitute for the longitudinal interview and collateral history. NICE specifically advises against using questionnaires to identify bipolar disorder in primary care; reported overactivity/disinhibition lasting four days or more merits consideration of specialist assessment. Suspected mania, dangerous behavior, psychosis, or severe depression requires urgent evaluation. Mixed depressive and energized/agitated symptoms deserve particular attention, not automatic antidepressant escalation. [NICE bipolar recognition and referral](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185/chapter/recommendations)

**Clinical Case.** A 39-year-old with recurrent low mood reports separate five-day periods of little sleep without fatigue, unusual talkativeness, and excessive commitments noticed by colleagues. Clarify these apparently productive episodes and arrange bipolar assessment before choosing an antidepressant-only plan.

Antidepressant monotherapy is not an appropriate treatment for bipolar I depression. Bipolar depression, mania, and maintenance have different medication strategies, and bipolar II should not be treated as interchangeable with bipolar I. Coordinate specialist care rather than improvising from a list of mood stabilizers. Lithium requires serum-level and renal/thyroid monitoring and attention to dehydration and interacting medicines; lamotrigine requires careful titration and rash precautions and is not an acute antimanic agent. Antipsychotics need metabolic and movement-adverse-effect monitoring. Valproate has substantial reproductive and other safety risks, not a routine primary-care default. [CANMAT/ISBD bipolar treatment safeguards](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11058959/)

Before lithium, obtain weight/BMI, electrolytes including calcium, eGFR, thyroid function, CBC, and an ECG when cardiovascular risk warrants. NICE checks a lithium level one week after starting and after each dose change, weekly until stable, then every three months during the first year; thereafter every six months, or every three months with higher risk. Check renal/thyroid function and calcium at least every six months. Vomiting/diarrhea, worsening coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, or confusion suggests toxicity: withhold lithium and arrange urgent assessment, including electrolytes, renal function, and a properly timed/interpreted level. Dehydration, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors/ARBs, and diuretics can increase risk; a previously satisfactory level is not reassurance during a new illness. [NICE lithium monitoring](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg185/chapter/recommendations), [Lithium toxicity label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1b65d27e-5086-4a87-8387-c49f3d060978)

### Choose depression treatment with the patient

Discuss psychotherapy, medication, and supportive care by benefit, burden, access, and preference. Uncomplicated depression may respond to evidence-based psychotherapy or medication; consider combined care for severe, persistent, or recurrent illness. Severity includes function, chronicity, psychosis, safety, and medical context. Behavioral activation, CBT, and interpersonal therapy are structured treatments. [VA/DoD adult treatment recommendations](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/mdd/VADODMDDCPG_ProviderSummary_Final_508_updated.pdf)

Set attainable activity, sleep, exercise, and support goals alongside clinical treatment. Address food/housing insecurity, caregiving, violence, work, and substances. Confirm therapy access and an interim plan for waiting lists; guided/remote care requires suitability and clinical oversight.

| Medication option | Potential clinical fit | Important limitation or adverse effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SSRIs | Common initial option for depression and selected anxiety-related disorders | GI symptoms, sexual effects, activation, bleeding/hyponatremia risks; indications differ by drug and age |
| SNRIs | Selected depression/anxiety presentations, sometimes with a relevant pain condition | Blood pressure, withdrawal, interactions, and product-specific renal/hepatic restrictions |
| Bupropion | Selected patients concerned about sexual adverse effects or low energy | Seizure and eating-disorder contraindications; activation and blood-pressure concerns |
| Mirtazapine | Selected depression with poor appetite or troublesome sleep | Sedation, increased appetite/weight, dizziness; not automatically suitable with obesity or falls |
| TCAs | Selected circumstances with careful risk review | Anticholinergic effects, orthostasis, conduction effects, and substantial overdose toxicity |
| MAO inhibitors | Specialist-directed treatment in selected patients | Major interactions and drug-specific washouts/dietary requirements; not an uncomplicated switch |

Choose medication by prior response, informative family experience, comorbidity, interactions, overdose risk, reproductive plans, tolerability, cost, and adherence. Dual reuptake does not inherently make an SNRI stronger. Bupropion is contraindicated with seizure disorder, current/prior anorexia or bulimia, and certain abrupt alcohol/sedative withdrawal states. Mirtazapine's appetite/sleep effects may help or harm. [Bupropion label](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=86530199-999b-9f92-b1bc-b8515ec829f3&type=display), [mirtazapine label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=2a0689ae-5650-42d1-ac9d-56dd2b467727)

**Clinical Case.** A 34-year-old requests an antidepressant with fewer sexual effects. Prior bulimia remains a bupropion contraindication despite remission. Explain the risk respectfully and discuss alternatives.

As an adult prescribing example, labeled sertraline initiation differs by indication: **50 mg daily for MDD**, versus **25 mg daily initially for panic disorder, PTSD, or social anxiety**, usually increasing to 50 mg after one week. Subsequent increases are spaced at least a week apart, with a maximum of 200 mg/day for these indications. In mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh 5-6), use half the usual starting dose and therapeutic range; use with Child-Pugh 7-15 is not recommended. This is not a universal pediatric or all-SSRI schedule. [Sertraline indication-specific label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=ab962a5a-fff6-2cf9-e053-2995a90ace0f)

### Monitor benefit, adverse effects, and emerging risk

Explain the expected lag between starting treatment and substantial benefit, possible early GI symptoms or activation, and what warrants an urgent call. Arrange follow-up before the patient leaves. NICE's adult prescribing framework generally reviews within two weeks, with review at **one week for ages 18-25 or particular suicide-risk concerns**; individual circumstances can require earlier/more intensive contact. Reassess after dose changes, not only after the first prescription. [NICE antidepressant review timing](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222/chapter/Recommendations)

The boxed warning describes increased suicidal thoughts/behaviors in children, adolescents, and young adults in short-term trials; monitor rather than categorically withholding treatment. Ask about agitation, akathisia, insomnia, impulsivity, suicidality, and mania/hypomania. Increased energy may not mean recovery. Review missed doses, sexual/GI effects, cost, and dependence concerns.

Check interactions before adding another drug or supplement. Serotonin toxicity may involve agitation, autonomic change, and neuromuscular findings such as clonus/hyperreflexia; fever and severe symptoms require emergency assessment. MAO inhibitors, linezolid, and other interacting exposures need specific review. Fluoxetine's long persistence matters: its label requires at least **five weeks after stopping fluoxetine before a psychiatric MAOI**, and at least 14 days after a psychiatric MAOI before fluoxetine. Do not generalize one cross-taper plan to every pair. [Fluoxetine interaction and switching label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6fff3732-efc3-4cbd-a8fa-cde3181f03e6)

Older adults have greater falls, hyponatremia, bleeding-interaction, and anticholinergic risks; review agent-specific organ restrictions. In pregnancy/lactation, weigh untreated illness, drug risks, and prior response without abrupt cessation or reflex switching. Assess bipolar illness before perinatal pharmacotherapy. Postpartum psychosis requires emergency care; detailed perinatal management follows in 12B. [ACOG perinatal assessment safeguards](https://www.acog.org/programs/perinatal-mental-health/patient-screening)

Youth treatment requires age-/indication-specific evidence, family support, and safeguarding. Fluoxetine has pediatric MDD approval/evidence from age eight and escitalopram from age 12; pediatric sertraline OCD approval does not establish an MDD indication. Other off-label use needs an evidence-based rationale and monitoring; indication, dose, and boxed warning remain distinct. [Fluoxetine pediatric scope](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6fff3732-efc3-4cbd-a8fa-cde3181f03e6), [escitalopram MDD label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=f4a12048-181e-45ed-a382-f53096d1b689)

### Reassess nonresponse before declaring treatment resistance

Track symptoms and function. Before declaring resistance, confirm diagnosis/polarity, actual adherence, therapeutic dose, duration, tolerability, medical/substance contributors, and therapy access. Distinguish partial response, no benefit, and deterioration. Inadequate exposure is not a clean failed trial; emerging mania requires diagnostic reassessment.

The VA/DoD adult MDD algorithm reassesses partial or absent response after at least **four to six weeks of maximized pharmacotherapy**. This is a decision point, not a requirement to tolerate dangerous activation or a guarantee that every patient has reached maximal benefit. Confirm actual exposure before optimizing, switching, or adding psychotherapy or specialist-guided augmentation. [VA/DoD adequate-trial algorithm](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/mdd/VADODMDDCPG_ProviderSummary_Final_508_updated.pdf)

| Follow-up finding | Useful reassessment | Possible next direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early adverse effects with little time at a therapeutic dose | Severity, safety, adherence, and patient's willingness | Adjust the plan and contact interval rather than label failure immediately |
| Partial benefit with residual impairment | Dose/duration, continuing stressors, coexisting illness | Optimize treatment or add appropriate psychotherapy |
| Adequate trial without meaningful benefit | Diagnosis/polarity and actual exposure | Shared decision about switching or specialist-guided augmentation |
| Psychosis, catatonia, inability to maintain intake, or major safety concern | Urgent medical/psychiatric needs | Escalate now rather than requiring completion of a routine trial |

After remission, continue the effective antidepressant at the therapeutic dose for **at least six months**, with longer maintenance considered for recurrent illness, residual symptoms, severe prior episodes, or other relapse risks. Revisit the decision periodically. When stopping, use an individualized gradual reduction; discontinuation symptoms are not automatically recurrent depression. Shorter half-life drugs can produce troublesome withdrawal, while fluoxetine's long half-life affects timing and interactions. [VA/DoD continuation recommendations](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/MH/mdd/VADoDMDDCPGFinal508.pdf)

ECT can be appropriate early when severe depression includes psychosis, catatonia, severe suicidality, or need for a rapid definitive response. It is not reserved for failure of every drug. Specialist options for inadequate response also include selected augmentation and neuromodulation. Intranasal esketamine has monitored-setting and safety requirements; adult treatment-resistant-depression labeling permits monotherapy or combination with an oral antidepressant, whereas its acute-suicidal-symptom indication has different conditions. Its ability to prevent suicide has not been established, and symptom improvement does not remove a hospitalization indication. [VA/DoD escalation framework](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/MH/mdd/VADODMDDCPG_ProviderSummary_Final_508_updated.pdf), [Current esketamine labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d81a6a79-a74a-44b7-822c-0dfa3036eaed)

Esketamine is administered in a REMS-certified setting with direct observation and at least **two hours of monitoring**, including respiratory status/pulse oximetry and blood pressure; discharge also requires clinical stability. Arrange transportation and no driving until the next day after restful sleep. Do not confuse this with zuranolone's different driving restriction in 12B or prescribe it as an unsupervised rescue spray. [Esketamine administration safeguards](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d81a6a79-a74a-44b7-822c-0dfa3036eaed)

### Identify which anxiety-related problem is being treated

Identify triggers, expected versus unexpected attacks, avoidance, and relief behaviors. Assess caffeine/stimulants, alcohol/sedative withdrawal, thyroid disease, arrhythmia, respiratory disease, hypoglycemia, and other mimics. Chest pain, syncope, severe dyspnea, or new neurologic findings warrant medical assessment despite an anxiety diagnosis.

GAD involves excessive, difficult-to-control worry on most days for at least six months. Adults also have at least three associated symptoms: restlessness, fatigue, concentration difficulty, irritability, muscle tension, or disturbed sleep. Assess whether worry is pervasive across domains rather than confined to panic, social evaluation, obsessions, or trauma reminders. [NIMH GAD diagnostic scope](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/generalized-anxiety-disorder-gad)

A panic attack is an abrupt fear/discomfort surge peaking within minutes, not necessarily panic disorder. Explain how sensations, catastrophic interpretation, arousal, and avoidance reinforce each other. Treatment addresses these responses and restores activity; repeated normal tests alone may leave the cycle intact. [NIMH panic-disorder assessment](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms)

| Disorder/presentation | Central pattern | Treatment implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generalized anxiety disorder | Difficult-to-control worry across domains, generally at least six months, with associated symptoms and impairment | CBT/applied relaxation or appropriate medication according to severity and preference |
| Panic disorder | Recurrent unexpected panic attacks with at least a month of anticipatory concern or maladaptive behavior | Panic-focused CBT and/or an appropriate SSRI/SNRI, not repeated reassurance alone |
| Social anxiety disorder | Persistent fear of scrutiny/negative evaluation with avoidance, typically at least six months | Disorder-specific individual CBT; selected medication when preferred/indicated |
| OCD | Intrusive obsessions and/or compulsions that are distressing, time-consuming, or impairing | Exposure and response prevention, selected SSRI treatment, and sometimes combined care |

For GAD or panic disorder, offer disorder-focused psychotherapy and/or appropriate medication, with shared decisions based on impairment, preference, response, and access. Start cautiously when early activation is a concern and explain that maintenance drugs are not immediate rescue treatment. In the NICE framework, benzodiazepines are not routine long-term GAD treatment and are not recommended for panic disorder; crisis use in selected circumstances is different from ongoing treatment. Account for dependence, falls, cognitive effects, alcohol/opioid interactions, and withdrawal risk. Do not abruptly stop an established dependent regimen. [NICE GAD and panic treatment](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg113/chapter/Recommendations)

Social anxiety substantially constrains life through fear and avoidance. Disorder-specific individual CBT addresses feared evaluation, attention, safety behaviors, and avoidance. If medication is selected, discuss duration, early effects, and follow-up; situational beta blockade does not adequately treat generalized social avoidance. [NICE social-anxiety care](https://www.nice.org.uk/Guidance/CG159/chapter/recommendations)

### OCD requires attention to rituals and the meaning of intrusive thoughts

OCD symptoms occupy substantial time, often over an hour daily, or cause clinically significant distress/impairment. Ask about intrusive thoughts/images/urges and both visible and mental rituals: checking, washing, counting, repeating, reassurance-seeking, or reviewing memories. Compulsions temporarily reduce distress but can reinforce the cycle. Distinguish OCD from ordinary habits, obsessive-compulsive personality traits, delusions, and another cause. An unwanted intrusive thought about harm is not the same as a wish or plan to cause harm, although actual intent, psychosis, and independent self-harm risk still need assessment.

**Clinical Case.** A 28-year-old spends hours mentally reviewing possible harm and seeking reassurance. The thoughts are unwanted and frightening. Assess safety and alternatives without equating content with intent; treatment targets the obsession-ritual cycle, including family reassurance.

Exposure and response prevention helps the person face relevant triggers while reducing rituals, in a structured and appropriately supported way. A selected SSRI may be used alone or with CBT/ERP depending on impairment and response. An adequate OCD trial can take longer than an initial depression assessment, often up to 12 weeks; do not declare failure after a few days. Severe impairment, diagnostic uncertainty, poor response, or complex comorbidity merits specialist care. Clomipramine or augmentation requires added safety review rather than casual primary-care escalation. [NICE OCD/ERP framework](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg31/chapter/Recommendations)

### Recognize trauma-related illness without requiring a detailed retelling

PTSD follows qualifying exposure to actual/threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence and involves intrusion, avoidance, negative cognitive/mood changes, and heightened arousal/reactivity with impairment for more than a month. Acute stress disorder involves symptoms lasting from three days to one month after trauma. Not every stressful experience is a qualifying trauma, and not everyone exposed develops PTSD. Anxiety disorders, OCD, and PTSD are distinct diagnostic groups, even when discussed together for primary-care treatment planning.

Use permission-based trauma questions without requiring graphic retelling. Assess current danger, violence, dissociation, sleep, substances, pain, depression, and safety. Avoid compulsory single-session emotional debriefing for universal prevention. Adapt care to culture, communication, disability, and access. [VA National Center for PTSD treatment framework](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/txessentials/cpg_ptsd_management.asp)

The VA/DoD PTSD framework preferentially recommends individual trauma-focused psychotherapy, including cognitive processing therapy, prolonged exposure, or EMDR. These are structured treatments delivered by trained clinicians, not forced exposure during an ordinary visit. When the patient prefers medication or therapy is inaccessible/not feasible, selected options with evidence include sertraline, paroxetine, and venlafaxine. Do not assume every antidepressant has equivalent PTSD evidence. [VA trauma-focused psychotherapy overview](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/txessentials/overview_therapy.asp)

Prazosin may be considered for PTSD-associated nightmares in selected patients, with blood-pressure/orthostasis review, but it is not recommended as a general treatment for all PTSD symptoms in the VA/DoD framework. Benzodiazepines are recommended against for PTSD. Coexisting substance use should be treated in coordination rather than imposing an automatic requirement to achieve complete abstinence before any trauma-focused care. [VA PTSD medication guidance](https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/treat/txessentials/clinician_guide_meds.asp)

**Clinical Case.** Months after serious workplace injury, a 48-year-old has nightmares, site avoidance, and hypervigilance to machinery sounds but requests only a hypnotic. Assess safety, pain, substances, and PTSD; offer tolerable evidence-based care without forced retelling. Nightmare treatment does not replace broader care.

### Make follow-up a shared system of care

Link primary care, therapy/care management and psychiatric consultation through measurement-based follow-up. Use a warm handoff and address contact, privacy, cost, transport, language and remote-care barriers. Treat coexisting sleep, pain, substance and medical problems together rather than requiring one to resolve first.

Review safety, daily function, symptoms, actual medicines, adverse effects and diagnostic fit. Give urgent precautions for suicidal intent, inability to remain safe, new mania/psychosis, severe activation, serotonin toxicity or loss of basic self-care. Assign ownership of results, consultations, follow-up and missed visits; confirm the patient can obtain the plan.
