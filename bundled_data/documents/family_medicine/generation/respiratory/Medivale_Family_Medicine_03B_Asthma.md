# Medivale Family Medicine

## 03B. Asthma

GINA 2026 is the main asthma framework here. US NAEPP recommendations and product labeling are distinguished when age eligibility or the intended reliever use differs.

### Connect variable symptoms to variable expiratory airflow

Asthma is a heterogeneous disease involving airway inflammation, hyperresponsiveness, and variable airflow limitation. Smooth-muscle contraction, mucosal swelling, and mucus impede expiration, causing wheeze, cough, tightness, and breathlessness. Remodeling can leave less reversible limitation. Bronchodilators rapidly relax muscle; anti-inflammatory treatment addresses the underlying inflammation.

Symptoms vary with infection, exercise, allergens, cold air, smoke, or work. Ask about nocturnal/early-morning symptoms, symptom-free intervals, childhood disease, atopy, and family history. Adult-onset disease and cough-predominant presentations occur. Reduced activity can conceal limitation, and examination may be normal between episodes. [NHLBI causes and triggers](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/causes)

Seek objective evidence when feasible, preferably before long-term treatment obscures variability. Spirometry with bronchodilator testing is a usual first test. With normal initial testing but a compatible history, repeat testing during symptoms, serial peak flows, or a supervised challenge may help. Methacholine testing assesses hyperresponsiveness, not a unique marker of asthma, and should be ordered in a setting that can screen contraindications and manage induced bronchoconstriction. Do not delay necessary treatment in a clinically urgent presentation merely to preserve an untreated test. [NHLBI diagnostic testing](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/diagnosis)

For the named GINA adult diagnostic pathway, a bronchodilator-related increase in FEV1 or FVC of at least 12% and at least 200 mL from baseline supports variability. Children aged 6-17 use an age-specific criterion, including FEV1 improvement of at least 12% of predicted. A negative test between episodes does not exclude asthma. Record the actual values and testing circumstances rather than only writing reversible obstruction. [GINA 2026 diagnostic criteria](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

FeNO, blood eosinophils, and allergy testing can help characterize inflammation or exposures. They neither prove every asthma diagnosis nor exclude it when low. Rhinitis can raise FeNO; corticosteroids can suppress biomarkers. Nonasthmatic eosinophilic bronchitis, COPD, bronchiectasis, HF, foreign body, central-airway obstruction, and inducible laryngeal obstruction can mimic parts of the presentation. Inspiratory throat noise and symptoms peaking during exercise suggest a different mechanism from typical postexercise bronchoconstriction, although disorders can coexist.

### Assess today's control and tomorrow's risk separately

Assess daytime symptoms, night waking, reliever use, activity restriction, and school/work effects. Establish each inhaler's actual use. Frequent rescue use or avoidance of exertion can conceal poor control; few current symptoms do not exclude severe-attack risk. Record previous intubation or intensive care.

Review attacks, emergency visits, systemic steroid courses, lung function, adherence, technique, smoke/vaping, and comorbidities. SABA reliance without adequate anti-inflammatory treatment increases concern. Address stress, housing, cost, and follow-up barriers as clinical risks.

For symptom control, review the previous 4 weeks: daytime symptoms more than twice weekly, any asthma-related night waking, activity limitation, and, for a SABA-based plan, symptom-relief use more than twice weekly. The SABA frequency question does not apply unchanged to an ICS-formoterol reliever. Zero positive features indicates well-controlled symptoms; one or two indicate partly controlled symptoms, and three or four indicate uncontrolled symptoms. Risk assessment remains separate. [GINA symptom-control assessment](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

Asthma **severity** describes the treatment required to achieve control after assessment and optimization; **control** describes current symptoms and risk. Severe asthma is not synonymous with a dramatic visit or a high initial prescription. First confirm the diagnosis and address undertreatment, inhaler errors, inconsistent access, exposures, and comorbid disease. Persistently uncontrolled disease despite optimized high-intensity treatment warrants specialist phenotyping. [GINA 2026 assessment framework](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/GINA-2026-Strategy-Report-WMS.pdf)

**Clinical Case.** A college student has few symptoms during examinations but needed emergency treatment twice in the previous year. The normal office examination does not erase this risk. Establish reliable anti-inflammatory treatment and identify triggers, including viral illness, medication shortages, or exposures.

### Choose a coherent anti-inflammatory and reliever strategy

For adults and adolescents, GINA's preferred approach uses low-dose ICS-formoterol as needed at the lower treatment steps, and daily maintenance plus the same reliever at higher steps. An alternative uses maintenance ICS-containing treatment with an appropriate separate reliever. All plans must address inflammation; SABA-only treatment is not the GINA pathway. The choice depends on symptoms, attacks, age, technique, availability, and patient preferences. [GINA treatment pathways](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

**AIR** means an anti-inflammatory reliever. **MART**, also called SMART, means maintenance-and-reliever therapy using a suitable ICS-formoterol inhaler for both roles. Formoterol's rapid onset makes that strategy possible; it does not follow that every ICS-LABA combination can be used as rescue. Do not give an ICS-formoterol rescue plan on top of a different LABA-containing maintenance regimen without an explicit compatible treatment plan.

For example, with budesonide-formoterol **160/4.5 mcg delivered per inhalation** in patients at least 12 years old, GINA uses one inhalation when needed at Steps 1-2. A Step 3 MART regimen is one inhalation twice daily plus one when needed; Step 4 uses two inhalations twice daily plus one when needed. Count maintenance and relief together: the usual ceiling for this regimen is 12 inhalations in 24 hours, not 12 extra rescue doses. Need beyond that ceiling requires medical assessment; rapid worsening warrants care before any maximum is reached. Children 6-11 use 80/4.5 mcg delivered per inhalation and a maximum of 8 total inhalations for the specified pediatric regimen. These limits must not be transferred to another strength, product, or preschool child. [GINA AIR/MART dose reference](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

| Treatment component | Practical distinction |
| --- | --- |
| ICS | Suppresses airway inflammation and reduces exacerbation risk; it is not an immediate bronchodilator by itself. |
| ICS-formoterol AIR/MART | A supported product/regimen can combine rapid relief with anti-inflammatory dosing. |
| ICS-SABA reliever | Combines a rapid short-acting bronchodilator with ICS; it is not a formoterol maintenance-and-reliever regimen. |
| Other ICS-LABA maintenance inhaler | Provides scheduled control; do not assume it can replace the designated rescue treatment. |

US NAEPP supports ICS-formoterol SMART from age 4 for appropriate moderate-to-severe persistent asthma. US Symbicort labeling covers maintenance from age 6, not acute relief; the SMART rescue role may therefore be off-label. Confirm age/formulation, explain use, and ensure supply/payer coverage for both roles. [NAEPP focused update](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/sites/default/files/publications/Asthma%20Clinicians%20At-a-Glance%20508_02-03-21.pdf) [Current US product label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a645756c-c692-4bc6-a858-7e7e249561bd)

Albuterol-budesonide is a US-labeled as-needed ICS-SABA option for adults aged at least 18, not a substitute for every maintenance regimen. Each actuation delivers 90 mcg albuterol and 80 mcg budesonide; two actuations make one dose. The maximum is six doses (12 actuations) in 24 hours. Continue indicated maintenance treatment. Device strength, age eligibility, instructions, and daily maximums are product-specific. An adult label does not become a pediatric approval because an international strategy discusses a class option in children. [Albuterol-budesonide labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=e60aa9f9-5139-44df-b886-ebddc3c7eb13)

Choose starting intensity from symptoms, lung function, and attacks: symptoms on most days or weekly night waking generally need maintenance treatment, not AIR alone. Before stepping up, confirm diagnosis, technique, adherence, and exposures. Review 1-3 months after starting treatment. After 2-3 months of good control, consider reducing maintenance ICS by 25-50%, with an action plan and follow-up; reassess stability before another reduction. Do not completely stop ICS in adults/adolescents, or step down during pregnancy or another high-risk period. [GINA treatment adjustment](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

### Understand drug benefits, adverse effects, and add-ons

Low-dose ICS is generally safer than uncontrolled attacks and repeated systemic steroid bursts. Technique, a compatible spacer, and rinsing/spitting reduce candidiasis and dysphonia. Higher cumulative exposure raises adrenal, bone, and childhood-growth concerns; monitor without unnecessarily withdrawing protective treatment.

LABA monotherapy is unsafe in asthma. This warning should not be misrepresented as a reason to avoid indicated ICS-LABA combination treatment: the FDA's reviewed safety trials did not show a significant increase in serious asthma outcomes when LABAs were combined with ICS. The anti-inflammatory component must actually be taken. Never add multiple LABA-containing products inadvertently. [FDA combination-treatment safety review](https://www.fda.gov/media/109953/download)

Montelukast can help selected patients, but it is not an interchangeable replacement for an effective ICS-containing plan. Discuss the boxed warning for serious neuropsychiatric effects, including mood/behavior changes and suicidal thoughts or actions. New mental-health symptoms require stopping montelukast and contacting a clinician immediately; symptoms may also persist or appear after discontinuation. A tablet's convenience does not remove the need to compare efficacy and harms. Reserve its allergic-rhinitis use for inadequate response or intolerance to alternatives. [FDA montelukast warning](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/fda-requires-boxed-warning-about-serious-mental-health-side-effects-asthma-and-allergy-drug)

Add-on LAMA or phenotype-directed biologics may benefit selected patients according to allergy, type-2 inflammation, attacks, steroid dependence, age, and product criteria. Biologics are not rescue drugs. Confirm diagnosis, exposure control, and adherence before escalation; maintenance systemic steroids are a specialist-supervised last resort. [NHLBI treatment options](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/treatment-action-plan)

### Make inhaler delivery and self-management part of treatment

Ask the patient to demonstrate each device. A pMDI generally needs appropriate preparation, a slow coordinated inhalation, and a breath hold when possible; a compatible spacer reduces coordination demands. A dry-powder device instead requires an adequate forceful inhalation and should not be used with a spacer. Do not exhale into it or assume it performs normally during severe airflow limitation. Device-specific instructions determine priming, shaking, cleaning, and dose counting.

During acute use of a pMDI with spacer, deliver one actuation at a time rather than spraying several doses into the chamber together. A very breathless patient may need an appropriate tidal-breathing technique. Nebulization is not inherently superior to a correctly used pMDI/spacer for every attack; severity, ability to use the device, and the monitored setting matter. Check the remaining dose count and replacement access, not merely the presence of an inhaler in a bag.

Use a written action plan that matches the actual regimen. It should identify routine treatment, how to recognize worsening, the exact reliever and maximum use, when to contact the team, and when to seek emergency help. A form written for SABA rescue cannot be applied unchanged to MART. If peak flows are used, establish the patient's personal best during good control, teach technique, and explain the individualized action thresholds. Symptoms still matter when a peak-flow number appears reassuring.

Address cost, refills, literacy, language, school/work access, caregiver skills, and transport. Use teach-back for maintenance versus rescue and the response to night symptoms. After treatment changes, reassess attacks, function, lung function when appropriate, adverse effects, and satisfaction; review sooner at high risk. [NHLBI self-management guidance](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/living-with)

### Recognize an exacerbation and judge the response repeatedly

An exacerbation is a clinically important worsening from the patient's usual state. Assess ability to speak, respiratory effort, air entry, mentation, pulse, saturation, and lung function if feasible without delaying care. A quiet chest in a distressed patient can mean too little airflow to generate wheeze. Exhaustion, confusion, cyanosis, or inability to speak/drink are emergencies. Prior ICU care or rapid-onset attacks should lower the threshold for escalation. [NHLBI attack recognition](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/asthma/attacks)

| Presentation | Primary-care priority |
| --- | --- |
| Mild symptoms with preserved speech and air entry | Give appropriate rapid inhaled treatment and reassess the response; do not prescribe repeated doses without review. |
| More marked distress or incomplete response | Add indicated acute treatment and arrange monitored assessment/escalation according to severity. |
| Severe distress, poor air entry, low saturation, or altered mentation | Start emergency treatment and transfer urgently; a reassuring isolated measurement must not delay escalation. |

Use inhaled bronchodilator promptly while assessing severity. For a moderate presentation in patients at least 6 years old, GINA 2026 uses albuterol 4-6 puffs by pMDI/spacer, repeated if needed up to three times 20-30 minutes apart; severe attacks need emergency transfer while initial treatment is given. These puff counts refer to a 100-mcg metered dose (commonly labeled 90 mcg delivered in the US). Give one puff into the spacer at a time and reassess, not an unsupervised schedule for continued deterioration. Add ipratropium and early systemic corticosteroid for moderate/severe attacks or inadequate response. Selected mild presentations can use the specified ICS-formoterol alternative. Supplemental oxygen is generally indicated below 92%, targeting 92-95% when used for adults/adolescents and children 6-11; altitude and measurement limitations matter. Pregnancy requires pregnancy-specific assessment rather than applying this range without review. Anaphylaxis requires epinephrine first. [GINA acute primary-care pathway](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

An adult oral prednisolone course is commonly 40-50 mg each morning for 5-7 days when indicated. For children 6-11, GINA uses 1-2 mg/kg/day, maximum 40 mg/day, for 3-5 days; preschool children need their own pathway. A short course under 2 weeks generally needs no taper in a patient not otherwise steroid dependent. Repeated bursts or chronic exposure require review of cumulative harm and adrenal suppression, not automatic repetition of that stopping rule. Bronchodilators act rapidly, whereas steroid benefit is not immediate. Failure to improve needs escalation, not simply waiting for the steroid to work. [Current acute steroid regimen](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

A normal or rising PaCO2 in a tiring patient can signal impending ventilatory failure because early attacks usually provoke hyperventilation. Blood gases, radiography, and additional tests are selected for severity, complications, or competing diagnoses; they are not routine requirements for every mild attack. Consider pneumothorax, pneumonia, PE, HF, and foreign body when the response is atypical. Do not give sedatives to calm respiratory distress or routine antibiotics without an infectious indication.

Reassess after initial treatment and repeatedly thereafter. Persistent tachypnea can reflect continued obstruction, another diagnosis, or beta-agonist toxicity including lactate accumulation; consider the entire examination and trajectory before reflexively increasing bronchodilator. Disposition depends on sustained improvement, oxygenation, airflow, recent history, and whether the person can manage safely at home. A brief improvement after one treatment does not erase high-risk findings.

Before discharge, establish an ICS-containing prevention plan, review technique and supplies, provide return precautions, and arrange follow-up within 2-7 days for adults/adolescents and 2-5 days for children, earlier if needed. Review the episode's cause and whether the action plan actually worked. A discharge prescription without access to the medicine is an incomplete safety plan. Persistent or recurrent attacks warrant specialist review rather than serial rescue-only encounters. [GINA postattack follow-up](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

### Adapt the plan to age, pregnancy, exercise, and work

Children aged 6-11 need ICS-containing treatment, selected from an age-specific pathway. Current GINA options for infrequent symptoms include an anti-inflammatory reliever; more frequent symptoms can require daily ICS-based treatment. Product approvals and usable devices differ from adult choices. [GINA age-specific treatment](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

For preschool children, recurrent viral wheeze, interval symptoms, alternative diagnoses, growth, and caregiver observations require a separate assessment; do not label every infant wheeze as asthma. NAEPP has a specific short-course ICS strategy for selected recurrent infection-triggered preschool wheeze, not all children with a first cold. [NAEPP age-specific scope](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health-topics/asthma-management-guidelines-2020-updates/faqs)

During pregnancy, adequate control protects both patient and fetus. Continue indicated ICS-containing treatment and review every 4-6 weeks; do not stop treatment or perform bronchial challenge testing to confirm a diagnosis during pregnancy. Treat exacerbations promptly; maternal hypoxemia is not a benign alternative to appropriate medication. Review technique, adherence, rhinitis, and obstetric considerations. Do not assume every new breathless episode in pregnancy is asthma; PE and other serious causes remain possible. [GINA pregnancy guidance](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/GINA-Summary-Guide-2026-WEB-WMS.pdf)

Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction involves airway narrowing associated with increased ventilation, especially in cold/dry air. Diagnosis rests on objective airflow change after a suitable challenge when needed, not symptoms alone. Warm-up, an appropriate pre-exercise reliever plan, and improved background control can preserve activity. Frequent need for pre-exercise rescue or symptoms despite it prompts reassessment; avoiding all exercise is not the goal. Inspiratory throat tightness at peak exertion may require assessment for laryngeal obstruction. [ATS exercise-related guidance](https://www.thoracic.org/statements/resources/allergy-asthma/exercise-induced-bronchoconstriction.pdf)

For work-related asthma, identify tasks, agents, timing, and improvement away from exposure. Serial peak flows at and away from work can support assessment when safely obtained; never deliberately re-expose a patient to a dangerous agent to prove a diagnosis. Specialist occupational/pulmonary input helps distinguish new sensitizer-induced disease, irritant injury, and work-exacerbated asthma. Exposure elimination is generally the most effective approach, but employment and income consequences require shared planning. [CDC workplace treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/asthma/hcp/treatment/index.html)

### A treatment-access example

**Clinical Case.** A 35-year-old bakery worker develops cough and chest tightness late in the shift, improving during a week away. Technique is adequate, but cost leads her to ration maintenance treatment. Confirm airflow variability, investigate occupational exposure, restore supply, and establish a clear reliever plan.

At follow-up, review attacks, refills, work modification, and lung-function trends. Improvement away from exposure supports occupational investigation but does not prove that every symptom shares a cause.
