# Medivale Family Medicine

## 15C. Primary Care Hematology

Interpret blood counts by mechanism, time course and urgency. Anemia reflects loss, underproduction or destruction; platelet abnormalities range from artifact to drug reactions and emergencies.

### Decide whether the abnormality can wait

Ask about active bleeding, chest discomfort, breathlessness at rest, syncope, fever, confusion and rapidly worsening weakness. Assess circulation, mental status and trajectory before outpatient testing. Acute hemorrhage can initially preserve hemoglobin concentration through concurrent plasma loss. A rapid decline matters even within the reference interval.

Review previous counts, recent hospitalization, transfusion, chemotherapy, infection, pregnancy and all medicines. Include antithrombotics, NSAIDs, supplements and nonprescription products. Determine whether one cell line or several are affected. Confirm unexpected results without delaying emergency care for instability. [NHLBI anemia diagnosis](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/anemia/diagnosis)

| Presentation | Immediate concern | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bleeding with hypotension, syncope or ischemic symptoms | Hemorrhage with threatened perfusion | Emergency stabilization and source control |
| Anemia, thrombocytopenia and fragmented red cells | Thrombotic microangiopathy | Urgent hospital and hematology assessment |
| Fever or systemic illness after anticancer treatment | Neutropenic sepsis | Immediate emergency assessment and empiric treatment |
| New blasts, multiple falling cell lines or concerning systemic symptoms | Marrow failure, leukemia or another serious process | Expedite specialist assessment; urgency follows clinical condition |

### Read the whole count, not hemoglobin alone

Anemia is hemoglobin below the age-, pregnancy- and laboratory-appropriate threshold. Adult thresholds do not apply to children; mild anemia in older adults still needs investigation, without race-based assumptions. Dehydration concentrates and fluid expansion dilutes counts without equivalent changes in red-cell mass. [WHO hemoglobin framework](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088542)

WHO 2024 defines anemia at ages 15-65 as hemoglobin below **12 g/dL in nonpregnant women** or **13 g/dL in men**. Its pregnancy thresholds are 11, 10.5 and 11 g/dL by trimester. Above 65, use an appropriate reference and clinical assessment; this table supplies no universal older-adult threshold. WHO reports these values in g/L: divide by ten for g/dL. [WHO final cutoff table](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK602185/table/ch5.tab2/)

MCV is the average red-cell volume. In adults, values below 80 fL are usually termed microcytic, 80-100 fL normocytic and above 100 fL macrocytic. These categories guide investigation, not diagnosis. Early iron deficiency or combined iron/B12 deficiency can be normocytic. RDW reveals size variation; transfusion can obscure the pattern.

Assess marrow response with the absolute reticulocyte count and, when relevant, correction for anemia and prolonged maturation. A raw percentage may overstate production when mature red cells are depleted. An inappropriately low response suggests impaired production; a strong response suggests blood loss, hemolysis or recovery after treatment. Allow for delayed marrow compensation and interpret smear morphology in context. [ARUP anemia evaluation](https://arupconsult.com/content/anemia)

| Adult pattern | Mechanisms to consider | Useful next information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low MCV | Iron restriction, thalassemia, selected toxin or heme-synthesis disorders | Ferritin and iron availability, history, red-cell count and smear |
| Normal MCV, inadequate reticulocyte response | Kidney disease, inflammation, early deficiency or marrow disease | Kidney function, clinical context, selected nutrient tests and other cell lines |
| High MCV | B12/folate deficiency, alcohol, liver or thyroid disease, drugs, marrow disorders | Smear, reticulocytes, B12 and targeted history/testing |
| Anemia with increased reticulocyte production | Blood loss, hemolysis or recent recovery | Bleeding assessment, bilirubin, LDH, haptoglobin and morphology |

### Separate iron depletion from iron sequestration

Ferritin reflects stores but rises with inflammation; preserved values during infection, inflammatory disease or CKD do not exclude deficiency. Low serum iron alone cannot distinguish depletion from sequestration. Transferrin saturation measures occupied iron-binding capacity and helps assess availability alongside inflammatory markers and clinical context.

AGA's gastrointestinal evaluation guideline uses ferritin below 45 ng/mL to identify iron deficiency in a patient with anemia, rather than the more restrictive 15 ng/mL threshold. This diagnostic threshold is not a universal CKD, pregnancy or inflammatory-disease treatment rule. Depletion still matters with normal MCV. [AGA gastrointestinal evaluation of iron-deficiency anemia](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/gastrointestinal-evaluation-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/)

Inflammatory signaling increases hepcidin, reducing intestinal iron entry and trapping iron in storage cells. Inflammation can also blunt erythropoietin responsiveness and shorten red-cell survival. Absolute deficiency and inflammatory restriction can coexist. Distinguish absent stores, inaccessible stores and mixed disease.

| Pattern | Typical iron-study relationship | Interpretation caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depleted stores | Ferritin low, saturation low; binding capacity often increased | Seek the cause, not only a replacement dose |
| Inflammatory restriction | Saturation often low, ferritin preserved or increased; binding capacity often reduced | Ferritin is an acute-phase reactant |
| Mixed depletion and inflammation | Low availability with ferritin less clearly reduced | Integrate trends, inflammation, renal disease and treatment response |

**Clinical Case: a normal average does not exclude iron deficiency.** A 55-year-old, three years past menopause, has exercise intolerance, hemoglobin 9.8 g/dL, MCV 84 fL and ferritin 9 ng/mL. Periodic blood donation may contribute, but normal MCV and absent visible bleeding or abdominal pain do not exclude occult loss. Replace iron and investigate gastrointestinal and other sources.

### Find the reason iron was lost or not absorbed

Ask about menstrual volume, intermenstrual bleeding, gastrointestinal symptoms, visible blood, donation, pregnancy, diet and prior gastrointestinal surgery. Review NSAIDs and antithrombotics while investigating the bleeding source. Consider celiac disease and malabsorption when the history or response supports them. Dietary inadequacy does not exclude an underlying lesion.

AGA recommends bidirectional endoscopy for iron-deficiency anemia in men and postmenopausal women; in premenopausal women, its recommendation is conditional and calls for discussion of competing explanations and preferences. When celiac disease is plausible, use appropriate serology with confirmatory evaluation as indicated. After unrevealing bidirectional endoscopy and no other cause, noninvasive H. pylori testing is part of the pathway. Uncomplicated patients with a negative initial evaluation generally receive an iron trial before routine capsule endoscopy. A negative screening stool test does not complete diagnostic assessment of unexplained iron-deficiency anemia.

### Replace iron and verify that treatment worked

Distinguish iron salt from elemental iron: a common ferrous sulfate tablet contains 325 mg salt and 65 mg elemental iron; confirm the formulation. For a suitable adult, **one such tablet orally once daily** is a practical initial regimen; every-other-day dosing can improve tolerability. AGA's 2024 advice favors oral iron no more than once daily, consistent with BSG's one-tablet starting approach. Low hemoglobin alone does not justify multiple daily doses. [AGA iron management advice](https://gastro.org/clinical-guidance/management-of-iron-deficiency-anemia/) [Example ferrous sulfate label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5514d582-19b5-4f1e-b794-f790afb8211c)

Discuss nausea, constipation and dark stools versus bleeding, and review absorption barriers/interactions with pharmacy. Adjust for intolerance. Secure iron from children because overdose can be fatal; pediatric dosing differs.

For follow-up, the BSG adult guideline recommends checking the early hemoglobin response within the first four weeks and continuing treatment for roughly three months after hemoglobin normalizes to replenish stores, individualized to ongoing losses and circumstances. If the response is poor, reconsider actual intake, formulation, continued bleeding, malabsorption, inflammation and the original diagnosis. AGA supports intravenous iron when oral treatment is not tolerated, stores fail to improve or absorption is unlikely. Continue investigation of the cause during replacement. [BSG adult iron-deficiency guidance, monitoring scope](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8515119)

Marked microcytosis with a normal or increased red-cell count and adequate stores can suggest thalassemia. Hemoglobin analysis helps identify many beta-chain disorders, while alpha-thalassemia trait can have unremarkable routine hemoglobin analysis and may require targeted genetic evaluation. MCV and ancestry alone cannot diagnose a trait. Inherited disease may first be recognized in adulthood; avoid indefinite iron without deficiency. [ARUP thalassemia testing](https://arupconsult.com/content/thalassemias)

**Clinical Case: treating the wrong mechanism.** A 34-year-old planning pregnancy has longstanding MCV 64 fL, red-cell count 6.1 million/microliter and ferritin 95 ng/mL without inflammation or bleeding. Evaluate for hemoglobinopathy rather than automatically prescribing iron; confirm the diagnosis before partner testing and reproductive counseling.

### Recognize B12 deficiency even without macrocytosis

B12 and folate deficiency impair DNA synthesis, causing large precursors, ineffective hematopoiesis and sometimes multiple cytopenias. B12 also supports neurologic function. Paresthesias, loss of position sense, gait difficulty or cognitive change can occur without anemia or a high MCV. Ask about diet, gastric or ileal surgery, autoimmune disease, medicines affecting B12 and nitrous oxide exposure. [NIH B12 physiology and test interpretation](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminB12-HealthProfessional/)

NICE's framework applies to people **aged 16 and older**. Obtain diagnostic blood samples before replacement when feasible, but do not delay treatment when suspected B12 deficiency has concerning neurologic manifestations. An indeterminate B12 result may need methylmalonic acid testing; impaired kidney function can elevate MMA independently. Homocysteine is less specific because folate deficiency and other conditions also increase it. Nitrous oxide can cause functional B12 inactivation despite a misleading total B12 result, requiring an exposure-aware testing approach. [NICE B12 recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations)

Choose replacement by cause, severity, absorption and reliability of treatment. NICE recommends lifelong intramuscular replacement for autoimmune gastritis, total gastrectomy or complete terminal ileal resection; other causes require an individualized oral or intramuscular plan. Follow neurologic function as well as blood counts, and investigate a poor response. Folate alone can improve counts while leaving B12 neurologic injury untreated. Use age- and pregnancy-specific guidance.

When an oral pathway is appropriate despite suspected malabsorption, NICE specifies **at least 1 mg B12 orally daily**; that option does not replace its lifelong-IM recommendation for the irreversible causes above. Arrange initial review at about three months, earlier for severe or worsening symptoms, and at one month during pregnancy or breastfeeding. Check symptoms, adherence and the actual preparation; a low-dose multivitamin is not equivalent replacement. [NICE replacement and follow-up recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng239/chapter/Recommendations)

Confirmed folate deficiency also needs replacement and investigation of diet, alcohol, malabsorption and medicines. **Folic acid 1 mg orally daily** is a usual adult therapeutic regimen, but exclude B12 deficiency or treat it adequately at the same time. A better count does not establish neurologic protection. Review response and the ongoing cause before selecting maintenance treatment; this is not a substitute for pregnancy-specific preventive dosing. [Folic-acid prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=607d23e8-21ef-4d73-88f1-990cf267d22e)

**Clinical Case: the count is not the neurologic examination.** A 68-year-old with complete terminal ileal resection develops unsteadiness in dim light and impaired toe-position sense despite MCV 93 fL. Obtain samples, assess other neurologic causes and begin indicated B12 replacement promptly; the surgery determines the long-term route.

### Keep renal and inflammatory anemia diagnoses accountable

CKD reduces erythropoietin production and iron availability but can coexist with bleeding, B12 deficiency or marrow disease. Evaluate reversible causes and ensure symptoms, anemia trajectory, iron status and other cell lines fit the explanation, especially with new anemia in stable CKD.

Under KDIGO 2026, erythropoiesis-stimulating therapy is individualized with attention to symptoms, transfusion avoidance and cardiovascular/thrombotic harms. It is not an automatic response to every hemoglobin below 10 g/dL, and the goal is not normalization. In adults receiving an ESA, the recommended maintenance target is below 11.5 g/dL. Correct reversible contributors and arrange appropriate specialist monitoring; do not transfer general-population ferritin rules into the separate CKD iron-treatment algorithm. [KDIGO 2026 selected clinical takeaways](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/KDIGO-2026-Anemia-Guideline-Key-Takeaways-for-HCPs.pdf)

That guideline ceiling is not a universal US product target. Epoetin alfa labeling calls for reduction/interruption above 10 g/dL in nondialysis CKD and as hemoglobin approaches or exceeds 11 g/dL in dialysis patients, using the lowest dose needed to reduce transfusions. Use the product label and 06C pathway; 11.4 is not automatically safe because it is below 11.5. [US epoetin alfa CKD labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f2d0b28-9cc5-4523-80b8-637fdaf3f7a5)

### Identify destruction and dangerous microangiopathy

Hemolysis releases red-cell contents and increases hemoglobin breakdown. Supporting findings include increased LDH and indirect bilirubin, reduced haptoglobin and an increased reticulocyte response, although marrow suppression or concurrent deficiency may blunt that response. A direct antiglobulin test evaluates immune hemolysis. Interpret spherocytes, fragments and other smear findings clinically to guide further testing. [ARUP hemolysis evaluation](https://arupconsult.com/content/hemolytic-anemias)

Consider drug exposures, infection, mechanical injury and inherited conditions alongside immune causes. G6PD deficiency can produce oxidant-associated hemolysis. Testing during an acute episode may be falsely reassuring because the most deficient older cells have been destroyed, leaving younger cells with greater enzyme activity; recent transfusion also complicates interpretation. If suspicion persists, arrange appropriately timed repeat testing with the laboratory. Known sickle cell disease with fever, chest symptoms or hypoxemia requires urgent assessment, not routine anemia follow-up. [University of Michigan G6PD testing limitations](https://mlabs.umich.edu/tests/g-6-pd-qualitative-screen)

Thrombocytopenia with microangiopathic hemolytic anemia raises concern for TTP and other thrombotic microangiopathies. TTP does not require the complete historical pentad of findings. Draw ADAMTS13 samples before plasma therapy if this can be done without delaying care. High clinical suspicion requires emergency hematology involvement and prompt treatment rather than waiting for the assay. ISTH's 2025 update retains plasma exchange with corticosteroids for immune TTP, with specialist-directed additional therapies such as rituximab and caplacizumab. Congenital TTP is a different treatment pathway. [ISTH 2025 focused TTP update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13470878/)

**Clinical Case: the missing pentad is not reassurance.** A 37-year-old has bruising, headache, fatigue, platelets 22,000/microliter, schistocytes and high LDH despite no fever and only mildly elevated creatinine. Arrange emergency thrombotic-microangiopathy assessment, including TTP, without waiting for the complete pentad or send-out results.

### Recognize inadequate production and clonal disease

An unexplained low reticulocyte response, persistent macrocytosis, more than one cytopenia, abnormal precursors or circulating blasts increases concern for marrow disease. Review medications, alcohol, nutritional deficiency, infection and recent cytotoxic treatment, but do not let a plausible reversible contributor postpone referral when the pattern is concerning. Marrow infiltration, aplasia and myelodysplastic disease cannot be reliably separated by MCV alone. A specialist may need marrow examination and additional studies.

A persistently high hemoglobin or hematocrit also requires explanation. Confirm persistence and consider reduced plasma volume, hypoxic disease, sleep-disordered breathing, smoking-related exposure and medications before assuming a primary marrow disorder. Unexplained erythrocytosis, thrombocytosis or leukocytosis with splenomegaly, unusual thrombosis or systemic symptoms warrants evaluation for a myeloproliferative neoplasm. Select molecular tests through the clinical and specialist pathway. [ARUP myeloproliferative evaluation](https://arupconsult.com/content/myeloproliferative-neoplasms)

### Use absolute white-cell counts and the timeline

Percentages can mislead. Interpret the absolute neutrophil, lymphocyte and other differential counts alongside total WBC, smear and trend. Infection, inflammation, physiologic stress and corticosteroids can cause reactive leukocytosis. Persistent unexplained abnormalities, immature forms, basophilia, a monomorphic lymphocyte population, lymphadenopathy or splenomegaly deserve more investigation. Normal total WBC can conceal differential abnormalities; one elevation does not establish leukemia.

The absolute neutrophil count is WBC multiplied by the fraction of segmented neutrophils plus bands. Infection risk depends on severity, duration, cause, mucosal injury and the patient's condition. Duffy-null associated neutrophil counts can be lower at baseline without increased infection risk in an otherwise well person. Assess the phenotype rather than race, and investigate new decline, fever or chemotherapy-associated neutropenia. [ASH Duffy-null education](https://www.hematology.org/education/danc)

**Clinical Case: calculate the relevant count.** A 59-year-old receiving chemotherapy calls with temperature 38.4 C and shaking chills. WBC is 2,000/microliter, with 15% segmented neutrophils and 5% bands. ANC is 2,000 x 0.20 = 400/microliter. Arrange immediate emergency assessment and empiric treatment for neutropenic sepsis. NICE's cancer-treatment pathway recognizes neutropenia at or below 0.5 x 10^9/L with fever above 38 C or other clinically significant sepsis features. [NICE neutropenic sepsis recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg151/chapter/recommendations)

### Distinguish platelet artifacts, destruction and consumption

For an unexpected low platelet count, ask the laboratory about clumping and confirm with smear review and an appropriate repeat specimen, sometimes using a different anticoagulant. Then examine the trend, bleeding, other cell lines, liver/splenic findings, medicines and systemic illness. Severe bleeding or neurologic symptoms require urgent assessment before definitive diagnosis. Isolated thrombocytopenia does not automatically establish immune thrombocytopenia; TTP, HIT, drugs, infection and other causes must be considered in context.

For newly diagnosed adult ITP with no or only minor mucocutaneous bleeding, ASH's retained observation framework generally favors observation at **platelets at least 30 x 10^9/L**; counts below 30 usually prompt treatment assessment. Age, anticoagulants, comorbidity and procedures can change that decision. A new diagnosis with platelets below **20 x 10^9/L** generally favors admission even with minor bleeding; stable outpatient care requires reliable access and expedited hematology review, generally within 24-72 hours. Significant bleeding or neurological symptoms overrides a routine count-based plan. [ASH observation and disposition framework](https://www.hematology.org/-/media/hematology/files/education/clinicians/guidelines-quality/documents/watermarked-pocket-guides/watermark-ash-itp-pocket-guide.pdf)

For adults requiring initial treatment, the 2026 ASH update conditionally favors rituximab plus corticosteroids or a thrombopoietic agent plus corticosteroids, with or without IVIG. Selection is specialist-led and depends on risks, preferences and availability; this is not an emergency-bleeding regimen or guidance for children and pregnancy. Avoid unnecessarily prolonged steroid courses. [Current ASH guideline resources](https://www.hematology.org/education/clinicians/guidelines-and-quality-care/clinical-practice-guidelines/immune-thrombocytopenia-guidelines), [2026 recommendation summary](https://www.hematology.org/-/media/hematology/files/clinicians/guidelines/ash-cpg-itp-guidelines-visual-summary-0824.pdf)

HIT is particularly dangerous because thrombosis, not just bleeding, is central. A substantial platelet fall can matter even if the absolute count remains above the laboratory's lower limit. Use the validated 4Ts framework: magnitude of thrombocytopenia, timing, thrombosis and alternative explanations. Intermediate or high probability calls for stopping heparin, including flushes, appropriate nonheparin anticoagulation and the diagnostic pathway with urgent specialist input. A genuinely low-probability assessment generally avoids indiscriminate HIT testing. Anticoagulant intensity and choice depend on bleeding risk, organ function and the clinical setting. [ASH HIT guidance](https://www.hematology.org/education/clinicians/guidelines-and-quality-care/clinical-practice-guidelines/venous-thromboembolism-guidelines/heparin-induced-thrombocytopenia)

Thrombocytosis commonly accompanies iron deficiency, inflammation or recovery from illness, but persistent unexplained elevation can be clonal. Treat the underlying process and assess persistence. Aspirin requires assessment of cause, thrombosis risk and bleeding risk; a high count does not ensure effective hemostasis.

### Investigate the bleeding pattern and the medicines

Ask whether bleeding is lifelong or new, spontaneous or procedural, mucosal or deep, and whether relatives are affected. Include nosebleeds, heavy menses, dental bleeding, postpartum hemorrhage, large hematomas and hemarthroses. Check liver and kidney disease, alcohol, supplements and the actual doses of anticoagulants and antiplatelet medicines. Investigate new bruising rather than assuming fragile skin or anticoagulation explains it.

Mucocutaneous bleeding often points toward platelets or von Willebrand factor; deep muscle or joint bleeding raises concern for coagulation-factor problems. These are useful tendencies, not exclusive categories. Initial testing commonly includes CBC/smear, PT/INR and aPTT, with fibrinogen and other studies according to severity and context. Normal routine tests do not exclude von Willebrand disease or a platelet-function disorder. Specific VWF activity, antigen and factor VIII testing may be needed; stress, inflammation and pregnancy can alter VWF levels. [ARUP von Willebrand disease testing](https://arupconsult.com/content/von-willebrand-disease)

| Initial laboratory pattern | Possibilities to investigate | Important limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low platelets, routine coagulation tests near normal | Platelet destruction, production failure or microangiopathy | Smear, clinical illness and other cell lines distinguish causes |
| PT prolonged more prominently | Vitamin K antagonist effect, vitamin K deficiency or selected factor/liver problems | Interpret with drugs, nutrition and liver assessment |
| Isolated prolonged aPTT | Factor deficiency, inhibitor or anticoagulant effect | Not every prolonged aPTT predicts bleeding; mixing studies need expert interpretation |
| Falling platelets with abnormal coagulation and increased fibrin breakdown | DIC in an appropriate clinical setting | Fibrinogen can remain normal or increased early in inflammation |

DIC reflects systemic coagulation activation with consumption and organ injury, often accompanying severe infection, malignancy or obstetric disease. Both thrombosis and bleeding can occur. Follow serial results and the patient, not one isolated fibrinogen value; an acute-phase rise can initially conceal consumption. Management requires hospital-level treatment of the cause and individualized hemostatic support. Relatively preserved coagulation tests can favor a thrombotic microangiopathy over DIC, but a single test does not safely rule either process in or out. [ARUP DIC evaluation](https://arupconsult.com/content/disseminated-intravascular-coagulation)

### Manage anticoagulant bleeding with the correct reversal pathway

Major or life-threatening bleeding requires emergency stabilization, interruption of the implicated anticoagulant, identification of the last dose, assessment of kidney function and source control. Determine the indication and interacting medicines. Routine PT/INR or aPTT cannot reliably exclude clinically important direct oral anticoagulant exposure. Drug-specific assessment, when available, supports rather than delays an urgent treatment decision. Balance reversal's thrombotic risk and plan reassessment for restarting treatment.

For warfarin-associated major bleeding, four-factor PCC provides rapid factor replacement and intravenous vitamin K supports sustained reversal under the emergency protocol. Kcentra's dose is weight- and pretreatment-INR-based; its label requires concurrent vitamin K and warns about thromboembolism. It contains heparin and is contraindicated in known HIT and DIC. Use the product-specific hospital protocol rather than a fixed dose for every bleeding patient. [Kcentra prescribing information](https://labeling.cslbehring.com/pi/us/kcentra/en/kcentra-prescribing-information.pdf)

Idarucizumab is specific to dabigatran when reversal is needed for life-threatening or uncontrolled bleeding or an emergency procedure. The labeled adult dose is 5 g intravenously, supplied as two 2.5 g vials. It does not reverse factor Xa inhibitors. Vitamin K does not reverse apixaban; for severe factor Xa inhibitor bleeding, current U.S. hospital protocols may use PCC off label after assessing exposure and risks. PCC is not an approved specific apixaban antidote; follow the current institutional pathway and formulary. [Praxbind label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c7400f8a-dcf4-a6df-6d07-983081b1bf34) [Current apixaban label, reversal scope](https://packageinserts.bms.com/pi/pi_eliquis.pdf)

Andexanet alfa is not a routinely available US reversal option: FDA concluded that Andexxa's risks outweighed its benefits and reported the end of US manufacture/sales after December 22, 2025. Confirm the current emergency formulary. [FDA Andexxa safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/update-safety-andexxa)

### Use transfusion thresholds only in their proper population

Transfusion treats an immediate oxygen-carrying deficit; it does not establish why the anemia developed or replace iron and nutrient repletion. Consider symptoms, hemodynamics, ongoing losses, ischemia and the patient's preferences. Explain relevant risks and reassess response. A numeric trigger should never delay stabilization of major hemorrhage or become an automatic outpatient transfusion order.

AABB's 2023 recommendations generally support considering red-cell transfusion below 7 g/dL in hemodynamically stable hospitalized adults, with different thresholds for selected groups, including 8 g/dL in some orthopedic or preexisting cardiovascular disease contexts. Acute myocardial infarction is not interchangeable with stable cardiovascular history: AABB's 2025 AMI guideline conditionally suggests a more liberal strategy when hemoglobin is below 10 g/dL in hospitalized patients with recent AMI. Apply each threshold only within its population and clinical context. [AABB 2023 threshold resource](https://www.aabb.org/docs/default-source/default-document-library/resources/updates-in-red-blood-cell-transfusion-thresholds.pdf) [AABB 2025 AMI recommendation](https://www.aabb.org/news-resources/news/article/2025/08/20/expert-panel-recommends-liberal-transfusion-strategy-for-hospitalized-patients-with-ami)

### Recognize cancer warnings without equating every symptom with cancer

Persistent unexplained lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, fevers, drenching night sweats, weight loss, recurrent infections or unexplained cytopenias require a coherent evaluation. Examine the distribution and character of nodes, medication and exposure history, and associated counts. An obvious infection may explain temporary nodes, but document the expected course and reassessment. Circulating blasts, rapidly worsening counts or systemic illness need expedited assessment rather than a prolonged empirical treatment trial.

Myeloma belongs in the differential of unexplained bone pain or fractures, anemia, renal dysfunction, hypercalcemia or recurrent infections. Evaluation may include serum protein electrophoresis, immunofixation and serum free light chains with appropriate further testing. Distinguish MGUS and other plasma-cell states from symptomatic myeloma using their criteria and follow-up pathways. Investigate organ injury even with modest protein levels; refer by clinical findings, not age alone. [NCI plasma-cell neoplasm diagnosis](https://www.cancer.gov/types/myeloma/patient/myeloma-treatment-pdq)

### Share ownership of survivorship care

Obtain the actual cancer diagnosis, stage, operations, systemic therapies, radiation fields and treatment dates. A survivorship plan should identify who owns surveillance, late-effect monitoring, preventive care and new symptom assessment. Use cancer- and risk-specific surveillance, not universal scans or tumor markers. Continue chronic-disease care and address access barriers. [NCI follow-up care](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/coping/survivorship/follow-up-care)

Past treatment can matter years later. Selected chemotherapy and chest radiation exposures can produce delayed cardiac disease; other treatments can affect nerves, endocrine function, fertility, bone health or the risk of subsequent malignancy. Assess the symptom on its merits while retrieving exposure details. A survivor with fatigue may have anemia, sleep disturbance, depression, cardiopulmonary disease, treatment toxicity, recurrence or an unrelated condition. Tailor late-effect testing; normal CBC does not settle the complaint. [NCI late effects](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/coping/survivorship/late-effects)

**Clinical Case: fatigue after cancer is not always a blood-count problem.** A 62-year-old with prior anthracycline exposure develops exertional breathlessness and edema despite normal hemoglobin. Assess promptly for delayed cardiac injury and other cardiopulmonary causes while retrieving exposure details; past chemotherapy anemia does not justify iron.

Ask about fear of recurrence, sexual health, work, financial strain, caregiver needs and access to rehabilitation. Arrange support without assuming that completion of treatment means recovery of function. Communicate new findings to the oncology team and clarify which symptoms should prompt an urgent call. Clarify responsibilities with the patient and clinicians. [NCI survivorship care standards](https://cancercontrol.cancer.gov/ocs/special-focus-areas/national-standards-cancer-survivorship-care)

### Close the diagnostic and follow-up loop

Assign each abnormal count an owner, working explanation, next step and risk-based review interval. Tell the patient which symptoms should interrupt the schedule: worsening breathlessness, syncope, chest pain, significant bleeding, fever during cancer therapy or new neurologic findings. Confirm completion of referrals and pending tests.

Follow the cause as well as the count: explain iron loss, track neurologic B12 recovery and absorption needs, and address cytopenia mechanisms and treatment harms. Section 15D develops symptom relief and goal-concordant care in advanced illness.
