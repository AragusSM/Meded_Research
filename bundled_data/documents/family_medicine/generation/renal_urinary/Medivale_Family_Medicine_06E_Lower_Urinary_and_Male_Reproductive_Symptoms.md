# Medivale Family Medicine

## 06E. Lower Urinary and Male Reproductive Symptoms

Urinary and sexual symptoms affect sleep, mobility, relationships and work. Ask privately about symptoms, anatomy, sexual activity and goals. Weak stream, leakage and erectile difficulty do not identify their causes or simply represent aging; assess obstruction, infection, neurologic disease, vascular risk and malignancy.

The main LUTS framework is EAU 2026, with AUA/SUFU overactive-bladder principles and indication-specific product precautions. Infection and urine testing are developed in 06A; hematuria, stones, and renal injury in 06B-06D. Screening an asymptomatic person is distinct from investigating a new symptom or mass. [EAU 2026 scope](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/management-of-non-neurogenic-male-luts/chapter/introduction)

### First distinguish impaired storage, emptying, and urine production

Lower urinary tract symptoms, or **LUTS**, are a symptom group, not a synonym for benign prostatic hyperplasia. Storage complaints include urgency, frequency, nocturia, and urgency leakage. Voiding complaints include hesitancy, straining, weak or interrupted stream, and prolonged emptying. Postvoid symptoms include incomplete-emptying sensation and dribbling after the main void. These patterns overlap: obstruction can coexist with detrusor overactivity, and a poorly contracting bladder can cause both retention and leakage.

Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) is proliferation of tissue in the prostate's transition zone. Enlargement can contribute to outlet obstruction through tissue bulk and smooth-muscle tone, but histologic hyperplasia, measured enlargement, functional obstruction, and symptom severity are not interchangeable. Detrusor dysfunction, urethral stricture, medication effects, infection, pelvic-floor dysfunction, and neurologic disease can produce similar complaints. BPH is not a premalignant lesion, although BPH and prostate cancer can coexist. [NIDDK enlarged-prostate overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/prostate-problems/enlarged-prostate-benign-prostatic-hyperplasia)

Ask about progression, pain, blood, infection, instrumentation, pelvic surgery/radiation, neurologic symptoms, constipation and voided volumes. Review diabetes, sleep apnea, edema, heart failure, fluids, caffeine, alcohol and diuretics. Anticholinergics impair emptying; sympathomimetic cold remedies increase outlet resistance. A diary of voids, volumes, drinks, urgency and leakage distinguishes reduced capacity from excess production. Nocturia may also reflect interrupted sleep.

Use a validated score to quantify bother and response. IPSS has seven symptom questions plus quality-of-life assessment; it does not prove obstruction. Examine for bladder distension, genital/prostate abnormalities and indicated neurologic or pelvic findings. A smooth enlarged prostate neither excludes cancer nor establishes effective emptying.

### Choose tests that will change the decision

Urinalysis checks for blood, inflammatory findings, and glycosuria; culture is guided by the infection context. Measure **postvoid residual (PVR)** when evaluating male LUTS and when retention is plausible in a patient with leakage. Obtain it promptly after voiding. A high residual can reflect obstruction, impaired contraction, or both; neither a particular prostate size nor a single residual proves the mechanism. Repeat or confirm an unexpected value and assess its consequences rather than calling every result below an arbitrary cutoff safe. [EAU diagnostic evaluation](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/management-of-non-neurogenic-male-luts/chapter/diagnostic-evaluation)

Assess renal function when impairment, hydronephrosis, substantial retention or procedural planning warrants it. Ultrasound can assess upper tracts, bladder, residual and prostate. Uroflowmetry cannot alone separate obstruction from weak contraction; consider voided volume and repeatability. Reserve cystoscopy, stricture imaging and pressure-flow studies for consequential anatomic, diagnostic or procedural questions.

Discuss PSA when cancer risk or progression assessment will affect care. Infection, retention, instrumentation and prostate drugs alter interpretation. Suspicious examination or persistent concerning PSA warrants diagnostic evaluation even with mild symptoms; urgency alone does not diagnose cancer. Explain downstream consequences and distinguish screening from investigating a suspicious finding.

**Clinical Case: nocturnal urine production.** A 66-year-old requests stronger prostate treatment for four nightly voids. Stream is unchanged, residual small and nighttime volumes high; he has edema and untreated sleep apnea. Assess cardiopulmonary disease, fluid redistribution, medicines and sleep before increasing an outlet drug that may add dizziness without benefit.

### Acute retention requires relief and an explanation

An inability to void with suprapubic discomfort or distension suggests **acute urinary retention**, but neuropathy or chronic overdistension may blunt pain. Arrange prompt assessment and bladder drainage; do not wait for an elective imaging appointment in a patient unable to urinate. Evaluate renal function, infection, medicines, constipation, neurologic symptoms, and the precipitating event. Difficult catheterization or suspected urethral injury requires appropriate expertise rather than repeated forceful attempts. New retention with back pain, saddle sensory change, or leg weakness raises concern for a spinal emergency. [NIDDK retention diagnosis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/urinary-retention/diagnosis), [Immediate drainage and cause-directed care](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/urinary-retention/treatment)

After drainage, reassess circulation, urine, renal function and output. Substantial longstanding obstruction can cause postobstructive diuresis needing monitored fluid/electrolyte care. Systemic infection, AKI, bleeding/clots, persistent pain or unsafe drainage management changes disposition. Catheter relief does not establish BPH as the cause.

For likely prostate obstruction, an alpha-blocker may support a supervised trial without catheter. Arrange timing, failure contingencies and follow-up. Persistent retention may require intermittent or indwelling catheterization chosen with urology and the patient, accounting for infection, obstruction, skin, comfort and caregiver needs while evaluating reversible causes.

**Clinical Case: overflow after a sleep aid.** A 79-year-old with diabetes develops dribbling after an over-the-counter sedating sleep aid. Despite little pain, the bladder is palpable and residual large. Address retention, anticholinergic exposure, renal effects and possible diabetic bladder dysfunction. Adding oxybutynin for presumed urgency leakage could worsen retention.

### Match prostate treatment to bother, progression risk, and preferences

For mildly bothersome uncomplicated symptoms, offer education and planned reassessment. Adjust fluid, caffeine/alcohol and diuretic timing without dehydration or compromising the diuretic's indication; address constipation and consider double voiding. Retention, infection, hematuria or renal decline requires earlier assessment regardless of symptom score.

| Treatment approach | Main purpose | Important tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alpha-blocker | Relaxes outlet smooth muscle and can improve symptoms relatively quickly | Dizziness, orthostasis, falls, ejaculation effects; does not shrink the prostate or reliably prevent progression |
| 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor | Reduces androgen-driven prostate growth over months in appropriately enlarged glands | Slow benefit, sexual adverse effects, altered PSA interpretation |
| Alpha-blocker plus 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor | Combines early symptom relief with longer-term progression reduction in selected higher-risk patients | Greater treatment burden and combined adverse effects |
| Daily tadalafil | Can improve LUTS and coexisting erectile dysfunction in suitable patients | Nitrate/riociguat contraindications, hypotension and product-specific interaction limits |
| Storage-directed treatment | Targets persistent urgency/frequency rather than outlet resistance alone | First assess residual/obstruction and drug-specific retention, cognitive, or cardiovascular risks |

EAU supports alpha-blockers for bothersome moderate-to-severe LUTS and 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors for enlarged glands with progression risk, using **above 40 mL** as an example threshold. Combination therapy joins prompt symptom relief with longer-term progression reduction. Smaller glands change the rationale for shrinkage treatment, not eligibility for all symptom treatment. [EAU 2026 treatment selection](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/management-of-non-neurogenic-male-luts/chapter/disease-management)

For example, a usual labeled tamsulosin capsule starting regimen is **0.4 mg once daily, about 30 minutes after the same meal**. Check the actual preparation, interactions, blood pressure, and fall risk before prescribing. Tell the ophthalmologist about current or past use before cataract surgery because intraoperative floppy iris syndrome can occur even after discontinuation. More prostate-selective agents can still cause dizziness or ejaculation changes. Tamsulosin is not a hypertension treatment, and a patient's need for blood-pressure care should not obscure orthostatic risk. [Tamsulosin product precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e4202c52-be9d-41d9-b5ff-122740298544)

Do not crush, chew, or open the cited tamsulosin capsules. If benefit remains inadequate after two to four weeks and treatment is tolerated, the label permits **0.8 mg daily**; after interruption for several days, restart at **0.4 mg**, not the previous higher dose. Avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as ketoconazole. Persistent symptoms should still trigger diagnostic reassessment rather than repeated dose escalation. [Tamsulosin label, dosage and interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e4202c52-be9d-41d9-b5ff-122740298544)

Finasteride and dutasteride require months for meaningful gland-related benefit; they are not immediate treatment for a patient currently unable to void. Discuss sexual adverse effects and handling precautions for pregnancy exposure to the relevant products. Finasteride lowers PSA by about **50% within six months**. Establish a new baseline after treatment; an isolated PSA after at least six months is commonly doubled for comparison with untreated values, while any confirmed rise from the treatment nadir deserves evaluation. Do not conclude that a low number excludes cancer. [Finasteride labeling and PSA interpretation](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3fec2bcb-91b8-4041-9ee8-e5a4ebe5fd04)

The labeled BPH finasteride dose is **5 mg orally once daily**, not the 1-mg hair-loss regimen. Pregnant people or those who may become pregnant should not handle crushed or broken tablets because of potential fetal exposure; intact coated tablets prevent ordinary contact with the active ingredient. [Finasteride label, dose and handling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3fec2bcb-91b8-4041-9ee8-e5a4ebe5fd04)

**Example: PSA during finasteride treatment.** PSA 2.4 ng/mL after eight adherent months corresponds to approximately **4.8 ng/mL** for untreated comparison, not a biopsy threshold. Interpret prior values, nadir, adherence, examination, infection/retention, life expectancy and goals; a confirmed rise from nadir matters even within the laboratory interval.

Daily tadalafil can address both LUTS and erectile difficulty, but the U.S. label does **not recommend combining tadalafil with an alpha-blocker specifically for BPH** because of hypotension and labeling limitations. Do not turn guideline discussion of selected combinations into an assumption of unrestricted label approval. Any such specialist-selected use requires explicit indication, interaction, and blood-pressure assessment. Renal function and CYP3A4 inhibitors can alter permissible dosing, and nitrate or riociguat use is a contraindication. [Tadalafil indication-specific labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=dc14daa3-cae9-4751-9034-b0e9c18054b4)

For eligible adults, the labeled BPH dose is **tadalafil 5 mg orally once daily**. With creatinine clearance 30-50 mL/min, start at 2.5 mg daily and consider 5 mg according to response; daily use is not recommended below 30 mL/min or during hemodialysis. These are BPH/daily-use instructions, not the as-needed erectile-dysfunction schedule. [Tadalafil label, BPH and renal dosing](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=dc14daa3-cae9-4751-9034-b0e9c18054b4)

### Escalation is not reserved for failure of every medicine

Refractory/recurrent retention, obstruction-related upper-tract dilation or renal impairment, recurrent infection/stones and attributable persistent bleeding can justify procedures before every medicine is tried. Bother, adverse effects and preferences also matter. Establish the mechanism: prostate surgery will not correct unrelated nocturnal production or every detrusor disorder.

Urologic options include transurethral resection, laser enucleation or vaporization, selected minimally invasive procedures such as urethral lift or water-vapor treatment, and other techniques matched to anatomy and available expertise. Discuss bleeding/anesthesia risk, recovery, catheter needs, durability, retreatment, and effects on ejaculation, erections, and continence. A minimally invasive label does not guarantee equal durability or suitability for every median lobe and gland size. A simple prostate operation for benign obstruction is also not the same operation as radical prostatectomy for cancer. [AUA 2026 procedural guideline publication](https://www.auajournals.org/doi/10.1097/JU.0000000000005099)

Reassess symptoms, adverse effects, residual/flow when appropriate, and whether the original goal was met. The EAU follow-up framework reviews many symptom medicines at **four to six weeks**, while 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor response needs a longer horizon, including review at 12 weeks and six months. Earlier review is necessary for dizziness, retention, infection, or other deterioration. A prescription should never become indefinite renewal without confirming benefit. [EAU treatment-specific follow-up](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/management-of-non-neurogenic-male-luts/chapter/followup)

### Classify leakage before choosing a bladder medicine

Incontinence is involuntary urine loss. Ask when it occurs, whether urgency precedes it, whether the patient empties normally, and what prevents reaching the toilet. Multiple mechanisms may coexist. Review constipation, medicines, glycemic control, fluid pattern, infection symptoms, pelvic/prostate treatment, and neurologic disease. Persistent pain, hematuria, recurrent unexplained infections, fistula concern, or significant emptying difficulty needs a broader evaluation. [NIDDK causes of bladder-control problems](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/bladder-control-problems/symptoms-causes)

| Pattern | Typical description | Initial management direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stress | Leakage with cough, lifting, exercise, or increased abdominal pressure | Assess sphincter/pelvic support and offer appropriate pelvic-floor treatment |
| Urgency | A compelling urge followed by leakage, often with frequency | Exclude a clear secondary cause and assess emptying; use bladder-directed care |
| Overflow | Leakage from an inadequately emptied bladder | Identify retention and restore safe emptying rather than suppressing contraction |
| Functional | The person cannot reach or use the toilet because of mobility, cognition, clothing, or environment | Improve access, assistance, and timed/prompted toileting while checking coexisting urinary disease |
| Mixed | More than one pattern, commonly stress and urgency | Identify the most troublesome component and treat the relevant mechanisms |

Examine pelvic support/atrophy, cough stress leakage, prostate/rectum and neurologic function as indicated. Use a diary and urine testing; obtain PVR for possible voiding dysfunction. Uncomplicated urgency does not routinely require cystoscopy or urodynamics. Reserve specialized testing for consequential uncertainty, failure, prior surgery or invasive-treatment planning.

For women with stress or mixed incontinence, the NICE pathway recommends supervised pelvic-floor muscle training for **at least three months**, including confirmation of an effective contraction. For urgency or mixed symptoms, it recommends bladder training for **at least six weeks**. Training involves a coached program and follow-up, not a handout that assumes the patient knows which muscles to use. Address constipation, relevant weight goals, cough, and high or inadequate fluid intake. Selected patients may benefit from a continence pessary or specialist procedures. [NICE NG123 conservative continence pathway](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng123/chapter/recommendations)

### Overactive-bladder care should fit the person

Overactive bladder is a clinical urgency syndrome, often with frequency/nocturia and sometimes leakage, after excluding infection or another evident cause. It is not established simply by observing detrusor contractions on a test, and it can coexist with BPH. The AUA/SUFU approach offers behavioral treatment and shared choices among appropriate therapies rather than requiring every patient to fail an identical ladder. Discuss the patient's desired improvement and tolerance of medication or procedures. [AUA/SUFU 2024 overactive-bladder guideline](https://www.auanet.org/documents/Guidelines/PDF/2024%20Guidelines/OAB%202024%20Unabridgedv2.pdf)

Antimuscarinics may improve urgency but cause dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, retention and cognitive effects. Review narrow-angle glaucoma, gastric-emptying problems, retention, cumulative anticholinergic exposure and frailty. Discuss the observational association with cognitive impairment/dementia without presenting it as individual certainty. Address drug-induced symptoms before adding another anticholinergic.

Beta-3 agonists provide another medication option. **Mirabegron** can raise blood pressure, is not recommended in severe uncontrolled hypertension, inhibits CYP2D6, and requires attention to renal/hepatic dosing and retention risk. **Vibegron** has a different interaction profile, including digoxin monitoring, and also has a retention warning; its label includes adult men with OAB already receiving pharmacologic BPH treatment. Neither drug guarantees safe emptying in a patient with substantial obstruction. [Mirabegron precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e597023d-6dcc-4d9e-8ce0-2d28a5d862f3), [Vibegron labeling](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=25f21d25-14f8-4fda-91f6-7aa8b68aa1c8)

Persistent symptoms may warrant tibial nerve stimulation, sacral neuromodulation or intradetrusor botulinum toxin. Before botulinum treatment, assess PVR and discuss infection, retention and catheterization ability/support. Stress-incontinence surgery treats a different mechanism and requires procedural/material-specific counseling. Absorbent products and skin care support comfort while evaluation and treatment proceed.

**Clinical Case: mixed leakage.** A 58-year-old leaks with running/coughing and sometimes with urgency. Severe fluid restriction has worsened constipation. Assess pelvic-floor function and urine findings, explain the mixed pattern and begin coached treatment. Measure functional recovery and troublesome leakage rather than pad counts alone.

Use desmopressin only within a product-specific nocturnal-polyuria pathway with sodium, kidney, fluid and interaction safeguards. Hyponatremia can be life-threatening, especially with age, interacting drugs or fluid-balance disease. Check availability and formulation; childhood bedwetting regimens are not substitutes. Treat edema, sleep apnea, diabetes or medication timing when these drive nocturia.

### Erectile difficulty deserves a medical and sexual history

Distinguish erectile difficulty from reduced desire, ejaculation/orgasm problems, curvature and relationship distress. Ask about onset, consistency, morning/stimulated erections, pain, surgery, trauma, drugs, tobacco/alcohol, mood and preferred activities. Situational variability or preserved morning erections suggests a psychologic contribution but does not exclude mixed vascular, hormonal, neurologic or medication-related disease.

Assess blood pressure and cardiovascular risk, examine relevant vascular, genital, endocrine, and neurologic findings, and obtain glycemic/lipid testing when not current. The EAU framework includes an early-morning fasting testosterone measurement; an unexpectedly low result requires confirmation and appropriate endocrine assessment, not immediate testosterone prescribing. Specialist physiologic testing is selective rather than a routine nocturnal-erection study for every young patient. New erectile difficulty can be an opportunity to address vascular disease, not just provide a performance medicine. [EAU erectile-dysfunction evaluation](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/sexual-and-reproductive-health/chapter/management-of-erectile-dysfunction)

Ask whether sexual activity provokes chest pain or disproportionate breathlessness and whether cardiovascular disease is unstable. Stabilize high-risk symptomatic disease before recommending sexual activity or vasodilating treatment. Optimize contributing conditions and review medicines without abruptly stopping needed antidepressant or cardiovascular therapy. Counseling or psychosexual therapy can accompany medical treatment rather than being presented as a dismissive alternative. Obtain permission before involving a partner, and respect confidentiality.

PDE-5 inhibitors augment nitric-oxide/cGMP signaling; sexual stimulation is still needed. Explain timing, the influence of meals for the chosen drug, and the need for an adequate trial before declaring failure. They are **contraindicated with nitrates or guanylate-cyclase stimulators such as riociguat**. This includes an as-needed nitrate prescription and recreational nitrites, not only daily antianginal use. Alpha-blockers, other antihypertensives, potent metabolic inhibitors, age, and renal/hepatic function require a product-specific plan. [Sildenafil prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=15372a7c-2935-48dc-a55a-8966719b50be)

If chest pain occurs after a PDE-5 inhibitor, seek urgent care and disclose the agent and last dose; do not self-administer a nitrate. A single universal nitrate waiting interval is inappropriate because drugs and patient clearance differ. Warn about an erection lasting **more than four hours** and sudden visual or hearing symptoms, which need urgent assessment. A pill obtained outside regulated supply may contain undeclared ingredients or the wrong dose.

Alternatives include vacuum erection devices, intraurethral or intracavernosal therapy, and penile prostheses after individualized counseling. Injection therapy needs supervised teaching because of dosing, bleeding, fibrosis, and priapism risks. Testosterone is reserved for a properly established indication; it is not a general erection enhancer and can suppress sperm production. Address fertility goals before hormone treatment. [NIDDK treatment choices](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/erectile-dysfunction/treatment)

**Clinical Case: an as-needed nitrate.** A 61-year-old requesting sildenafil denies daily nitrate use but carries sublingual nitroglycerin for intermittent chest pressure. Review symptoms and cardiovascular care before sexual-function treatment. Any antianginal change needs coordination; intermittent nitrate use still makes an uncoordinated PDE-5 inhibitor plan unsafe.

### Sudden scrotal pain is a time-critical presentation

**Testicular torsion** twists the spermatic cord, threatening perfusion. Abrupt unilateral pain, nausea/vomiting, abnormal lie, or a high-riding testis raises concern, but presentations vary and can include abdominal pain. Examine the scrotum when appropriate in a child or adolescent with otherwise unexplained lower abdominal symptoms. A history of spontaneous resolution can represent intermittent torsion rather than reassurance. Age, urinary symptoms, a cremasteric response, or relief with elevation cannot independently exclude it.

High clinical suspicion requires immediate urologic/surgical assessment without waiting for routine imaging. Salvage declines with time; presentation after several hours is still an emergency, not a reason to abandon evaluation. Doppler ultrasound is helpful when it can resolve uncertainty without delaying definitive care, but **preserved arterial flow does not exclude early, partial, or intermittent torsion**. Manual detorsion, if performed by an experienced clinician while arranging surgery, does not replace exploration and fixation. [EAU acute-scrotum guidance](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/paediatric-urology/chapter/acute-scrotum)

For acute nontraumatic scrotal symptoms needing imaging, use Doppler ultrasound rather than routine CT/MRI. Interpret cord anatomy, comparative perfusion, timing and examination, not flow alone. Persistent concern after an equivocal scan warrants urgent assessment. Other emergencies include strangulated hernia, traumatic rupture, abscess and necrotizing infection. [ACR acute scrotal imaging](https://acsearch.acr.org/docs/69363/narrative/)

### Epididymitis treatment follows exposure and organism risk, not age alone

Epididymitis often produces unilateral epididymal tenderness and swelling, with possible urinary symptoms or urethritis. First assess torsion and systemic severity. Obtain urine gonorrhea/chlamydia NAAT and a bacterial urine culture in suspected adult acute cases. Sexual exposures, instrumentation, obstruction, cultures, and local resistance matter more than a rigid age split. Pain out of proportion, rapid skin progression, systemic toxicity, or diabetes with severe illness warrants urgent hospital assessment.

The CDC adult STI-associated pathway uses **ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once plus doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 10 days**; ceftriaxone is **1 g when weight is at least 150 kg**. For likely gonorrhea/chlamydia plus enteric infection with insertive anal exposure, use the same weight-specific ceftriaxone dose plus **levofloxacin 500 mg orally daily for 10 days**. For likely enteric-only infection, that levofloxacin course alone requires exclusion of gonorrhea by the CDC-specified urethral stain assessment and culture-guided selection, not merely older age. Adjust levofloxacin for renal impairment and review interactions and fluoroquinolone precautions. [CDC epididymitis regimens](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/epididymitis.htm)

Provide analgesia/support and test for **HIV and syphilis**. When gonorrhea/chlamydia is suspected, arrange evaluation and presumptive treatment of sex partners from the preceding **60 days**, or the most recent partner if the last contact was earlier. Abstain until the patient and partners are treated and symptoms resolve. Lack of improvement within **72 hours** needs reassessment; persistent swelling after treatment warrants investigation for tumor, abscess, infarction, or another cause rather than indefinite antibiotics. Acute prostatitis is covered in 06A. [CDC follow-up and partner management](https://www.cdc.gov/std/treatment-guidelines/epididymitis.htm)

**Clinical Case: pain improves before imaging.** A 17-year-old's abrupt severe unilateral pain improves en route. Ultrasound shows arterial flow, but examination remains abnormal. Possible intermittent or partial torsion still requires urgent urologic assessment; improvement and flow do not establish epididymitis.

### A testicular mass needs localization and timely referral

Localize a new lump within or outside the testis and assess pain and examination completeness. Ultrasound distinguishes intratesticular lesions from epididymal cysts, hydroceles and other extratesticular findings. A solid intratesticular mass remains suspicious despite small size, absent pain, hydrocele or normal tumor markers. Arrange imaging/referral rather than repeated empiric antibiotics.

In suspected testicular cancer, coordinate urgent urology, preoperative **AFP, beta-hCG, and LDH**, and subsequent staging. The usual cancer operation is through an inguinal approach; an office transscrotal biopsy is not the routine diagnostic step. Selected testis-sparing decisions belong with specialists. Discuss sperm preservation before treatment, endocrine effects, fertility, and the option of a prosthesis rather than assuming these concerns will be addressed later. [EAU testicular-cancer diagnostic pathway](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/testicular-cancer/chapter/diagnostic-evaluation)

| Presentation | Working distinction | Appropriate response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid intratesticular lump | Malignancy must be evaluated; pain or normal markers do not exclude it | Ultrasound and urgent urologic assessment |
| Fluid around the testis | Hydrocele may be benign but can obscure the testis or accompany another process | Confirm the underlying testis and cause rather than relying on transillumination alone |
| Soft venous swelling or separate epididymal cyst | Often an extratesticular process | Confirm localization; assess pain, fertility, atypical features, and referral needs |
| Sudden painful swelling | Torsion or another emergency remains possible | Use the urgent scrotal pathway rather than an elective lump pathway |

The USPSTF recommendation against routine testicular-cancer screening does **not** apply to a symptomatic lump, swelling, or pain. Likewise, declining PSA screening does not remove the need to investigate a concerning prostate examination or obstructive complication. See 01B for routine screening. [USPSTF testicular screening population](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/testicular-cancer-screening)

### Investigate prostate concerns without equating PSA with cancer

PSA reflects prostate tissue, not cancer alone. A new modest elevation in an otherwise stable patient may warrant repeat measurement after accounting for infection, retention, recent procedures, and medication effects. Use comparable testing conditions and a documented follow-up plan. A suspicious examination, marked concern, or persistent abnormality should accelerate urologic evaluation rather than be repeatedly deferred. A raised PSA alone does not establish bacterial prostatitis or justify antibiotics merely to lower the number.

Specialist assessment combines examination, family/genetic risk, PSA, prostate volume, and usually MRI to guide biopsy. MRI neither proves cancer nor excludes every significant lesion. Histology, grade, and staging guide treatment; symptom relief or a laboratory cutoff cannot replace them. Discuss both indolent-disease detection and missed-cancer risks in light of the patient's priorities. [EAU 2026 prostate diagnostic assessment](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/prostate-cancer/chapter/diagnostic-evaluation)

### Adapt the plan and make follow-up practical

In children, daytime wetting, holding behaviors, constipation, recurrent infection, and poor emptying require a bladder-bowel history and age-appropriate examination. Urotherapy includes regular relaxed voiding, appropriate drinks, bowel management, and supportive follow-up. Continuous leakage, an abnormal stream, neurologic findings, or recurrent significant infections can indicate a structural or neurologic problem. Do not apply an adult BPH or OAB prescription to a child's symptoms. [EAU childhood daytime urinary conditions](https://uroweb.org/guidelines/paediatric-urology/chapter/daytime-lower-urinary-tract-conditions)

Postpartum leakage still requires assessment for retention, fever or neurologic symptoms. In older adults, consider falls, cognition, vision, dexterity, toilet access and caregiver capacity before drugs or catheter care. Neurologic and post-prostate-treatment leakage needs specialist adaptation. Base care on anatomy, operations and hormones, not gender assumptions.

Agree on a functional goal and a review of response, adverse effects, and pending results. Give urgent return instructions for inability to urinate, fever with severe urinary/scrotal symptoms, hematuria with clots, sudden scrotal pain, or a prolonged erection.
