# Medivale Family Medicine

## 06C. Kidney Disease

Kidney disease may be silent. Beyond waste clearance, kidneys regulate fluid, electrolytes, acid excretion, blood pressure, erythropoiesis and mineral metabolism. Recognize dangerous acute changes, establish cause and chronicity, and coordinate kidney and cardiovascular protection.

KDIGO CKD, AKI and 2026 anemia guidance and indication-specific product labels guide care. See 06A-06B for specimens, protein measurement, glomerular warning findings and stones, and 06D for electrolyte emergencies. [KDIGO CKD guidance](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/), [KDIGO AKI guidance](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/acute-kidney-injury/)

### Recognize acute injury from a change, not just an abnormal number

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a rapid loss of kidney function. Under the active KDIGO definition, any of the following qualifies: creatinine rising by **at least 0.3 mg/dL within 48 hours**, rising to **at least 1.5 times baseline within seven days**, or urine output **below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for six hours**. AKI may be nonoliguric. Conversely, low urine output needs explanation even before creatinine has risen, because the blood marker lags behind a change in filtration. [KDIGO AKI definition and staging](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf)

Severity uses the worse qualifying creatinine or urine-output category. Stage 1 includes a 1.5-1.9-fold rise or the qualifying 0.3-mg/dL increase; stage 2 includes a 2.0-2.9-fold rise; stage 3 includes a threefold rise, an acute rise to at least 4.0 mg/dL, or kidney-replacement therapy. Urine-output staging uses below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6-12 hours in stage 1 and for at least 12 hours in stage 2. Stage 3 includes output below 0.3 mL/kg/hour for at least 24 hours or anuria for at least 12 hours. Use the complete age-specific criteria when staging children. Neither a stage label nor a reassuring absolute creatinine replaces clinical assessment.

**Clinical Case: a modest creatinine rise.** A 53-year-old with diarrhea has creatinine 1.4 mg/dL versus 1.0 mg/dL two days earlier. The 0.4-mg/dL rise meets AKI criteria. In a 70-kg patient, 140 mL urine over eight hours is **0.25 mL/kg/hour**. Assess perfusion, medicines, infection, drainage and complications promptly.

### Look for interacting causes

Prerenal, intrinsic and postrenal mechanisms often coexist. Prolonged hypoperfusion may progress to tubular injury; sepsis affects circulation and kidney tissue. Heart failure may impair filtration through congestion as well as low forward flow, so edema does not establish a need for IV fluid. [National Kidney Foundation AKI overview](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/acute-kidney-injury-aki)

| Working category | Examples and clues | Initial decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Perfusion-related | Gastrointestinal losses, hemorrhage, hypotension, sepsis, or cardiorenal congestion | Determine the actual hemodynamic problem; replace a deficit or treat congestion rather than reflexively giving fluid |
| Intrinsic kidney injury | Ischemic/toxic tubular injury, glomerulonephritis, interstitial nephritis, vascular injury, or pigment injury | Review exposures and urine findings; recognize inflammatory, systemic, or rapidly progressive disease |
| Impaired outflow | Retention, bilateral obstruction, or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney | Assess bladder/drainage promptly and arrange appropriate decompression |

NSAIDs reduce prostaglandin-supported afferent blood flow, which becomes particularly important during volume depletion. ACE inhibitors and ARBs lower intraglomerular pressure through a different mechanism and can aggravate filtration loss during certain acute hemodynamic stresses, despite being protective in appropriate stable disease. Diuretics can worsen true depletion but may be essential when congestion is the problem. The combination of dehydration, an NSAID, a diuretic, and renin-angiotensin blockade is a reason for careful reassessment, not a claim that each medicine is permanently forbidden. [NIDDK medicine safety](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/keeping-kidneys-safe)

Tubular injury can follow ischemia, toxins, or severe systemic illness; granular casts and tubular epithelial cells can support it. Blood/protein with red-cell casts raises concern for glomerular injury. Interstitial nephritis may follow a medication exposure, but fever, rash, and eosinophilia need not all be present. Urine eosinophils and fractional-excretion indices are not definitive answers in isolation; diuretics, CKD, sepsis, and mixed disease complicate interpretation. Review the full exposure timeline, including antibiotics, acid-suppressing medicines, supplements, and recent procedures. [NIDDK glomerular disease assessment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/glomerular-disease)

### Assess danger before deciding on outpatient follow-up

Compare prior results and assess intake/losses, urine volume, voiding, weight, dyspnea, fever, bleeding, rash and new medicines. Examine pressure, perfusion, volume, lungs, edema and bladder. Obtain creatinine, electrolytes, bicarbonate and urinalysis, adding CBC, ECG, CK, cultures or immune/hemolysis studies as indicated. Creatinine-derived eGFR is unreliable during rapid change and cannot establish a chronic stage.

Suspected obstruction or an unexplained cause can require urgent ultrasound. NICE recommends ultrasound within 24 hours for AKI with no identified cause or risk of obstruction, and more immediate imaging for suspected infected obstruction. A bladder scan can help identify retention but does not exclude upper-tract obstruction. An infected obstructed kidney requires urgent urologic drainage and antibiotics. Red-cell casts, significant protein with hematuria, or systemic inflammatory features should accelerate nephrology assessment. [NICE NG148 evaluation and obstruction](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng148/chapter/Recommendations)

**Hyperkalemia with dangerous features, pulmonary edema, severe acidosis, anuria, shock, or uremic complications requires urgent hospital-level care.** Persistent vomiting, rapidly rising creatinine, uncertain cause, a solitary kidney, or inability to obtain timely reassessment also makes a routine outpatient plan unsafe. Arrange emergency stabilization and specialist input rather than waiting for every diagnostic result. A normal ECG does not reliably exclude dangerous hyperkalemia. Immediate care is driven by physiology and trajectory, not a particular creatinine threshold.

Treatment corrects the cause while supporting circulation and preventing further harm. Give appropriate isotonic crystalloid for actual intravascular depletion, reassessing response and overload risk; shock or hemorrhage requires its own resuscitation pathway. Treat infection, stop or adjust contributing exposures, and relieve obstruction. Loop diuretics manage selected fluid overload but do not repair injured tubules, and low-dose dopamine should not be used as a kidney-restoring treatment. Refractory electrolyte, acid-base, volume, or uremic complications may require dialysis. [KDIGO AKI supportive management](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/KDIGO-2012-AKI-Guideline-English.pdf)

For selected mild outpatient AKI, document cause, medication changes, prompt repeat testing, result ownership and return precautions. After discharge, reconcile held medicines and restart plans. Evaluate recovery and CKD risk by three months after AKI, with earlier monitoring as needed. Return to baseline creatinine does not erase future risk. [NICE AKI follow-through](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng148/chapter/Recommendations)

### Establish chronicity, cause, and two dimensions of severity

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means abnormalities of kidney structure or function lasting **at least three months** with health implications. It can be established by persistent eGFR below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 or persistent evidence of kidney damage, including albuminuria, certain sediment or tubular abnormalities, structural disease, or histologic disease. CKD does not require a continuously falling eGFR. One abnormal result during an illness is insufficient to prove chronicity, while old results or imaging may establish that the problem predates today's visit. [KDIGO CKD definition and evaluation](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

Describe CKD by **cause, GFR category and albuminuria category**. G1/G2 requires another persistent damage marker. Use **A1 below 30 mg/g, A2 from 30 to below 300 mg/g, and A3 at least 300 mg/g**, matching the ADA/KDIGO monitoring grid. Confirm urine measurements as in 06B; filtration and albumin leak independently contribute to prognosis. [KDIGO 2024, Figure 13](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

| GFR category | eGFR in mL/min/1.73 m2 | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | 90 or above | Normal/high filtration; another damage marker is required for CKD |
| G2 | 60-89 | Mildly lower filtration; another damage marker is required for CKD |
| G3a | 45-59 | Mild-to-moderate reduction |
| G3b | 30-44 | Moderate-to-severe reduction |
| G4 | 15-29 | Severe reduction |
| G5 | Below 15 | Kidney failure; dialysis is not determined by this label alone |

**Example: CKD classification.** eGFR 42 and UACR 420 mg/g over five months indicate **G3b A3 CKD**, with cause still to be specified. eGFR 78 (G2) without persistent albuminuria or another damage marker does not establish CKD; substantial persistent albuminuria with eGFR 95 can. Explain clearance and leakage as separate measures, not eGFR as an exact percentage of normal function. [NIDDK kidney testing](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/tests-diagnosis)

### Interpret filtration estimates with their limitations

Use a validated race-free estimating equation, generally a current CKD-EPI equation for adults and an appropriate pediatric equation for children. Creatinine reflects muscle generation, diet, tubular secretion, and kidney clearance. A frail patient with low muscle mass can have deceptively reassuring creatinine, while a muscular patient may have a higher value without equivalent loss of filtration. Some medicines alter creatinine secretion without producing the same change in true GFR; interpretation still requires attention to the drug's other renal effects.

Combined creatinine-cystatin C estimation can help when accuracy will change care. Cystatin C also has nonfiltration influences, including thyroid disease and corticosteroids. Discordant estimates or high-stakes decisions may require measured GFR or specialist assessment. Confirm small isolated category-boundary changes before labeling progression. [National Kidney Foundation cystatin C](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/cystatin-c), [NIDDK race-free filtration assessment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/news/archive/2024/alternatives-race-based-kidney-function-calculations)

Establish cause rather than automatically attributing CKD to diabetes. Review pressure/glycemic history, prior AKI, obstruction, family history, infections, systemic symptoms, medicines and toxic exposures. Sediment and albumin/total-protein patterns may challenge the presumed cause. Ultrasound assesses selected structural, obstructive, cystic and chronicity questions; small kidneys suggest scarring but do not prove every acute change irreversible. [NIDDK causes of CKD](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/causes)

Abrupt decline, active sediment, unexpected heavy proteinuria, systemic findings or inherited-disease clues warrants targeted investigation and often nephrology. Select serology, monoclonal-protein studies, genetic testing or biopsy when they can change care. Biopsy decisions balance diagnostic value and procedural risk rather than follow an isolated imaging description.

### Build kidney protection around the patient's indication and tolerance

Combine cause-directed treatment, pressure control, cardiorenal medicines, smoking cessation, activity, nutrition and injury prevention. Address affordability and monitoring. A modest early hemodynamic eGFR dip can accompany long-term protection; progressive decline, symptomatic hypotension or dangerous hyperkalemia requires reassessment. [NIDDK longitudinal CKD care](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/chronic-kidney-disease-ckd/managing)

For non-dialysis adults with high blood pressure and CKD, the named KDIGO approach targets systolic pressure **below 120 mm Hg when tolerated, measured with a standardized office protocol**. Do not apply this threshold to a hurried, nonstandard measurement. Frailty, postural symptoms, falls, limited life expectancy, pregnancy, and pediatric care require adaptation. Home readings and correct cuff/measurement technique help identify overtreatment or masked hypertension; the cardiovascular chapter develops practical assessment. [KDIGO blood-pressure guidance](https://kdigo.org/guidelines/blood-pressure-in-ckd/)

ACE inhibitors or ARBs reduce intraglomerular pressure and protein leakage. KDIGO recommends one for **G1-G4 CKD with A3 albuminuria without diabetes, or A2/A3 with diabetes**, and suggests one for A2 without diabetes. A1 alone is not a kidney-specific indication, although hypertension or heart failure may independently justify treatment. Use one class, not combined ACE inhibitor/ARB/direct-renin blockade, and titrate toward the highest tolerated approved dose. Check blood pressure, creatinine, and potassium within two to four weeks after starting or increasing treatment, sooner if high risk. A creatinine rise above 30% within four weeks calls for reassessment of volume, interacting drugs, renovascular disease, and other causes. Stable eGFR below 30 alone does not mandate stopping an otherwise tolerated agent. [KDIGO RAS recommendations 3.6.1-3.6.4 and practice points](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

**Example: creatinine after an ARB.** A rise from 1.2 to 1.44 mg/dL is **20%** and may be acceptable with stable potassium, pressure, intake and symptoms. A later 1.8 mg/dL is **50% above baseline** and warrants prompt reassessment, especially after diarrhea or NSAID use. [National Kidney Foundation ACE inhibitor/ARB counseling](https://www.kidney.org/kidney-topics/ace-inhibitors-and-arbs)

SGLT2 inhibitors offer kidney and heart benefits beyond glucose lowering. KDIGO recommends them for T2D with CKD and eGFR at least 20, and for adults with CKD who have eGFR at least 20 with UACR at least 200 mg/g, or heart failure irrespective of albuminuria. It also suggests treatment for eGFR 20-45 with lower albuminuria. These are guideline populations, not interchangeable product labels or routine type 1 diabetes treatment. Once initiated, continuation below eGFR 20 can be reasonable unless treatment is not tolerated or kidney-replacement therapy starts. An expected reversible early dip alone is not ordinarily a reason to stop. [KDIGO SGLT2 recommendations and continuation practice point](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

Check the actual agent's indication, renal limits, and safety instructions. Empagliflozin labeling distinguishes CKD protection from its glucose-lowering indication, cautions about volume depletion and genital/urinary infection, and warns that ketoacidosis can occur without marked hyperglycemia. Hold it for at least three days before surgery or procedures involving prolonged fasting when possible, and restart only when clinically stable with oral intake restored. Acute illness and ketosis symptoms need a specific plan; do not compensate by independently stopping essential basal insulin. [Empagliflozin prescribing information](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5777b8a8-ada6-4950-8548-43a1de11f075)

Finerenone requires indication-specific selection. Its current U.S. label distinguishes established outcome benefits in T2D-associated CKD from a T1D-associated CKD indication for reducing UACR, with expected renal benefit; the latter is not identical evidence for hard kidney outcomes. Do not extend either indication to all nondiabetic CKD. Check potassium/eGFR before treatment; the label does not recommend initiation below eGFR 25 and prohibits initiation with potassium above 5.0 mEq/L. Recheck potassium after four weeks and follow the dose/hold algorithm. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors and adrenal insufficiency are contraindications. The separate heart-failure indication has its own dosing, so its regimen should not be copied into CKD care. [Finerenone prescribing information, September 2026](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=fc726765-5d5a-4d6e-b037-b847bda9fb7c)

For the labeled diabetes-associated CKD indications, finerenone starts at **10 mg orally daily with eGFR 25 to below 60**, or **20 mg daily with eGFR at least 60** (eGFR units: mL/min/1.73 m2). The CKD target is 20 mg daily, not the HF regimen's possible 40 mg. At four-week review, potassium at or below 4.8 permits increasing 10 to 20 mg unless eGFR has fallen more than 30%; above 4.8 through 5.5, maintain the dose. **Above 5.5, withhold; restart at 10 mg only after potassium is at or below 5.0**, with monitoring. [Finerenone label, CKD Tables 1-2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=fc726765-5d5a-4d6e-b037-b847bda9fb7c)

Choose GLP-1-based treatment, glycemic targets, lipid therapy and cardiovascular prevention by diabetes, weight, vascular disease, renal function and product indication. Reduced insulin/drug clearance increases hypoglycemia risk; anemia or altered RBC turnover may distort A1C. Coordinate with diabetes/cardiovascular care rather than universally intensifying glucose treatment or prescribing aspirin. [ADA 2026 kidney-risk management](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690176/)

### Make nutrition and volume advice practical

An individualized kidney diet should preserve nutritional adequacy. KDIGO suggests about **0.8 g protein/kg/day** for adults with G3-G5 CKD and sodium below **2 g/day**, with exceptions and dietitian support. Sodium is not the same mass as salt. Frailty, poor intake, active illness, childhood growth, pregnancy, and dialysis alter nutritional needs; do not turn a non-dialysis target into a universal restrictive diet. Avoid unsupervised high-protein or extreme low-protein plans. Potassium and phosphorus advice should reflect actual abnormalities and food sources rather than banning all fruits, vegetables, or protein foods. [KDIGO management takeaways](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline-Top-10-Takeaways-for-Nephrologists-Management.pdf)

For congestion, review weight trends, sodium intake, diuretic access/adherence, pressure and symptoms. Loop diuretics can relieve advanced-disease edema; combinations need close electrolyte monitoring. Tailor fluids and monitoring to the patient's circumstances. Rest dyspnea, hypoxia or rapidly worsening edema requires urgent assessment.

### Evaluate anemia rather than assuming erythropoietin deficiency

CKD anemia involves reduced erythropoietin, altered iron availability and inflammation. Blood loss, nutritional deficiency, hemolysis, marrow disease and medicines may coexist. Begin with CBC, reticulocytes, ferritin and TSAT. Normocytosis does not exclude iron deficiency; inflammation can elevate ferritin despite restricted iron availability. [NIDDK CKD anemia mechanisms](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/anemia)

KDIGO 2026 separates populations when considering iron treatment. These thresholds concern **people with anemia**, and are treatment considerations rather than a universal definition of iron deficiency or permission to skip a bleeding evaluation. Oral versus intravenous iron depends on severity, response, tolerability, access, and dialysis setting. [KDIGO 2026 anemia guideline](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/KDIGO-2026-Anemia-in-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

| Setting | KDIGO 2026 iron-initiation consideration | Follow-through |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Not on dialysis or receiving peritoneal dialysis | Ferritin below 100 ng/mL with TSAT below 40%, or ferritin 100 to below 300 ng/mL with TSAT below 25% | Choose route with the patient; assess response and underlying cause |
| Receiving hemodialysis | Ferritin at or below 500 ng/mL and TSAT at or below 30% | Intravenous iron is generally favored in the dialysis pathway |
| During iron treatment | Consider withholding routine iron if ferritin exceeds 700 ng/mL or TSAT is at least 40% | Reassess the whole clinical picture; these are not target values to reach |

**Example: iron availability.** A stable nondialysis patient with hemoglobin 9.8 g/dL, ferritin 180 ng/mL and TSAT 18% meets the iron-treatment consideration despite preserved ferritin. Investigate causes, replace appropriately and reassess before assuming an ESA is needed. Systemic infection may require temporary iron suspension; infusion risks and oral adverse effects remain relevant. [KDIGO anemia implementation summary](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/KDIGO-2026-Anemia-in-CKD-Guideline-Executive-Summary.pdf)

ESA initiation is individualized after correctable causes are addressed; symptoms, transfusion avoidance, and treatment risks matter. KDIGO's adult ESA target is **below 11.5 g/dL**, not normalization. The U.S. epoetin alfa label is stricter: in nondialysis adults, consider initiation below 10 g/dL only when the decline suggests likely transfusion and reducing transfusion risks is a goal; **reduce or interrupt if hemoglobin exceeds 10 g/dL**. In dialysis adults, reduce or interrupt as hemoglobin approaches or exceeds 11 g/dL. Use the lowest dose sufficient to reduce transfusions. Higher targets increase cardiovascular/stroke risk, and uncontrolled hypertension is a contraindication. The KDIGO ceiling is not a mandate to maintain 11.4 in every U.S. patient. Transfusion is a separate decision, especially with acute bleeding or instability; sensitization matters for transplant candidates. [Epoetin alfa label, adult CKD instructions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f2d0b28-9cc5-4523-80b8-637fdaf3f7a5)

### Treat mineral, acid-base, and potassium problems in context

As kidney function declines, phosphate handling and vitamin D activation change. Compensatory PTH and FGF23 responses contribute to CKD mineral and bone disorder; a normal calcium level does not exclude it. Evaluate calcium, phosphate, PTH, alkaline phosphatase, and vitamin D when appropriate to stage and clinical findings. Interpret trends, bone symptoms, fractures, and vascular risks together rather than labeling any single PTH elevation an indication for treatment. [NIDDK mineral and bone disorder](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/mineral-bone-disorder)

The active KDIGO mineral-bone pathway bases phosphate-lowering decisions on progressively or persistently elevated phosphate, not an eGFR cutoff alone. Review intake, especially highly absorbable additives, and avoid hypercalcemia or indiscriminate calcium loading. In adults with nondialysis G3a-G5 disease, calcitriol and active vitamin D analogues are not routine; selected severe progressive hyperparathyroidism needs specialist management after modifiable factors are addressed. Bone-density testing can help when it will change treatment, but CKD-related bone turnover and medicine-specific renal/calcium risks complicate osteoporosis decisions. [KDIGO mineral-bone guidance](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/02/KDIGO-2017-CKD-MBD-GL-Update-Synopsis.pdf)

Reduced acid excretion can produce chronic metabolic acidosis, but confirm the abnormality and consider gastrointestinal losses or other causes. KDIGO suggests considering pharmacologic treatment, with or without dietary intervention, for acidosis with potential clinical consequences, giving adult bicarbonate below 18 mmol/L as an example. This is not a universal automatic bicarbonate prescription at every slightly low result. Monitor response, blood pressure, potassium, and fluid status; sodium-containing alkali can worsen volume problems. Severe acute acidemia belongs in urgent care, not a gradual outpatient titration plan.

Hyperkalemia management begins with severity, ECG/clinical assessment, specimen validity, kidney trajectory, and causes. Review potassium supplements, salt substitutes, constipation, acidosis, and interacting drugs. Correcting contributors, selected diuretics, or binders may allow continued beneficial RAS therapy, but uncontrolled or dangerous hyperkalemia requires escalation and sometimes treatment interruption. Potassium binders do not replace immediate stabilization when indicated. Never wait for dietary change to treat a life-threatening potassium disturbance. [KDIGO complications and medication management](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

### Adjust medicines without confusing kidney estimates

Review prescriptions, nonprescription products, supplements and recent changes at transitions. Antimicrobials, anticoagulants, gabapentinoids, some opioids and diabetes drugs may accumulate. Check magnesium/aluminum products, bowel preparations and potassium remedies. Renal dosing depends on indication, loading/maintenance roles, drug levels, dialysis and trajectory, not simply halving doses. AKI can make a stable dose unsafe.

Check whether the specific dosing instruction uses estimated creatinine clearance, indexed eGFR, or absolute GFR. These are related but not identical quantities. Removing body-surface-area indexing when appropriate uses **absolute eGFR = reported eGFR x BSA/1.73**. For example, 40 mL/min/1.73 m2 with BSA 2.0 m2 gives about **46.2 mL/min**. This arithmetic does not authorize switching a label's required method or independently changing a narrow-therapeutic-index drug. Near a dosing boundary, a combined creatinine-cystatin C estimate or measured assessment may improve confidence. [NIDDK renal drug dosing](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/research-funding/research-programs/kidney-clinical-research-epidemiology/laboratory/ckd-drug-dosing-providers)

Select imaging and contrast by indication, current function, AKI, prior reactions and alternatives. Hydration must account for congestion. Contrast-associated and proven contrast-caused injury are distinct. Follow radiology protocols in higher-risk patients; CKD alone does not justify withholding necessary imaging or ordering dialysis solely to clear contrast. [ACR contrast guidance](https://www.acr.org/Clinical-Resources/Clinical-Tools-and-Reference/Contrast-Manual)

For temporary medication holds, specify drug, reason, contact and restart criteria. Blanket stopping or unmonitored restarting can harm. ACE inhibitors/ARBs require pregnancy planning; SGLT2 and other cardiorenal drugs need pregnancy/lactation review. Adult creatinine-based dosing tables do not establish pediatric or pregnancy prescriptions.

### Refer using cause, complications, trajectory, and predicted risk

Nephrology referral is appropriate for uncertain or inherited disease, rapid or unexplained decline, advanced CKD, important albuminuria/active sediment, resistant hypertension, persistent electrolyte or acid-base abnormalities, and difficult anemia, bone disease, or nutritional complications. In KDIGO's adult pathway, eGFR below 30 is an important criterion. Other examples include sustained unexplained red-cell casts, substantial albuminuria with hematuria, or persistent UACR above 700 mg/g. A patient can need referral before reaching G4; a referral never replaces emergency stabilization. [KDIGO referral framework](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

For appropriate G3-G5 adults, use an externally validated kidney-failure prediction equation, such as the four-variable KFRE using age, sex, eGFR, and UACR, to supplement clinical judgment. KDIGO allows a five-year kidney-failure risk of about 3%-5% to help guide referral, and a two-year risk above 10% to help time multidisciplinary care. Models must fit the population and use correct units; do not apply a G3-G5 tool indiscriminately to AKI, children, or G1-G2 disease. Prediction of kidney failure is not prediction of every important cardiovascular outcome. [KDIGO risk-based care takeaways](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline-Top-10-Takeaways-for-Nephrologists-Management.pdf)

### Plan kidney-failure care before a crisis

Begin kidney-replacement education early enough for shared decisions, transplant evaluation and access planning. KDIGO suggests considering preemptive transplantation/dialysis-access planning below eGFR 15-20 or above 40% two-year kidney-replacement risk, alongside trajectory. Protect future vascular access when appropriate; preparation does not mean dialysis must start immediately. [KDIGO kidney-replacement planning](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/KDIGO-2024-CKD-Guideline.pdf)

Starting dialysis depends on symptoms, signs, laboratory problems, quality of life, and patient preferences, not a G5 label alone. Refractory fluid overload, potassium or acid-base abnormalities, pericarditis, encephalopathy, or progressive uremic nutritional/symptom burden can require treatment. A person may still make urine despite inadequate clearance. Discuss in-center or home hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, transplantation when eligible, and comprehensive conservative care. Each has practical requirements involving access, home circumstances, training, support, and ongoing care. [NIDDK choosing kidney-failure treatment](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/choosing-treatment)

Hemodialysis uses an external dialyzer and vascular access. Choose fistula, graft or catheter according to preparation needs and patient-specific complications. In-center treatment offers staff support with travel/schedule burdens; home treatment requires training, a safe setting and support. Hypotension, cramps, fatigue and access problems affect daily life. Anemia, bone-mineral, cardiovascular and medication care continues. [NIDDK hemodialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/hemodialysis)

Peritoneal dialysis uses the peritoneum as an exchange membrane, with solution instilled and drained through a catheter by manual exchanges or a cycler. Assess abdominal suitability, clean-connection skills, storage and assistance. Monitor glucose exposure, fluid balance, catheter problems and infection. **Cloudy effluent or abdominal pain can signal peritonitis**: contact the dialysis team promptly even without fever. [NIDDK peritoneal dialysis](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/peritoneal-dialysis)

Transplant evaluation includes medical suitability, donor options, operative risk, and the ability to sustain follow-up and immunosuppression. A living-donor transplant can sometimes be arranged before dialysis; prior dialysis is not universally required. Transplantation does not end chronic care: rejection, infection, cardiovascular risk, drug toxicity, and interactions remain important. Coordinate preventive care and vaccination with the transplant team, and seek prompt advice for fever, declining graft function, or inability to take antirejection medicines. Do not independently stop immunosuppression because a patient has a routine infection or a newly prescribed interacting drug. [NIDDK kidney transplantation](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/kidney-transplant)

Conservative kidney management is active care: symptom relief, medication adjustment, treatment of complications, psychosocial support, and advance planning without dialysis. It is not abandonment. Explain likely benefits and burdens in the person's situation rather than promising that one approach always prolongs or improves life. Palliative care can accompany dialysis or other treatment as well as conservative care. Revisit goals when function, cognition, or support changes, and involve chosen caregivers with permission. [NIDDK conservative management](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/kidney-disease/kidney-failure/conservative-management)

**Clinical Case: planning before dialysis.** A patient with progressive G4 disease fears that discussing dialysis means immediately losing his role caring for a dependent partner. Distinguish education, access preparation and treatment initiation. Explore home treatment, transport, respite, transplantation and conservative care in light of his priorities and predicted course.

Children need growth, development, congenital/inherited disease, and pediatric blood-pressure/nutrition assessment, with coordinated transition to adult care. Pregnancy warrants preconception and obstetric-nephrology coordination rather than reliance on ordinary adult eGFR estimates. Older adults need attention to falls, sarcopenia, competing illness, and goals, without dismissing actionable disease as aging. Transplant recipients have additional infection, rejection, and interaction risks; unexplained graft dysfunction needs their transplant team.

At follow-up, review symptoms, volume/blood pressure, eGFR and albumin trends, complications, and medication safety. Check eGFR/UACR at least annually in established CKD and more often with higher risk or treatment changes. An **eGFR change above 20% or a doubling of UACR** exceeds expected variability and warrants evaluation, not automatic attribution to progression. Ask about itching, sleep, appetite, mood, fatigue, treatment burden, and access to the agreed plan; assign responsibility for pending tests and referrals.
